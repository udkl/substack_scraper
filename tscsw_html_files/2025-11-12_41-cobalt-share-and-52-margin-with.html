In the early 1840s, a big shift occurred in the British psyche. A new technology, the steam locomotive, promised to annihilate distance and reshape commerce.

At the center of this euphoria stood George Hudson, the “Railway King.” The market was not pricing his profits (many of his lines had yet to lay a single mile of track) but the promise of future dominance. It was a frenzied realignment of capital based on narrative, and it sowed the seeds of its own destruction.

Today, this historical echo reverberates, but in reverse.

The market, now populated by fragile, narrative-following analysts, looks at CMOC Group Limited (CMOC) and sees only the story of “risk.” They see “Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC)” and “Management Turnover,” run their models, and panic.

They are correct about the existence of risk but profoundly wrong about its implication.

This report will argue a high-conviction long thesis based on provable facts. CMOC is an asymmetric opportunity because the market is pricing it for a fragile exposure to the DRC, while the evidence shows a company that is structurally anti-fragile. Its operational monopoly in a critical metal, combined with a new, purpose-built management team, is actively converting chaos into cash flow.

Attempts by the DRC to “control” CMOC (e.g., export quotas) serve only to (a) skyrocket the price of its main commodity and (b) force CMOC to diversify that cash flow into non-DRC assets. We are not buying a “risky miner.” We are buying a world-class monopoly, run by a new team of specialist operators, at the “failed state” discount.

The 2025F Price/Earnings to Growth (PEG) ratio of 0.69 is a confession of the market’s complete lack of understanding.

In this post, we will unpack the company’s fundamentals, the pricing structure, and, most importantly, explore the promising (and often overlooked) future outlook.

Investment Case: The Anti-Fragile Arbitrage

Strengths (The Anti-Fragile Core)

A-Class, Low-Cost Assets: CMOC does not own “mines”; it owns geologic abnormalities. The TFM and KFM operations in the DRC are two of the world’s largest, lowest-cost copper-cobalt deposits. These are Tier-1 assets. KFM, for example, commenced production and immediately reached full capacity, a rare feat in this industry. This is not “digging for metal”; it is harvesting.

The Cobalt Monopoly (Provable Fact): This is not an exaggeration. CMOC’s global market share in mined cobalt exploded from 24% in 2023 to 41% in 2024. As the Chairman stated in the H1 2025 earnings call, they are “far ahead of other manufacturers”. In 2024, the company produced 114,165 tonnes of cobalt, a staggering 31% above its stated capacity. CMOC is the cobalt market.

Vertically Integrated Moat (IXM): The company owns its own world-class trading house, IXM. This is not the “synergy” that consultants sell; it is a real-time, global intelligence-gathering and risk-management machine. It allows CMOC to capture margin across the value chain and manage the volatility inherent in its operations. While a DRC export ban forced IXM to declare force majeure in June, the company’s response was a masterstroke: on October 24, it appointed Branko Buhavac, the CEO of IXM, as the new Chief Commercial Officer of the entire CMOC Group. The integration is now complete. The mining company and the trading company are one entity.

Non-DRC Cash-Flow Engines: The market fixates on TFM/KFM and ignores the ballast. CMOC is a top-two niobium producer in Brazil (operating in a stable duopoly) and a world-leading molybdenum and tungsten producer in China. These assets are highly margin-accretive and, per the H1 2025 earnings call, provide a stable cash flow backstop completely uncorrelated to Congolese politics.

Weaknesses (The Internal Fragilities)

Execution Friction (The “New Team” Tax): A “dream team” of operators has been assembled (see Management section). But this much turnover in a single year (Chairman, CEO, CFO, COO, and CCO) is not frictionless. The company’s “2025 Year of Change” brings very real “integration risk.” Any thesis must underwrite the potential for 1-2 quarters of coordination costs or operational fumbles as this new team finds its rhythm.

Working Capital Volatility: The combination of the IXM trading arm and the DRC’s logistical nightmare consumes cash. The cobalt export ban (now a quota) means inventory gets stranded on-site, as seen in H1 2025 cobalt sales figures. This creates air pockets in cash conversion. This is a structural feature of the business.

Capital Allocation Appetite: The new, M&A-focused Chairman, Liu Jianfeng, just completed the acquisition of a giant gold project (Lumina Gold) in a new jurisdiction (Ecuador). Simultaneously, the board has authorized USD 1.084 billion for the KFM Phase II expansion. This company has a ferocious appetite for capex. The risk is that they over-extend the balance sheet by fighting wars on two new fronts.

Opportunities (The Asymmetric Upside)

The “Accidental Cartel”: The DRC’s new quota system is the single most bullish catalyst for CMOC’s margins. The new annual quota (96,600 tonnes) is roughly 50% of 2024 export levels. This massive, state-mandated supply shock is the reason cobalt prices are “heading higher”. CMOC publicly “opposes” the quota system, which is savvy public theater. Privately, their (now outgoing) CCO, Kenny Ives, admitted in October that cobalt demand was “underwhelming”. The DRC quota solves the demand problem by forcing a price solution. CMOC, as the world’s largest and lowest-cost producer, benefits exponentially. They sell less volume for dramatically more margin.

KFM Phase II & Operating Leverage: The $1.084 billion authorization for KFM Phase II, targeting an additional ~100 ktpa of copper by 2027, is a sign of absolute confidence. The financial forecasts are this operating leverage story: Net Income is projected to grow 62% from 2024 to 2027, while Revenue grows only 8%. This margin expansion is the entire thesis. The appointment of Peng Xuhui, a manufacturing specialist from microelectronics, as the new CEO is the final piece. His job is not to find the metal; his job is to turn TFM and KFM into high-efficiency factories, institutionalizing the cost controls mentioned in the H1 2025 call and delivering the margins in the forecast.

The “Two-Pole” Hedge: The acquisition of Lumina Gold in Ecuador is the second pole of the new strategy, as articulated by Chairman Liu. This demonstrates a sophisticated, third-order strategic pivot. Liu is taking the politically toxic cash flow from Pole 1 (DRC Copper/Cobalt) and reinvesting it into Pole 2 (Ecuador/Gold), a completely uncorrelated asset class in an uncorrelated (though still risky) jurisdiction. This is a geopolitical arbitrage that de-risks the entire enterprise long-term.

Threats (The Black Swan Farm)

  1. The DRC (The Obvious Body Risk): This is not a “tail risk.” It is the entire risk.

    • Sovereign Risk: President Tshisekedi has threatened permanent export bans on violators. The 2022 TFM dispute, which halted exports, cost CMOC USD 800 million to resolve. Full expropriation is a non-zero, though unlikely, probability.

Regulatory Risk: The new quota system is already dysfunctional. As of October 24, 2025, producers were still awaiting approvals to ship under the new regime. This is a direct, ongoing attack on cash flow and working capital.

Logistical Risk: The DRC lacks power and transport. The situation is so dire that CMOC is building its own hydropower (the Heshima project) just to operate. They are literally building the state around their asset.

  1. Ecuador (The New Black Swan Farm): The Lumina/Cangrejos gold project is not a “win.” It is the purchase of a new, unknown set of problems: permitting, community engagement, and a complex new stakeholder map in a historically difficult mining jurisdiction.

  2. Cobalt Substitution (The Kenny Ives Worry): The (outgoing) CCO, Kenny Ives, explicitly warned in October 2025 that the “biggest challenge” for cobalt is battery chemistry pivoting away (i.e., LFP batteries, which use no cobalt). This is the true long-term bear case. The new quota system, while excellent for 2026-2027 prices, accelerates the economic incentive for OEMs to engineer cobalt out of the battery.

Porter, Competition, and Competitive Advantage

This framework is a favorite of MBA programs that teach people how to sound smart without saying anything provable. We will indulge it, but with facts instead of theories. The “industry” is not “global mining”; it is the brutal, high-stakes business of digging critical metal out of a failed state.

Porter and Competitive Industry Position

  • Suppliers (Power: HIGH): The primary supplier is the DRC government, which supplies the “license to operate.” Its power is near-total, as demonstrated by export bans and quota implementation. Secondary suppliers (power, logistics) are also powerful bottlenecks. CMOC’s counter-strategy is to make itself an indispensable “supplier” of cash to the DRC state, paying USD 599.42 million in taxes and royalties from KFM alone in 2024.

  • Customers (Power: MEDIUM): Customer power is concentrated in a few large battery manufacturers, including CATL (which is also a major CMOC shareholder). This power is mitigated by a simple fact: cobalt is a critical, non-substitutable (in the near term) input for high-performance batteries. They cannot make their product without CMOC’s 41% market share.

  • Barriers to Entry and Exit (Power: ASTRONOMICAL): You cannot “enter” this market. You cannot find another KFM or TFM. You cannot get the permits. You cannot raise the billions in capital. And you almost certainly cannot survive the political risk that hounds operators. The “barrier” is the very chaos that makes the stock cheap. CMOC, backed by Chinese state-associated capital, is one of the only entities on earth with the risk tolerance and balance sheet to climb that barrier.

  • Substitutes (Power: HIGH - Long Term): As noted, LFP batteries (no cobalt) are the key substitute. This is a technological threat, not a competitive one. In niobium, by contrast, there are effectively no substitutes.

  • Competition (Power: LOW): Rivalry is muted. Competitors are not competing with CMOC; they are surviving it. CMOC’s 2024 volume ramp-up broke the cobalt price and flooded the market. Glencore, its key “competitor,” publicly supports the DRC’s new quota system because it cannot compete with CMOC’s volume and cost structure. This is not competition; it is a cry for regulatory protection.

  • Overall Comment (Score: 40/50): The framework is useless here. The reality is this: CMOC is a monopolist (Cobalt) and oligopolist (Niobium, Copper) whose primary competitor is the sovereign state it operates in. Its moat is not built on brand or IP; it is built on geology, scale, and geopolitical backing.

Key Competitors

Glencore (LON: GLEN): The old king of cobalt. Operates the KCC and Mutanda mines in the DRC. Glencore is now fragile to CMOC’s strength. It was CMOC’s massive 2024 production surge that weighed on prices and forced Glencore to declare its own force majeure.

Zijin Mining Group (HK: 2899): A fellow Chinese giant and a formidable peer in copper through its Kamoa-Kakula JV and COMMUS mine. However, it is not a cobalt competitor; its DRC cobalt production is trivial (830 tonnes in 2024). Zijin is more of a potential partner in solving DRC logistics, such as activating the Tanzanian Railway.

CBMM (Private): The only real competitor in Niobium. This private Brazilian company controls a dominant ~77% to 85% of the global market. CMOC, with its Brazilian assets, is the only other major producer. This is a true duopoly.

Market Shares

  • Cobalt: 41% of global mined supply in 2024. This is a monopoly.

  • Niobium: #2 Producer globally, in a duopoly with CBMM.

  • Copper: Top 10 Global Producer.

  • Molybdenum/Tungsten: “World’s leading producer”.

Competitive Analysis of the Company

  • Sustainable Competitive Advantage:

    1. Geological Supremacy: Their assets (TFM, KFM, Brazil) are Tier-1. The cost of extraction is low, and the reserve life is long (nearly 50 years at current capacity).

    2. Scale & Integration: They are the only producer with 41% cobalt market share and a world-class, in-house trading arm (IXM). This allows them to manage volatility and capture margin across the entire value chain.

  • Evaluation of Economic Moat: The moat is deep, wide, and filled with crocodiles. It is not a “Western” moat built on brand or patents. It is a barbarian’s moat built on three pillars:

    1. Hard Assets: World-class, low-cost mines.

    2. Scale: 41% market share.

    3. Jurisdictional Tolerance: The ability, backed by Chinese capital, to operate in a jurisdiction (DRC) that Western firms have fled. Tellingly, Freeport-McMoRan sold both TFM and KFM to CMOC. That chaos is the moat.

What it is: Exploration, extraction, and processing of metals.

The “Two Poles” Strategy: This is the new framework articulated by Chairman Liu on the H1 2025 earnings call.

Molybdenum & Tungsten:

Segment 2: Metal Trading (The Moat)

  • What it is: Global procurement, logistics, risk management, and trading, operated via IXM.

  • This is the nervous system. It gives CMOC global intelligence. The H1 2025 earnings call noted IXM “restructured product lines with low profitability” and its profit contribution reached its “best level ever”. The appointment of IXM’s CEO (Buhavac) to CCO of the entire group proves this segment is now at the center of corporate strategy.

Industry Overview: “Electrification” Narrative vs. Physical Reality

The consensus narrative is that CMOC is a “play on the EV revolution.” This is fragile, top-down thinking. We do not bet on narratives. We bet on inelastic demand for physical things.

Market Structure:

  • Copper: A massive, liquid global market. Demand is driven by everything (electrification, construction, grid). Supply is tight.

  • Cobalt: A small, opaque, illiquid market dominated by the DRC (70% of supply) and specifically by CMOC (41% of supply).

  • Niobium: A tiny, niche duopoly (CBMM and CMOC) servicing high-strength steel.

Trends:

  • The Narrative (Fragile): “EVs will drive infinite cobalt demand.” Reality: This narrative is breaking. High prices are forcing substitution (LFP batteries), a risk the outgoing CCO explicitly flagged.

  • The Fact (Anti-Fragile): The real story is supply destruction. The world cannot “de-risk” from the DRC; it is geologically impossible. And it cannot de-risk from CMOC, which is the DRC’s production.

  • The Regime Shift: The DRC quota is a regime shift. It is the formal cartelization of the cobalt market. This guarantees higher prices, which benefits the lowest-cost producer (CMOC) the most.

Revenue Trends & Drivers, Margins, and Profitability

This is where the thesis becomes mathematically provable.

Revenue trends

  • Overall Trends: Revenues are forecast to be relatively flat-to-modest growth (2024F-2027E). This is good. It shows the thesis is not about revenue growth; it’s about profitability.

  • The Disconnect: H1 2025 revenue was down 7.83% Y/Y. But...

  • Profit Trends:...Net Profit was up 60.07% Y/Y. Q3 2025 YTD profit is up 72.6% Y/Y. This is pure operating leverage.

  • Volumes: The key driver. H1 2025 Copper production 12.68% Y/Y, Cobalt 13.05% Y/Y. CMOC is ramping volume into a market where the regulator is trying to restrict volume.

  • Pricing: The DRC quota is putting a floor under cobalt prices. Copper prices are also strong.

Margins and ROCE analysis

Summary of Cost Structure: The H1 2025 call was a master class in cost control. Operating costs fell 10.96%. Financial expenses decreased by 44%. The new CFO (Chen) is clearly working.

COGS & Gross Margins: The mining segment is a cash-gushing machine. H1 2025 gross profit margin for mining was 52%. Cobalt margins were 61.8%, and Tungsten margins were 64.3%. The forecasts show consolidated Gross Margin expanding from 16.46% (2024F) to 20.05% (2027F). This is the entire story.

OPEX & Operating Margin: EBITDA margins are forecast to expand from 16.11% to 19.37%. This is the operating leverage of (a) scaling volume over a fixed asset base and (b) the new “manufacturing discipline” from CEO Peng Xuhui.

ROCE: Weighted Average ROE was 11.70% in H1 2025, up 2.88 percentage points Y/Y. This is a strong return for a “mining” company and reflects superior asset quality.

The H1 2025 actuals (60% profit growth, EBITDA margins over 20%) demonstrate that the full-year 2025F consensus forecasts are conservative. The company is already beating these high growth expectations.

The market’s 2026F forecast for only 4.09% growth is ludicrously low. It reflects a total disbelief in the KFM Phase II ramp, price stability, or both. This is the mispricing.

Management: The “Year of Change” and the New Killers

The most bullish signal of 2025 was not the earnings; it was the purge. On October 24, 2025, the company announced the resignation of the CEO (Sun Ruiwen) and CCO (Kenny Ives) and the appointment of a new CEO (Peng Xuhui) and CCO (Branko Buhavac).

This was not a “panic” move. It was a deliberate, planned pivot from a “building” team to an “operating” team, as signaled in the “2025 Year of Change” initiative. The market sees “turnover risk.” We see the installation of a specialist team to execute the next phase of growth.

This is a purpose-built team. The Board has separated the key roles:

  1. Liu (Chairman) allocates the portfolio.

  2. Peng (CEO) optimizes the system.

  3. Que (COO) optimizes the mine.

  4. Branko (CCO) optimizes the trade.

  5. Chen (CFO) optimizes the cost.
    The market is misreading this organizational sophistication as “instability.”

Present: Reinvesting.

Valuation, Reverse Thesis, and Conclusion

Valuation & Margin of Safety

The valuation is a joke. It is the punchline to the market’s misunderstanding.

  1. The PEG Ratio: The 2025F PEG ratio is 0.69. The market is pricing in 34.5% EPS growth at a massive discount. This single data point is the thesis.

  2. Operating Leverage: The market is ignoring the margin expansion story. H1 2025 Net Profit +60% Y/Y on declining revenue. Q3 YTD Net Profit +72.6% Y/Y. The analyst consensus for 2026 showing a deceleration to 4% growth is demonstrably false and ignores the KFM II ramp.

  3. Analyst Targets (The “Experts”): The sell-side is systematically behind reality. Their models are broken.

    • The “consensus” 12-month target for the A-Share is CNY 12.86-13.99. The stock is already at CNY 15.90.

    • The “consensus” for the H-Share is HK$9.15. The stock is at HK$14.72.

    • Morgan Stanley has a HK$11.70 target. Citi raised their target to *only* HK$13.40 in August. They cannot keep up. Do not trust their models. Trust the provable cash flow.

  4. Margin of Safety: The margin of safety is not in the balance sheet (P/BV is 4.4x). The margin of safety is:

    • (a) The price (PEG 0.69).

    • (b) The monopoly (41% cobalt share).

    • (c) The non-DRC assets (Brazil Niobium, China Moly) which provide a16% G.P. backstop.

    • (d) The political reality: The DRC needs CMOC’s 600M+ in annual tax receipts more than CMOC needs the DRC’s drama.

Reverse Thesis (How We Go To Zero)

We must be cynical. How does this blow up?

Successful Expropriation (The “Idiot” Scenario): The DRC government does the irrational thing and actually expropriates TFM/KFM. Not a “dispute” (which they settled for $800M), but a full seizure. This would vaporize 84% of the mining G.P.. Likelihood: Low. You don’t kill your golden goose.

Management “Dream Team” Fails: The new team (Liu, Peng, Que, Chen, Buhavac) is a collection of all-stars who cannot play together. Execution on KFM II fumbles, costs overrun the $1.084B budget, and the “manufacturing discipline” (Peng) fails to materialize. Likelihood: Medium. Integration risk is real.

Cobalt Goes to Zero (The “Technological” Scenario): The Kenny Ives worry comes true, faster than anyone expects. OEMs perfect LFP/Sodium-Ion batteries, and cobalt demand structurally collapses. The DRC quota accelerates this R&D. Likelihood: High (long-term), Low (18-36 month thesis horizon).

The “Two-Pole” Trap: The new Ecuador gold project turns into a money pit, sucking away the DRC cash flow just as the DRC also increases its “tax” demands. The company is whipsawed between two unstable jurisdictions. Likelihood: Medium. This is a non-trivial risk.

Conclusion

The investment case for CMOC Group Limited is an exercise in second-level thinking.

The first-level thinker sees: A Chinese company in the DRC. They see “political risk,” “management turnover,” and “export bans”. They see fragility. They sell.

The second-level thinker sees:

  1. The “risk” (DRC chaos) is the moat. It’s why competitors (Glencore) are weak and why CMOC could acquire these world-class assets from a fleeing Freeport.

  2. The “export ban” is a gift. It’s a forced cartelization that raises the price of the commodity where CMOC is the lowest-cost, 41%-market-share monopolist.

  3. The “management turnover” is a masterstroke. It is a deliberate pivot from a “building” team to a world-class “operating” team of specialists. This new team is why the margin expansion is believable.

  4. The “valuation” (PEG 0.69) is a glaring mispricing. It is the market’s fee for filtering out the first-level thinkers.

We have yet to decide a long position but we can see how one would be long CMOC because of the provable facts 41% market share, 52% mining margins, +72.6% YTD profit growth) over fragile narratives (”DRC risk”). The company is successfully converting geopolitical chaos into anti-fragile, diversified cash flow. However this could a high-conviction long.
