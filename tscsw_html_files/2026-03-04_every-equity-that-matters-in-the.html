Macro - Capital Cycles, Debt, and “The Blob”

For the past decade, Hyperscaler capex was largely funded by internal operating cash flow (OCF). However, the sheer magnitude of the current cycle, projected to reach $600 billion by 2026, has broken this model.

Funding Gap (and Debt usage)

Historically, the “Big Tech” cohort funded growth purely through the prodigious cash generation of their core monopolies (Search, E-commerce, Operating Systems). The AI infrastructure cycle has obviously reversed this. The capital intensity required to purchase NVIDIA GPUs, construct gigawatt-scale facilities, and secure energy assets has outstripped these massive cash flows.

Recent analysis indicates a structural shift in financing. Oracle, Amazon, and Meta have all recently tapped the debt markets with multi-billion dollar issuances. The AI infrastructure boom is no longer self-funded (hyperscalers are using debt). For example, Oracle’s recent $18 billion bond issuance and Amazon’s $15 billion raise highlight a critical reality: the cash flow from legacy businesses is insufficient to fund the GPU-intensive future without leverage.

This financing creates a “winner-takes-all” situation. Only entities with investment-grade credit ratings and access to deep capital markets can sustain this level of investment. For the equity investor in the supply chain, this reduces the risk of project cancellation for downstream suppliers (Vertiv, Eaton, Modine). The Hyperscalers are committed via bond covenants and strategic necessity; these are not projects that will be cancelled due to a single quarter of missed earnings. The debt covenants and the maturity schedules (extending into the 2030s and 2040s) is a long-term commitment to asset buildout.

The volume of issuance is mind boggling. In 2025 alone, Big Tech bond issuance reached $108 billion, a five-fold increase from previous years. This flood of supply impacts credit spreads, but crucially, it provides the “fuel” for the physical infrastructure buildout we are analysing.

The Burry Thesis - Accounting Irregularities or Fundamental Misunderstanding?

It is crucial to address the contrarian view to maintain intellectual honesty (which we have done many times). Michael Burry has stated that Hyperscalers are artificially inflating earnings by extending the useful life of their server assets, thereby understating depreciation expenses. He estimates this could overstate profits by up to $176 billion through 2028.

While directionally accurate regarding accounting mechanics, extending useful life does indeed boost near-term EPS, Burry misses the fundamental shift in the nature of the asset.

The H100/Blackwell clusters are not commodity x86 servers that become obsolete in 3 years. They are massively parallel supercomputers with a longer viable economic life in inference workloads even after they are surpassed for training (well, not always, but this isn’t the point of the post).

Cap Rates & Cost of Capital

The real estate economics of data centers are also changing, creating a divergence between public and private valuations. Cap rates (the ratio of a property's income to its price, a key real estate valuation metric) for turnkey data centers have compressed to the mid-6% range, while powered shells trade near 5%. This compression is occurring despite elevated borrowing costs, creating a negative leverage situation for some private developers who are borrowing at rates higher than their development yields.

However, for Data Center REITs (Digital Realty, Equinix), the “public-private valuation gap” creates an arbitrage opportunity.

Digital Realty (DLR ) and Equinix (EQIX ) are the dominant public pure-plays. DLR owns over 300 facilities globally with a focus on hyperscale campuses; their land bank in Northern Virginia (Ashburn) is effectively irreplaceable. Equinix operates 260+ facilities with a differentiated "interconnection" model: they charge premiums for cross-connects between tenants, creating recurring revenue beyond simple lease income. Both can raise equity capital at costs below private developers' borrowing rates, enabling accretive development. European exposure comes through Segro ( SGRO.L), which controls 2.3 GW of "power bank" across key European hubs.

Public REITs with access to lower-cost equity capital can acquire private assets or develop at yields (8-10% on cost) that are accretive relative to their weighted average cost of capital (WACC, the blended cost of debt and equity financing). The relationship between the 10-Year Treasury yield and REIT cap rates has decoupled slightly, driven by the intense scarcity of “powered land.”

This “powered land” scarcity is the defining constraint of the real estate layer. It is no longer about “location, location, location” in the traditional sense of proximity to population centers, but rather proximity to high-voltage transmission lines and fiber backbones.

Section 1: Power Generation

The most acute bottleneck in the AI value chain is Power Availability (see our posts on Data Centers & Copper). Transmission queues in major hubs (Northern Virginia, Silicon Valley) extend 3-5 years. This physical reality forces data center operators to adopt “Prime Power” strategies, generating their own electricity on-site rather than relying solely on the grid.

1.1 The Generator Duopoly: Caterpillar (CAT ) vs. Cummins (CMI )

Generators are transitioning from “standby” assets (used only during outages, perhaps 20 hours a year) to “mission-critical” assets that may run for extended periods during peak load or grid delays. This shift from standby to prime or continuous rating fundamentally changes the wear-and-tear profile and the replacement cycle of the equipment.

Caterpillar (CAT)

Caterpillar is a dominant force in large-scale power generation (Not just a construction company!). The company has aggressively changed to meet data center demand, increasing its data center engine manufacturing capacity by 125% compared to just two years ago. This is a massive industrial signal. CAT management sees this as a structural shift in demand.

From a first-principles engineering perspective, CAT’s dominance lies in its large-displacement diesel engines which offer superior “block load” capability. When a data center loses grid power, the generators must start and accept the entire electrical load of the facility within seconds. This “step load” can stall lesser engines. CAT’s engines are engineered with high rotational inertia and impressive fuel injection systems to handle these massive instantaneous torque demands without fluctuating frequency, which could damage sensitive IT equipment.

Financially, the Power Systems segment is outgrowing the traditional Construction Industries segment. While construction is cyclical and tied to interest rates, data center power demand is secular and tied to the AI arms race. Caterpillar could triple its annual data center power revenue by 2030.

Cummins (CMI): The Challenger

Cummins is the primary competitor to Caterpillar in this space. While its Engine segment remains the largest revenue contributor ($11.7B), the Power Systems segment is seeing faster growth. Cummins has differentiated itself by aggressively targeting the “bridge” market with HVO (Hydrotreated Vegetable Oil) compatible engines.

HVO is a paraffinic diesel fuel that can be used as a drop-in replacement for fossil diesel. It reduces net CO2 emissions by up to 90%. For data center operators like Microsoft and Google, who have “net zero” commitments, the ability to run their backup generators on HVO is a critical compliance tool. Cummins has certified its entire high-horsepower engine range for use with HVO, allowing operators to claim carbon reductions even while utilizing internal combustion technology.

1.2 Fuel Cell Arbitrage - Bloom Energy (BE )

Bloom Energy has emerged as a unique play on the “Time-to-Power” constraint. In many jurisdictions, the wait for a utility grid connection can exceed four years. A Bloom Energy Server (Solid Oxide Fuel Cell) can be deployed in as little as 90 days.

The Thermodynamics of Solid Oxide Fuel Cells (SOFC)

Bloom's Solid Oxide Fuel Cells operate at high temperatures (around 800°C), enabling up to 65% electrical efficiency, significantly higher than traditional turbines. This means less gas consumed per kilowatt-hour.

The “AI” Fit

AI workloads are “oscillating”, they spike during training runs and trough during idle periods. Traditional turbines prefer steady-state operation; rapid ramping causes thermal stress and efficiency loss. Bloom’s system utilizes an intermediary storage buffer of ultra-capacitors and batteries to handle these transient loads. The fuel cells charge the storage at a steady rate, and the storage handles the millisecond-level spikes of the GPUs. This architecture is ideally suited for the erratic load profile of an AI training cluster.

Strategic Validation & Financials

The recent partnership with CoreWeave and Brookfield to deploy megawatts of fuel cells is a validation of the “island mode” data center model. This deal involves deploying fuel cells as the primary power source, completely bypassing the utility grid. Financially, Bloom is pivoting from “unprofitable tech” to “industrial staple,” recently reporting positive operating cash flow and a record revenue backlog.

Grid constraints are forcing hyperscalers toward unconventional power sources.

Microsoft’s Three Mile Island restart, Amazon’s Talen Energy deal, and Google’s Kairos partnership signal a structural shift toward nuclear. We will cover Constellation Energy (CEG), Vistra (VST), and the SMR plays (NuScale, Oklo) in Part 2.

1.4 Battery Safety - Vertiv & ZincFive

As rack densities rise, the UPS (Uninterruptible Power Supply) batteries must sit closer to the servers to reduce DC cable losses (voltage drop is proportional to distance). This creates a fire risk. Lithium-Ion batteries, while energy-dense, are prone to thermal runaway, a catastrophic failure mode where the battery creates its own oxygen, making it nearly impossible to extinguish with standard suppression systems.

ZincFive (Nickel-Zinc) - The Safer Chemistry

Nickel-Zinc (NiZn) chemistry offers a distinct safety advantage from a first-principles perspective. Unlike Lithium-Ion, NiZn does not utilize flammable organic electrolytes and does not exhibit thermal runaway. If a cell fails, it does so benignly, without igniting its neighbors.

This safety profile allows ZincFive battery cabinets to be placed in the row directly alongside servers, saving valuable “whitespace” in the data center. Vertiv has recognized this advantage and integrated ZincFive cabinets into its flagship UPS lines. This exclusivity gives Vertiv a safety differentiation that competitors relying on generic Li-Ion lack. For data center operators, the choice is between risking a catastrophic fire that could destroy a billion-dollar cluster or using a chemically safer alternative.

Section 2: Thermal Management

We are currently hitting a “Heat Wall.” Air cooling (fans blowing over heat sinks) becomes physically inefficient.

2.1 Vertiv (VRT )

Vertiv has positioned itself as the “reference architecture” partner for NVIDIA, effectively co-engineering the cooling systems for the next generation of GPU clusters. Their market position is defined by the Coolant Distribution Unit (CDU).

The Mechanism of the CDU

The Coolant Distribution Unit (CDU) is the heart of liquid cooling: it isolates the facility water from the clean coolant loop that touches chips, managing pumps, heat exchange, and flow control. Vertiv has become Nvidia's 'reference architecture' partner for these systems.

Financial Momentum

Vertiv’s financial metrics reflect this technological leadership. The company recently reported a book-to-bill ratio of 1.4x (meaning for every $100 shipped, they receive $140 in new orders). This backlog accumulation provides high visibility into future revenue. Simultaneously, the company has deleveraged, bringing net leverage down to 0.5x. This combination of hyper-growth and balance sheet strength is rare in the industrial sector and justifies a premium valuation multiple.

2.2 Modine (MOD ) / Airedale

Modine has successfully transformed from a vehicular radiator company (a commoditized, cyclical business) to a data center cooling powerhouse via its Airedale brand.

The $180 Million Validation

Modine recently secured a massive $180 million order from a “leading AI infrastructure developer”. While the customer was not named, industry rumors and the scale of the order makes it likely that it is CoreWeave or a Hyperscaler pilot. This order serves as a crucial validation point: Modine is a strategic partner for the largest deployments in the world.

Digital Twins and “Cooling AI”

Beyond hardware, Modine is innovating in software. Their “Cooling AI” digital twin technology allows operators to simulate the thermal performance of a data center before it is built. By creating a virtual replica, Modine can optimize the control logic of the chillers to react to AI load spikes preemptively, reducing energy consumption. This “software-defined cooling” creates a recurring revenue tailwind and increases customer stickiness.

2.3 nVent (NVT )

nVent provides the veins and arteries.

Liquid Flow Through & VITA Standards

nVent’s technological edge comes from its usage in military electronics. Their “Liquid Flow Through” modules adhere to VITA 48.4 standards, which were originally designed for cooling high-performance radar and avionics systems in harsh environments. These standards dictate precise specifications for pressure tolerance, vibration resistance, and leak prevention.

The “Dripless” Value Proposition

The greatest fear of any data center operator is water leaking onto a $500,000 server rack. nVent addresses this with “blind mate” dripless connectors. These couplings allow a server blade to be inserted into a liquid-cooled chassis and connected to the water loop simultaneously, with zero spillage upon disconnection. This mechanical precision is a high barrier to entry; a cheap plastic connector can cause millions in damage. nVent is expanding its manufacturing capacity in Blaine, MN, specifically to meet the surging demand for these liquid cooling components.

2.4 Submer (Private Market Signal)

While public markets focus on cold plates, the private markets are funding Immersion Cooling, submerging the entire server in a bath of non-conductive dielectric fluid. Submer, a Barcelona-based startup, recently raised $55.5 million to scale this technology.

Immersion cooling eliminates fans entirely, potentially reducing the energy consumption of a data center by 50%. While currently a niche solution for the most extreme densities (hpc/crypto), investments from heavyweights like Planet First Partners signal that this technology could move mainstream as chip densities push past the limits of even cold plate liquid cooling.

Section 3: Electrical Infrastructure

The electrical chain is bifurcating into two distinct methods: Switchgear (Medium Voltage) and Busway (Low Voltage/Last Meter).

3.1 Switchgear: Eaton (ETN ) vs. Schneider Electric (SU )

Medium voltage switchgear sits at the entrance of the data center, taking high-voltage power from the utility and stepping it down for distribution. A major regulatory shift has disrupted the market: the banning of SF6 (Sulfur Hexafluoride). SF6 is a potent greenhouse gas (23,500 times more warming potential than CO2) commonly used as an insulator in switchgear.

Eaton - First-Mover Advantage

Eaton bet early on Vacuum Interruption technology, which uses a vacuum bottle rather than gas to extinguish the electrical arc when a circuit is broken. This technology is inherently SF6-free. As regulations in Europe (and increasingly the US) tighten against SF6, Eaton finds itself with a compliant product portfolio ready to ship, while competitors are still pivoting.

Schneider’s Response

Schneider Electric is rolling out its “AirSeT” technology, which uses pure air as the insulator. While an effective solution, Eaton’s legacy lead in vacuum technology gives it a manufacturing and reliability track record that appeals to risk-averse data center engineers.

Margin Expansion

Eaton’s Electrical Global segment is posting record margins of approximately 24.7%. This is significantly higher than the industrial average and reflects the “complexity premium” of data center projects. These are custom-engineered assemblies, giving Eaton pricing power.

3.2 Busway: Legrand vs. Atkore (Last Meter)

Busway is the overhead track that distributes power to server racks. It has largely replaced the “whip” (cables under the floor) method because it allows for faster reconfiguration of the data hall.

Legrand (Starline) - Flexible

Legrand’s acquisition of Starline was a strategic win. Starline is the “Kleenex” of data center busway. Its key differentiator is its continuous open-channel design, which allows power plugs (tap-off boxes) to be inserted at any point along the track. Competing systems often have fixed plug-in points every foot or two. For AI clusters, where rack widths and densities vary, the infinite flexibility of Starline is a massive operational advantage.

Atkore (Unistrut) - Value Trap?

Atkore is a formidable competitor with its Unistrut and Cope brands. However, the stock is currently facing significant headwinds. Atkore was a massive beneficiary of the pandemic-era spike in PVC and steel conduit pricing. As these commodity prices have normalized, Atkore’s EBITDA margins are compressing from their unsustainable highs.30 While their data center volume is growing, it is being masked by the deflation in their core electrical conduit business. For a pure data center play, Legrand’s Starline division is the superior asset, though it is diluted by Legrand’s broader building products portfolio.

3.3 Connectors: Amphenol (APH ) TE Connectivity (TEL )

High-speed data centers require specialized connectors that are easy to overlook but difficult to replicate. Two companies dominate:

Amphenol (APH): Amphenol manufactures the high-speed optical connectors for 800G and future 1.6T modules, power connectors rated for liquid-cooled racks, and backplane interconnects. Their data center segment is growing above 30% annually with operating margins exceeding 25%. Unlike commodity cables, these are precision-engineered components with meaningful qualification cycles, creating switching costs.

TE Connectivity (TEL): TE provides similar connectivity solutions with particular strength in power distribution. Their “High-Speed Computing” segment directly addresses AI infrastructure needs. Both companies benefit from every rack deployed, regardless of whether it contains Nvidia, AMD, or custom silicon.

Section 4: Connectivity

AI models are trained on thousands of chips acting as a single computer. This requires massive bandwidth and low-latency interconnects.

4.1 Corning (GLW ) & RocketRibbon

Corning has introduced a structural innovation in fiber optics: RocketRibbon.

The Geometry of Density

Traditional ribbon cables are flat and rigid, making them difficult to route through crowded conduits. RocketRibbon utilizes a “flow ribbon” design that allows fibers to bundle like a flexible yarn while maintaining the organization required for mass splicing. This geometry allows Corning to pack 3,456 fibers into the same duct space that used to hold 1,728.

Financial Impact: The “Springboard” Plan

Hyperscalers are running out of conduit space. By doubling the fiber count in existing ducts, RocketRibbon saves them from digging new trenches, a massive Capex saving. Corning projects its Optical Communications operating margin to hit 20% a year ahead of schedule due to the rich mix of these high-density AI cables. This is a clear example of how a physical innovation translates directly to margin expansion.

The physical cabling layer extends beyond fiber. Power cables rated for high-amperage liquid-cooled racks are another constraint. Prysmian (PRY.MI) and Nexans (NEX.PA) are the European leaders in high-voltage cabling, increasingly relevant as facilities require dedicated grid interconnections. Upstream copper exposure (Freeport-McMoRan, Southern Copper) offers indirect leverage to the buildout but with commodity price volatility.

4.2 CommScope (COMM ) - Distressed Competitor

CommScope offers competing 'Rollable Ribbon' technology but is hamstrung by heavy debt and ongoing restructuring, making it a risky supply chain partner for hyperscalers who prioritize stability.

Section 5: Physical Infrastructure & Safety

5.1 Structural: Kingspan/Tate

AI racks are heavy. A fully loaded NVIDIA NVL72 rack can weigh over 2,000 lbs. This weight challenges the structural integrity of traditional raised floors.

The Structural Ceiling Pivot

Kingspan’s subsidiary, Tate, is the dominant player in raised access floors. However, recognizing the trend towards slab-on-grade (concrete) floors for heavy AI racks, Tate has successfully pivoted to Structural Ceilings. These are heavy-duty grids suspended from the building shell that support the massive weight of power cables, busways, and liquid cooling pipes above the racks.

Kingspan’s Sustainability Moat

Kingspan is also leveraging its “Planet Passionate” sustainability program to differentiate. They are targeting a trading profit of €200m by 2027 for their data solutions division. By offering low-embodied-carbon materials (using recycled steel and renewable energy in manufacturing), they appeal to the ESG mandates of the Hyperscalers.

5.2 Acoustic Suppression: Johnson Controls (JCI )

A hidden “first principles” risk in data centers is Sound.

The Physics of Hard Drive Failure

Inert gas fire suppression systems release gas at supersonic speeds to extinguish fires quickly. The resulting acoustic shockwave can reach levels exceeding 130dB. This intense sound pressure wave can cause the platters in Hard Disk Drives (HDDs) to vibrate. Even microscopic vibrations can cause the read/write heads to off-track, leading to data corruption or permanent drive failure.

The Silent Nozzle Solution

Johnson Controls (via its Tyco brand) developed Acoustic Nozzles (silent nozzles) that diffuse the gas to reduce sound power below the 110dB threshold where HDD damage occurs.

This is a critical, high-margin niche product. It is a “check-the-box” requirement for any facility housing storage, granting Johnson Controls a defensive moat.

5.3 Access Control: Assa Abloy & Napco

Data centers are fortresses. The physical security layer is dominated by two players: Assa Abloy (HID Global) and Napco Security.

HID Global (Assa Abloy): HID is the standard for enterprise access control. Their “Mobile Access” solution, which allows employees to use phones instead of badges, is gaining traction. The integration with Apple Wallet is a key differentiator for user experience in high-end colocation facilities.

Napco Security (NSSC): Napco is a faster-growing, smaller player focusing on the “Air Access” cloud-based platform. Their recurring service revenue is growing at double-digit rates, driven by the shift to cloud-managed security. For investors, Napco offers higher growth potential but with higher volatility compared to the steady-state industrial giant Assa Abloy.

Part 1 Conclusion

Its important you know that the winners in this phase are not necessarily the companies with the best “AI story,” but those with the best physics-based solutions to the hard constraints of Power, Heat, and Space. Move beyond “buying NVIDIA” to “buying the infrastructure that makes NVIDIA work.”

Key Investment Takeaways:

1. Long the “Constraint Solvers”: Vertiv (VRT) and Eaton (ETN) own the critical path of power and cooling. Vertiv is the high-beta play on heat density; Eaton is the quality play on electrification. Their valuations are rich, but their order books justify the premium.

2. Monitor the Debt Markets: The “Funding Gap” is real. Watch for spread widening in Amazon/Oracle bonds as a leading indicator of capex fatigue.

3. The “Hidden” Industrial Alphas: Companies like Modine (MOD) (thermal) and Corning (GLW) (optical) offer specific, high-margin technologies (Airedale chillers, RocketRibbon) that are indispensable to the AI architecture but trade at lower multiples than pure-play AI stocks.

4. Avoid Commodity Exposure: In the “Grey Space” (infrastructure), avoid companies with commoditized products (standard cabling, basic metal framing) lacking the intellectual property moats of a Starline busway or an Acoustic Nozzle. Atkore’s margin compression is a warning sign here.

The physical world is the ultimate constraint. The equities that solve for these constraints, through superior chemistry, thermodynamics, and engineering, will capture the lion’s share of the trillion-dollar expenditure.
