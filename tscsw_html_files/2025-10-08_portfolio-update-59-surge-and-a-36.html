From De-Risking to Re-Rating

This section is dedicated to our positions where major, thesis-confirming catalysts have not just occurred but have fundamentally altered the investment landscape for each company.

Perpetua Resources ($PPTA.TSE): The Sovereign Backstop

Our position in Perpetua Resources, now up an incredible 58.93% since inception, has been predicated on the market mispricing the immense strategic value of its Stibnite Gold Project. In September, two developments transformed this thesis from potential to proof. First, on September 8th, Perpetua announced it had received a Preliminary Project Letter and a non-binding indicative financing term sheet from the Export-Import Bank of the United States (”EXIM”) for potential debt financing of up to $2 billion.

This is far more than a loan commitment. EXIM is an official export credit agency of the U.S. Federal Government. Its involvement signifies that the Stibnite project, set to become the only domestic mined source of the critical defense material antimony, is deemed vital to U.S. national and economic security. This is a geopolitical and strategic de-risking event of the highest order, providing a sovereign stamp of approval that dramatically reduces the political and permitting risk that has historically suppressed the stock’s valuation. This government backstop provides Perpetua with immense leverage in all other financing discussions, significantly lowering its future cost of capital and maximizing value for equity holders.

Second, on September 19th, the company received the critical Notice to Proceed from the U.S. Forest Service, authorizing the commencement of initial project development. This notice moves the project from a theoretical possibility to a tangible reality, clearing the final major administrative hurdle. With early works construction expected to begin this fall, the narrative has shifted permanently from “if” to “when,” forcing the market to re-price the equity accordingly.

Weatherford International ($WFRD.NASDAQ)

Our thesis in Weatherford, now up 28.80%, was that the company’s powerful internal operational turnaround was far stronger than its languishing stock price and credit profile suggested. On September 22nd, we received the ultimate external validation of this view. The company announced it had received credit rating upgrades from all three major agencies: Moody’s (to ‘Ba2’), S&P (to ‘BB’), and Fitch (to ‘BB’).

Credit rating agencies are conservative, data-driven, and typically lag operational reality. Their simultaneous upgrades are a lagging but irrefutable signal to the broader market that Weatherford’s financial health is fundamentally and sustainably improved. This validation, which coincided with a series of successful balance sheet optimizations including a $1.2 billion debt refinancing, attracts more conservative institutional investors, lowers the company’s cost of capital, and creates a virtuous cycle of positive perception that continues to drive the stock higher. The rest of the Street is now playing catch-up.

Quiet Compounders: Execution Over Noise

Not every holding requires a dramatic catalyst. This section highlights companies performing well by simply executing their business plans flawlessly, creating value far from the market’s noisy headlines.

Alamos Gold ($AGI.TSE): Sharpening the Focus

Alamos Gold, up 39.85%, has demonstrated how intelligent capital allocation can unlock significant shareholder value. The most significant development was the announcement on September 14th of the sale of its Turkish development projects for $470 million in cash.

This is a masterstroke. The Turkish assets, while promising, carried significant geopolitical risk that acted as an overhang on the company’s valuation. Selling them for a substantial sum removes this risk entirely, simplifies the investment story, and allows management to focus 100% of its attention and capital on its core, low-risk, high-return North American assets. This move, combined with a consistent dividend (reaffirmed August 28th) and recognition as a top performer on the TSX30 list, is forcing the market to reward Alamos with the premium valuation it deserves.

Conviction: Value Meets Price

This section addresses our underperformers, which we view not as failures, but as opportunities born from the market’s impatience.

Lectra ($LSS.EURONEXT): The Patience Test Continues

Lectra remains our only holding in the red, down 4.26% since inception. The market has placed the company in the “penalty box” for withdrawing its 2025 guidance earlier this year. However, the news flow since our last update has been purely operational and, crucially, devoid of any negative financial updates. The company has announced new customer wins like Manor and IKKS, published insights on new technology, and appointed a new President for the Americas. The stock’s weakness is driven by sentiment, not by a failing business. The complete absence of fundamental deterioration is a strongly positive signal that the company’s multi-year transition to a higher-margin SaaS model is proceeding under the surface. The market’s impatience is creating an opportunity.

Karnov Group ($KAR.STO) & Asseco Poland ($ACP.WAR): The Disconnect Deepens

For both of these European niche leaders, the disconnect between positive fundamental progress and stagnant stock performance has widened, making them even more attractive. Karnov, down 6.05%, reported strong Q2 results on August 21st showing accelerating organic growth, providing direct proof that our “digital toll road” thesis is strengthening. Asseco Poland, up a modest 1.46%, not only reported robust H1 results on September 3rd (8% sales growth, 14% profit growth) but also saw Topicus.com (a subsidiary of the world-class software compounder Constellation Software) complete its acquisition of a 14.84% stake on October 1st. This is a massive vote of confidence from a highly sophisticated strategic investor. The market’s current indifference to this positive news is a classic small-cap inefficiency our strategy is designed to exploit.

Whitbread ($WTB.LSE) & Kitwave ($KITW.LSE): Executing the Plan

These UK-based holdings are firmly “thesis-on-track.” For Whitbread, the most significant action has been the consistent execution of its share buyback program, a tangible, capital-backed statement from management that they believe their own stock is undervalued. For Kitwave, an August 11th announcement changing its fiscal year-end to better align with business seasonality is a small but sensible shareholder-friendly move. In both cases, management is quietly executing the plan, and the core theses remain perfectly intact.
