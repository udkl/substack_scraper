Financial Analysis

To understand why the market’s reaction to Q3 was boring, you have to appreciate the obsession retail investors and algorithmic traders have with “The Beat.” Wesdome reported an Earnings Per Share (EPS) of CAD 0.58 against a forecast of CAD 0.60.

In the binary world of headline trading, that 3.33% miss is a red flag. It triggers sell programs. It causes the casual observer to think, “Oh, they missed. Next.”

But this is a lazy, surface-level interpretation that misses the big change in the company’s capitalization. If you ignore the operational noise for a second and look strictly at the P&L and Balance Sheet, Wesdome looks less like a struggling miner and more like a printing press.

Top-Line Explosion

Revenue hit CAD 230 million, beating forecasts by nearly 9%. That is a 57% jump year-over-year. In mining, where guys are usually happy to flatline production and pray for gold price hikes, this is volume-driven growth amplified by pricing.

This revenue figure isn’t just a function of the gold price being high; it’s a function of Eagle River pushing high-grade ore through the mill at record rates.

The quality of this revenue is high. Sold ounces were 47,400 oz, up 10% year-over-year. This confirms that the revenue beat was driven by actual metal leaving the gate.

Margin Expansion: The Real Story

The most impressive metric in the entire report (and the one the market slept on) is the explosion in profitability.

  • Gross Profit: Increased by 112% year-over-year to CAD 149.0 million.

  • Operating Cash Margin: Grew 78% to CAD 168.4 million.

  • EBITDA: Increased by 77% to CAD 150 million.

Let’s do the math on that. If Revenue is CAD 230 million and EBITDA is CAD 150 million, Wesdome is running at a 65% EBITDA margin. That is elite territory. For context, most mid-tier producers fight to maintain margins in the 40-50% range.

Wesdome is converting 65 cents of every dollar of revenue into operating cash flow before tax and depreciation. This level of efficiency is typically reserved for royalty companies or open-pit mines with near-zero strip ratios, not underground operations in the Canadian Shield. It speaks to the incredible grade leverage at Eagle River.

The Cash Flow Machine

The ultimate arbiter of value is Free Cash Flow (FCF). Earnings can be manipulated with non-cash items; cash is truth. Wesdome’s free cash flow grew 2.5 times compared to the previous period.

  • Cash on Hand: CAD 266 million.

  • YTD Increase: CAD 143 million.

  • Free Cash Flow Margin: 34%.

This is a significant shift in risk profile. At the start of 2024, Wesdome had CAD 123 million in cash (CAD 266M current - CAD 143M increase). They have effectively doubled their liquidity in nine months. A mining company with zero net debt and a quarter-billion dollars in the bank has options. They can weather a storm, they can buy a competitor, or they can return capital to shareholders.

Buyback Signal (NCIB)

Management isn’t sitting on their hands. They just received TSX approval for a Normal Course Issuer Bid (NCIB) to buy back up to 3,013,315 shares, representing approximately 2% of the public float.

  • Translation: Management believes the stock is undervalued.

  • Cynical Take: They don’t have a better M&A target right now, so they are investing in themselves.

  • Strategic Take: Given the disconnect between their financial performance and their share price, this is the smartest allocation of capital available. Buying back shares at ~CAD 20 when you are printing cash at this rate is accretive to the remaining shareholders. It also puts a floor under the stock price, signaling to the shorts that there is a committed buyer in the market.

Operational Overview

This is where the story gets slightly uncomfortable. Wesdome isn’t one monolithic company; it is two distinct operations suffering from bipolar disorder. To understand the risk and the opportunity, you have to decouple Eagle River from Kiena. One is a golden goose; the other is currently an albatross.

Eagle River: The Golden Goose

Eagle River is the only reason the stock hasn’t collapsed under the weight of Kiena’s drama. This mine is a beast, an anomaly in an industry where grades usually degrade over time. Eagle River has been in production since 1995, and yet it is currently delivering some of the best grades in its history.

Production Metrics

  • Production: 34,296 ounces in Q3 2025. That’s up 31% year-over-year. (Note: Some comparisons suggest 45% depending on the baseline adjustment, but the conservative 31% is impressive enough).

  • Grade: 15.3 g/t. This is up 18% year-over-year from an already respectable level. To put that in perspective, most underground gold mines in Canada operate between 5 and 7 g/t. Eagle River is digging up jewelry boxes.

  • Throughput: 71,575 tonnes milled, an 11% increase.

The Geology of the Beat

How is a 30-year-old mine delivering record grades? Exploration. The company has been aggressively drilling the 300 Zone and the 6 Central Zone. Recent updates highlight intercepts like 115.9 g/t gold over 1.6 meters true width.10 These ultra-high-grade hits are not just anomalous spikes; they are part of a structural trend that the mining engineers are successfully exploiting. The “300 Zone” in particular has been a game-changer, offering coherent, high-grade stopes that allow for efficient mining with minimal dilution.

Execution

Management notes that Eagle River is “on track to deliver production at the high end of its 2025 guidance”. They cite “improved dilution control” and “stronger operating practices” as key drivers. This suggests that the team at Eagle River has dialed in the drill-and-blast parameters to minimize the amount of waste rock ending up in the mill feed. When you are mining 15 g/t ore, every tonne of waste you keep out of the mill is pure profit.

Kiena - The Detractor

If Eagle River is the golden child, Kiena is the teenager crashing the family car into the garage door. Repeatedly.

  • Production: 16,169 ounces. Down 25% year-over-year.

  • Grade: 10.2 g/t. Down 22% year-over-year.

  • Throughput: 50,147 tonnes, down 3%.

The Anatomy of Failure

The Kiena drop wasn’t a single catastrophic event; it was a cascade of failures, some mechanical, some human.

1. The Hoist Failure:

In July 2025, Kiena suffered a “longer than planned hoist shutdown”.4 In a deep underground mine like Kiena (where the shaft goes down 920 meters 9), the hoist is the umbilical cord. It brings ore up; it takes men and materials down. If the hoist stops, the mine stops. The shutdown resulted in “more than two weeks without mill feed”.11 For a continuous operation, losing 14 days of production is devastating to quarterly numbers. It disrupts the rhythm of the stope cycle, delays backfill, and creates a bottleneck that takes weeks to clear even after the hoist is fixed.

2. The Presqu’île Disaster:

The Presqu’île Zone is a satellite deposit accessible via a ramp from the surface. The strategic plan was for Presqu’île to provide “supplemental feed”—lower grade but easy tonnage to keep the mill full while the high-grade Kiena Deep ore was mined carefully.

It didn’t work. The quarter saw “contractor execution issues and underperformance at Presqu’île”.2 In plain English: the contractors hired to mine this zone didn’t hit their targets. They moved less rock than promised. This forced the mill to run under capacity or wait for feed, destroying efficiency.

3. Inconsistency:

Management’s commentary is littered with euphemisms like “inconsistent execution” and “limited operational flexibility”.4 This is code for “we are mining hand-to-mouth.” When you have limited flexibility, it means you don’t have enough prepared stopes (working faces) ready to go. If one stope encounters bad ground or equipment failure, you have nowhere else to send the scoop trams. You just sit and wait. Kiena is currently “stope-constrained,” leaving it vulnerable to even minor disruptions.

The Turnaround Thesis

Despite the carnage, management remains bullish on Q4, claiming it will be the “strongest of the year”. The plan relies on opening up three distinct mining horizons:

  1. Kiena Deep: The high-grade core (10.1 g/t reserve grade).

  2. Presqu’île: The bulk tonnage ramp zone.

  3. 136-Level: A reintroduced horizon to provide flexibility.

Bull Case: This creates redundancy. If the contractors at Presqu’île screw up again, the 136-Level can pick up the slack. If the deep hoist has maintenance, the ramp at Presqu’île can still move ore.

Bear Case: Complexity kills. Managing three active horizons requires precise coordination of ventilation, equipment, and labor. If they are already struggling with execution, adding complexity might just lead to more chaos.

Management

You don’t buy mining stocks for the rocks; you buy them for the people who know how to get the rocks out of the ground without going bankrupt. Wesdome has assembled a team that looks less like a standard mid-tier board and more like a turnaround special ops unit. The recent personnel moves are the strongest signal in this entire analysis.

Philip Yee - Kirkland Lake DNA

This is the alpha. Philip Yee was appointed CFO effective September 29, 2025. To the uninitiated, a CFO appointment sounds boring. To those who followed the gold sector from 2016 to 2020, this is a siren.

The Resume:

Philip Yee was the Executive Vice President and CFO of Kirkland Lake Gold (KL) from 2016 to 2018. During his tenure (and the subsequent years where his systems were in place), Kirkland Lake Gold was the best performing gold stock on the planet. They took the Fosterville mine in Australia and the Macassa mine in Ontario (high-grade, deep underground assets) and turned them into cash flow monsters through ruthless cost control and operational optimization. Before that, he was CFO at Lake Shore Gold 6, another success story in the Timmins camp.

The Move:

Here is the nuance: Yee was already on the Wesdome Board of Directors. He had a bird’s-eye view of the company. He saw the mess at Kiena, he saw the cash generation at Eagle River, and he decided to step down from the Board governance role into the operational C-suite role.

This is rare. Board members usually enjoy their quarterly meetings and stock options. They don’t jump into the trenches unless:

  1. They see a massive financial upside they can unlock personally.

  2. The situation is so critical that it requires their specific skillset to fix.

The Implication:

Yee is a “wartime” CFO. He is not here to manage a steady state. He is here to impose “Kirkland Lake discipline” on Kiena. He knows exactly what high-grade underground mining should cost.

He knows how to negotiate with contractors. He knows how to structure the balance sheet for M&A. His presence suggests that Wesdome is preparing for a major transition, either a rigorous turnaround of Kiena to prep for a sale, or an aggressive acquisition strategy using their cash pile.

Anthea Bath

Anthea Bath became CEO in July 2023. She is not a finance type; she is an engineer.

  • Background: She was the COO of Ero Copper, where she helped grow the company from a junior explorer to a CAD 2 billion international producer. Her background is in chemical engineering and “business optimization”.

  • Style: She describes herself by saying, “My heart pumps gold”. She is hands-on. Her experience at Ero Copper involved complex underground logistics and shaft sinking, exactly the technical challenges Kiena is facing.

  • The Mission: Bath was likely brought in to professionalize the operations after years of underinvestment. The transition to the “three horizon” plan at Kiena bears her signature, trying to build engineering redundancy into a fragile system.

The Verdict:

This isn’t a “maintain the status quo” management team. This is a “fix it or sell it” team. You have a CEO who specializes in technical turnarounds and a CFO who specializes in financial discipline and value crystallization. They are overqualified for a struggling mid-tier, which suggests they have big plans.

Val-D’Or and Labour War

You can have the best gold and the best management, but if you can’t get guys to go underground, you are dead. Wesdome is fighting a war in Val-d’Or, Quebec, and they are currently the underdog.

Agnico Eagle Major Issue

The Val-d’Or region is effectively a company town, and that company is Agnico Eagle. Agnico owns the Canadian Malartic (Canada’s largest gold mine), Goldex, and LaRonde mines, all within a short drive of Wesdome’s Kiena.

  • The Problem: Agnico is the “employer of choice.” They offer stability, massive benefits, and a “family” culture that they actively cultivate. They aim to hire 100% of their workforce locally.

  • The Impact on Wesdome: This is the root cause of the “contractor execution issues” at Kiena. The tier-1 miners, the best jumbo operators, the best scoop drivers, they all work for Agnico. Wesdome is left fighting for the scraps or relying on third-party contractors.

  • The Mercenary Risk: Contractors are mercenaries. In a tight labor market, the best contractor crews get sent to the biggest, most consistent clients (Agnico). The “B-teams” get sent to the smaller, more sporadic clients (Wesdome). This explains why Presqu’île underperformed. The contractors simply didn’t deliver the tons.

The Demographic Cliff

The broader context is even uglier for small producers.

  • Disinterest: 66% of youth aged 15 to 30 have absolutely no interest in working in the mining industry.

  • Shortage: The industry needs to recruit 191,000 workers in the next decade just to replace retirees and meet baseline demand. In an expansionary scenario, that number hits 256,000.

  • Wage Inflation: This scarcity drives up costs. Production costs per tonne at Kiena jumped to $506 in Q3 2025, up significantly from $426 the previous year. That isn’t just general inflation; that is “wage inflation”, paying more to get less efficient labor.

Until Wesdome can stabilize its own workforce and reduce reliance on contractors, Kiena’s costs will remain volatile. They are trying to compete with a whale in a swimming pool that is rapidly draining of water.

Macroeconomic Overview

Wesdome operates in a vacuum of its own operational challenges, but the external environment provides a massive tailwind that might just save them from themselves.

The Gold Price Super-Cycle

Gold has broken through the $3,800/oz threshold in late 2024 and is trading above $3,600/oz. Analysts are revising long-term assumptions upward, with some bullish forecasts hitting $4,500/oz for gold and $55/oz for silver by 2026/2027.

  • Leverage: Wesdome is a high-cost producer at Kiena but a low-cost producer at Eagle. The blended leverage is massive. A $100 increase in the gold price flows almost entirely to the bottom line because their fixed costs (hoist, mill, admin) are already paid.

  • The Hedge: Investors are flocking to gold as a hedge against economic uncertainty and currency debasement. Central banks (led by China) are buying. This creates a floor for the gold price that protects Wesdome’s margins even if Kiena’s costs spiral to $600/tonne.

Silver’s Opportunity

While Wesdome is a gold miner, the broader precious metals sentiment lifts all boats. The industrial demand for silver (solar, electronics) is surging, and the “silver squeeze” narrative helps maintain interest in the entire precious metals complex. Why does this matter for Wesdome? Because capital flows into the sector are indiscriminate. When generalist funds buy “gold exposure,” they buy the ETFs (GDX, GDXJ). Wesdome is a key component of these indices. A rising tide floats even the leaky boats.

Input Cost Moderation

There is a glimmer of hope on the cost side. Mining input cost inflation moderated to 3.4% year-over-year in March 2025, down from previous highs. While labor is expensive, other inputs like fuel and steel are stabilizing. This suggests that the worst of the “post-COVID inflation shock” might be over for the materials sector, provided the labor component can be managed.

History Lesson - Kiena’s History

To understand why the market is so skeptical of Kiena, you have to look at its history. This mine has broken hearts before.

  • 1981: First ore mined.

  • 1984-2003: Passed between Falconbridge, Campbell Red Lake, and McWatters Mines. It was a “trading card” asset, bought, sold, and eventually closed when prices got low.

  • 2003: Wesdome buys the Kiena complex.

  • 2013: Operations suspended. The mine was put on care and maintenance because it wasn’t profitable.

  • 2016: The Discovery. The Kiena Deep A Zone was found. This changed everything. It wasn’t just old remnants; it was a new, high-grade core.

  • 2021: Pre-Feasibility Study (PFS) for the restart.

  • 2024: First ore from Kiena Deep.

The Trauma:

Investors remember the 2013 shutdown. They remember the false starts. The “hoist failure” in 2025 triggers PTSD. It reinforces the narrative that Kiena is a “tired” asset with old bones (infrastructure from 1981) that will always struggle to deliver consistent ounces. Management’s job is to prove that the 2016 Deep Zone discovery is a “new mine” hiding inside an old shell, not just a scavenging operation.

Valuation & Recommendation - Asymmetric Bet

So, is it a buy?

The Bull Case:

  1. Valuation Dislocation: The stock is trading as if Kiena is a total write-off. But Eagle River alone, generating massive free cash flow, justifies a significant portion of the current market cap. The market is giving you the Kiena turnaround option for free.

  2. Cash Floor: CAD 266 million in cash acts as a massive buffer. It prevents dilution and funds the buyback.

  3. The “Yee” Put: Philip Yee won’t let this fail. He will cut costs, fire underperforming contractors, and optimize the balance sheet. If Kiena can’t be fixed, he will likely shut it down to preserve cash or sell the company.

  4. M&A Target: Wesdome is the perfect meal size for a senior producer. Agnico Eagle could buy them tomorrow to consolidate the Val-d’Or camp, shut down the labor competition, and take the high-grade Eagle River ore.

The Bear Case:

  1. Execution Risk: Kiena might be structurally broken. The ground conditions in the Deep Zone could be worse than the PFS suggested. The old shaft might have more mechanical gremlins waiting to strike.

  2. Labor Spiral: If Agnico continues to raise wages, Wesdome might never be able to staff Kiena profitably. Margins would erode until the mine is forced back into care and maintenance.

The Verdict:

Wesdome is a screaming buy for anyone with a stomach for volatility. The downside is capped by the fortress balance sheet and the Eagle River cash cow. The upside is a successful Kiena turnaround orchestrated by one of the best CFOs in the business.

Don’t buy it for the smooth ride. Buy it because the market is pricing it like a disaster, but the financials say it’s a gold mine.

Recommendation: BUY. Accumulate on Kiena-induced weakness. Watch the hoist. Trust the cash.
