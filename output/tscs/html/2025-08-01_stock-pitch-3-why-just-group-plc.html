The very strength of its investment case – significant undervaluation and an open shareholder structure – suggests that the ability to own it for the long term may be curtailed by the strong logic for a bid. The company currently trades at approximately half its “locked-in” book value, a stark contrast to its nearest peer, Legal & General, which trades around book value. Compounding this attractiveness to potential suitors, Just’s shareholder structure is notably open: there is no controlling shareholder or special voting rights that could be leveraged to successfully decline an offer for the business.

One might reasonably ask why Just trades so cheaply. It’s not due to any significant missteps or “own goals” by the company. The most plausible explanation is simply that with a market capitalisation that, until recently, hovered around £1 billion, it remained off the radar of many larger institutional investors. This, however, is beginning to change.

Just stands as the only listed pure-play UK retirement company. Its raison d’être is "helping people achieve a better life." It achieves this by providing guaranteed income for life, primarily through defined benefit (DB) pension plan de-risking (which constitutes 80% of its business) and personal annuities, known as Guaranteed Income for Life or GIFL (the remaining 20%).

The market Just serves is vast and expanding. The DB market, with over £1 trillion of remaining obligations, sees annual transactions in the realm of £50 billion, while the personal annuity market is a £7 billion per annum opportunity. On the DB side, companies are increasingly keen to offload pension scheme risks – which are not core to their operations – to specialists like Just. On the personal side, the recently introduced Consumer Duty regime mandates that financial advisors offer attractive and appropriate products, irrespective of their own incentives. And in the current environment, what could be more appealing than a guaranteed income for the rest of one’s life? Furthermore, the shift from DB to defined contribution (DC) schemes means a growing cohort of retirees are managing substantial pension pots themselves, increasing the demand for suitable retirement income solutions.

Just’s business model is straightforward: it profits from the spread between what it pays on its liabilities (the guaranteed lifetime payments) and what it earns on its assets (fixed income products matched in duration to those liabilities). This spread has remained broadly consistent over the years, leading to a “mid-teens or above” Internal Rate of Return (IRR) on new business written through the cycle.

Crucially for the investment thesis, this predictability extends not only to customers but also to shareholders. Unlike an investment management business where assets can be withdrawn rapidly – a phenomenon the UK active fund management industry has been experiencing – Just’s assets and liabilities are locked in until maturity. With an average duration currently at 12 years, they cannot simply leave. This "locked-in" nature is a cornerstone of its value. If Just were to cease writing new business today, the present value of the cash flows from the spread between its existing assets and liabilities, discounted at 6%, is estimated to be around twice its current market capitalisation. On top of this substantial embedded value, Just is actively writing profitable new business. In 2024, it wrote £5.3 billion of new business premium, a 36% increase over 2023, which translated into a 34% rise in underlying operating profit to £504 million. This profit figure stands in compelling comparison to its recent market capitalisation of around £1.4 billion to £1.7 billion.

The Investment Case: Why Just Group?

The current valuation prompts the question of why Just Group trades so cheaply. This isn't due to self-inflicted wounds but rather its previous status below the radar of many investors, a situation that is now evolving.

Strengths

  • Pure Play UK Retirement Specialist: Just is described as "the only listed pure play UK retirement company." This singular focus allows for deep specialization in a complex market.

  • Massive and Growing Market: The Defined Benefit (DB) de-risking market has over £1 trillion in remaining obligations, translating to an estimated £50 billion per annum opportunity. The personal annuity market adds another £7 billion annually. Growth is fueled by companies seeking to de-risk their pension schemes and the increasing number of retirees with substantial Defined Contribution (DC) pots to manage.

  • Predictable "Locked-In" Business Model: A crucial aspect is that "Just’s assets and liabilities are locked in until maturity... average duration is currently 12 years." This provides significant predictability for shareholders, unlike asset managers whose liabilities (AUM) can be withdrawn quickly. The embedded value is substantial: "If Just didn’t write another piece of business, the value of the cash flows from the spread between its assets and liabilities, discounted at 6%, is around twice its current market cap". This "locked-in" nature directly creates a margin of safety and underpins the embedded value that makes Just an attractive takeover target, especially given its open shareholder structure.

  • Strong Profitability & Growth: The company targets a "mid-teens or above” IRR on new business written through the cycle. In 2024, it wrote £5.3 billion of new business premium (a 36% increase over 2023), leading to a 34% increase in underlying operating profit to £504 million. The business aims for an ROE of over 15% on an underlying basis.

  • Excellent Credit Management: Historically, "actual defaults have averaged at less than 3bp per annum." The £18.7 billion bond portfolio is high quality, with 63% A to AAA rated and 37% BBB rated, and is well-diversified.

  • Highly Regarded Management & Strategy: The management team is held in high esteem, with a commitment to "continue growing the business organically only, with no intention of making any acquisitions" and a clear "UK focused" strategy. CEO David Richardson has extensive experience in the sector.

  • Conservative Capitalisation: The Solvency II ratio stands robustly over 200% (204% at FY 2024), indicating a strong capital base.

  • Open Shareholder Structure: As noted, with a float of 98.84%, there is no controlling entity, making a takeover bid more feasible.

Weaknesses (or Perceived Issues Leading to Mispricing)

  • Historically Off the Radar: Its previous smaller size meant it was "off the radar of a lot of investors," contributing to its valuation discount. This is more an explanation for mispricing than an intrinsic business flaw.

  • Complexity of Financials: Insurance accounting, particularly under IFRS 17, can be opaque to generalist investors. The notable difference between IFRS ROE and the underlying ROE highlighted by the company underscores this potential for confusion.

  • Past Regulatory Impacts: Historic changes, such as the 2014 "pension freedoms" affecting the GIFL market and 2018 changes to capital requirements for lifetime mortgages, demonstrate regulatory vulnerability. However, current regulations suggest changes would likely only apply to future business, not the existing locked-in profits.

Opportunities

  • Continued DB De-risking Trend: Companies remain keen to offload pension scheme risks from their balance sheets.

  • Growth in DC Market: An increasing number of retirees will have substantial DC pension pots requiring management solutions like those Just offers.

  • Consumer Duty Regime: Recently introduced regulations require advisors to offer "attractive and appropriate products," which should benefit guaranteed income solutions.

  • Takeover Target: The "strong logic for a bid" remains a primary opportunity for shareholders to realize value. The substantial, secure, and undervalued asset base represented by the locked-in book is highly attractive to potential acquirers.

Threats

  • 1) Longevity Risk: Customers living longer than expected is a core risk for annuity providers. Just mitigates this through reinsurance, with 70% of its back book and 90% of new business reinsured. This coverage is expected to rise to the 90% level over time. Reinsurance typically costs around 5% of the premium received.

  • 2) Interest Rate Declines: Lower real interest rates could make annuities less appealing for future business and increase the cost of funding liabilities. This risk applies mainly to future business flows, potentially slowing growth rather than halting it.

  • 3) Credit Defaults: Default by corporate or government bond issuers is "clearly the main risk for their existing book." This is mitigated by a high-quality, well-diversified £18.7 billion bond portfolio and an excellent historical default experience of less than 3bp per annum.

  • 4) Regulatory Changes: The Prudential Regulatory Authority (PRA) wields significant power. "This is a very real risk." However, any changes, for instance to the DB market, are anticipated to apply only to future business, not the existing locked-in profits.

  • 5) Inflation: Many DB liabilities and some GIFL products have inflation linkage. This is addressed by investing in inflation-linked assets and using inflation swaps from a deep and liquid market.

  • Competition: The market is attractive, drawing in new entrants like Royal London, M&G, Utmost, and Brookfield, while some, like Scottish Widows (in DB), have exited. This has led to some "tightening of spreads." However, there appears to be "enough profitable business for those that maintain pricing discipline." Just’s specialization in smaller DB deals and specific GIFL segments provides a degree of insulation.

The interplay of these risks and their mitigants is important. For example, reinsurance for longevity risk, while having a cost, secures that liability, allowing management to focus on optimizing the asset spread to manage credit and interest rate risk. The "locked-in" nature of the existing business also provides a substantial buffer against regulatory or interest rate changes that might primarily affect new business generation. This layered approach to risk management is indicative of a mature and well-managed specialist.

Industry Overview

Sector Description

Just Group operates within the UK retirement income market. This sector provides financial solutions for individuals approaching or in retirement, and for companies managing defined benefit pension obligations. It is a mature but evolving market, heavily regulated by the Prudential Regulatory Authority (PRA) and the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA).

Market Structure

The market features several large, diversified insurers (such as Legal & General and Aviva) and specialist players (like Just Group and Rothesay Life). While there are elements of an oligopoly, particularly in the very large DB scheme segment, there is also healthy competition and space for specialists who can differentiate on service, underwriting expertise, or focus on specific market niches. The entry of new players like Royal London and M&G into certain segments indicates the market's perceived attractiveness, though it also intensifies competition.

Key Trends

  • DB Scheme De-risking: This is a long-term structural trend. Companies are increasingly motivated to transfer their pension liabilities to specialist insurers to reduce financial risk, volatility on their balance sheets, and administrative burden. Companies don’t want to take the risk of their pension scheme requiring extra funding – it is not their core business – so increasingly are only too happy for specialists to handle them.

  • Growth of Defined Contribution (DC) Pots: Due to auto-enrollment and the widespread closure of DB schemes to new members, more individuals are retiring with substantial DC pension pots. This creates a growing need for retirement income solutions, as "more and more retirees have substantial pension pots to manage themselves".

  • Impact of Interest Rates: Higher interest rates generally make annuities more attractive as they allow providers to offer higher guaranteed incomes. They also improve the funding levels of DB schemes, potentially accelerating their de-risking journey. Conversely, a significant decline in rates could make annuities less appealing for new business.

  • Regulatory Influence: Regulation is a constant feature. Solvency II dictates capital requirements. The new Consumer Duty regime, which "means that advisors, quite rightly, must offer attractive and appropriate products for their customers," is expected to be favorable for transparent and suitable products like guaranteed lifetime income solutions.

  • Longevity Improvements: Advances in healthcare and lifestyles mean people are generally living longer. While this is a positive societal trend, it increases liabilities for annuity providers. This is managed through careful underwriting (especially for enhanced annuities) and reinsurance. The confluence of these strong tailwinds – DB de-risking, DC pot growth, and supportive regulation like Consumer Duty – creates a uniquely favorable and sustained demand environment for Just Group's core offerings. These are not isolated trends but interconnected drivers.

Market Size & Growth

The market is substantial and growing. The DB de-risking market alone has "over £1trn of remaining obligations" with an estimated annual flow of "c. £50bn per annum." The personal annuity market is around "£7bn per annum". This "huge and growing market" provides a significant runway for specialist providers. While the industry has large players, the sheer scale of the DB de-risking market and the complexity of individual needs mean there's ample room for specialists like Just Group. They can offer tailored solutions in segments that larger, more standardized players might overlook or find less economical, positioning them in a "sweet spot."

Valuation, Reverse Thesis, and Conclusion

Valuation & Margin of Safety

The core of the valuation argument rests on the significant discount at which Just Group trades relative to its intrinsic, or "locked-in," value.

  • Discount to "Locked-In" Value / TNAV: The company trades at half its 'locked in' book value. More specifically, its Tangible Net Asset Value (TNAV) was 254p per share at the end of FY 2024. With a share price around 148p, this represents a price-to-TNAV ratio of approximately 0.58x. This TNAV is an economic valuation of the in-force portfolio plus surplus capital, which the market appears to be heavily discounting. It is crucial to distinguish this from the IFRS book value per share (around 119.9p at YE 2024, based on £1,246m equity and 1,038.7m shares, to which the current share price is at a premium (P/IFRS Book Value of ~1.23x). The investment thesis centers on this economic, embedded value.

  • Inherent Value of In-Force Book: "If Just didn’t write another piece of business, the value of the cash flows from the spread between its assets and liabilities, discounted at 6%, is around twice its current market cap". This provides a substantial margin of safety.

  • Peer Comparison: Its nearest peer, Legal & General, trades at around book value. While direct comparison is complex due to different business mixes and scale, this highlights the potential for re-rating if Just were valued similarly on its TNAV.

  • Upside Potential: "Trading at NAV, like its closest peer... would mean a doubling of the share price".

  • Growing NAV: Importantly, "in the meantime, the actual NAV will continue to grow strongly". The TNAV increased by 30p per share during 2024 to reach 254p.

  • Analyst Price Targets: According to analyst consensus, Just Group's stock has a maximum estimate of 219.00p and a minimum estimate of 165.00p. With the current price around 148p, this suggests a potential upside of approximately 11% to 48% to meet these targets.

Reverse Thesis (What could derail the positive outlook?)

While the investment case is compelling, several factors could challenge the positive outlook:

  • Persistent Undervaluation (Value Trap Scenario): The discount to TNAV could persist or even widen if a catalyst, such as a takeover bid, fails to materialize and the broader market continues to overlook its intrinsic value. The company could become a "value trap" if the market fails to gain confidence in the long-term sustainability of profits and NAV growth, or if underlying assumptions about the in-force book prove too optimistic.

  • Risks Crystallize More Severely: A major credit event or a series of defaults in its bond portfolio significantly exceeding the historical average of less than 3bp per annum. An unexpectedly sharp and sustained fall in long-term interest rates, making new annuity business far less profitable or even unviable, and potentially impacting the valuation of the in-force book. A harsh, unforeseen regulatory change that negatively impacts the economics of the existing locked-in book of business (though this is considered less likely for in-force policies). Significant hardening of the reinsurance market, substantially increasing the cost of longevity reinsurance and eroding the profitability of new business.

  • Competition Intensifies Dramatically and Irrationally: New or existing competitors could engage in aggressive, value-destructive pricing to gain market share, driving down margins on new business to unsustainable levels for all participants.

  • Management Missteps or Departure: Any deviation from the current successful strategy of organic, UK-focused growth (e.g., a large, poorly judged acquisition – though management has stated no intention of this). Key person risk associated with the highly-rated current management team is also a consideration.

Conclusion

Just Group presents a compelling investment case, primarily rooted in its significant undervaluation relative to its robust and growing Tangible Net Asset Value. The company operates as a focused specialist in the large and structurally growing UK retirement income market, benefiting from strong demographic and de-risking tailwinds. Its "locked-in" business model provides a high degree of earnings visibility and a substantial margin of safety.

The management team is highly regarded, pursuing a disciplined strategy of organic growth within the UK, and has demonstrated an ability to generate attractive returns on equity on an underlying basis, alongside maintaining a strong capital position. As stated in the initial assessment, "It is only a matter of time until the 50% discount to its NAV closes substantially. And, in the meantime, the actual NAV will continue to grow strongly."

The combination of a deeply discounted valuation, strong underlying profitability, a growing NAV, and an open shareholder structure makes Just Group a logical takeover target. While the market is beginning to recognize its qualities, the full value may ultimately be realized through a bid from a larger entity seeking to acquire its valuable in-force book and market position. Analysts note that it is just the start of the Just story, though sadly, as we said at the start, its believed it is unlikely to be able to enjoy it for many years to come, implying that an acquisition may pre-empt a longer-term holding period. The investment thesis is thus supported by both fundamental value and a strong M&A rationale.
