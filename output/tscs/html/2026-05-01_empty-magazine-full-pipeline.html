The SM-3 sustainment trade

The MDA contract modification on March 18, 2026 lifts the SM-3 sustainment IDIQ ceiling from $3.33 billion to $11.74 billion through October 29, 2029. The ceiling went from $722 million in 2020 to $11.74 billion now, a sixteen-fold expansion in five years on a single IDIQ.

Structurally different from framework production. The work runs against the existing program of record, not new capacity. No Williams International, no Mk 72, no second-source qualification gating recognition. Sustainment task orders definitize on 60 to 180 day timelines versus the 9 to 18 month base rate for new production UCAs. Sustainment runs higher-margin than new-build. Recurring engineering, software updates, kit upgrades, and field service run 200 to 400 bps above new-build margin, per comparable LMT and NOC structures.

The math on EPS contribution. Sustainment IDIQs typically run at 70 to 90% of ceiling over their performance period, so the $8.41 billion lift translates to roughly $5.9 to $7.6 billion of incremental sustainment revenue across the four-year window, or $1.5 to $1.9 billion per year. Apply Raytheon segment operating margin in the 12 to 13% range for sustainment-weighted work, RTX’s effective tax rate of approximately 18%, and a share count of roughly 1.33 billion. Annualised SM-3 sustainment: $0.11 to $0.15 of EPS per year. Cumulative FY26 to FY29: $0.45 to $0.60 of EPS not visible as a discrete line in consensus.

Directional, not precise. But $0.11 to $0.15 against a $7.57 FY27 consensus base is large enough to matter. Task orders against the lifted ceiling will show in 10-Q backlog disclosures within two quarters of March 18, so the Q3 2026 print in October. The first quarter where SM-3 sustainment is visible above noise is Q1 or Q2 2027 reporting. That is the near-term print catalyst, well ahead of the 2028 framework recognition, and it tightens the floor on the dead-money case.
