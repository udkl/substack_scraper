We have to forget Hendry’s spark for a moment and remember, amidst the market noise, what this humble stock actually is.

Nokia Oyj (HEL:NOKIA), is not some exotic AI play mined for its own sake. It is, in the market’s mind, almost as an afterthought.

Think of it as the less glamorous sibling found alongside the Nvidia-led AI boom. Its primary business is selling Radio Access Network (RAN) hardware and IP routers to slow-moving, debt-laden telecom operators, a business that often drives the un-glamorous economics of the TMT sector. Mother Nature, in her infinite randomness, deposits these businesses in the deep “value” category, far from the sunny coastlines of “growth.”

Once extracted, this hardware isn’t typically used for generative AI. It’s installed in cell towers or dusty aggregation sites. The fate of most Nokia revenue, primarily, is about making 5G calls connect and enabling mobile video streaming. It’s about making sure your Netflix stream doesn’t buffer. Keep that perspective.

This is where the simple commodity story ends and the investment thesis begins.

The telecom hardware market is notoriously opaque, cyclical, and dominated by a few key players (Nokia, Ericsson, Huawei). Comprehensive, institutional-grade deep dives on a “Nokia as an AI play” thesis are virtually non-existent.

The new hard facts

This isn’t a theory. A series of hard, fast-moving events in 2025 has forced a re-evaluation of the “humble sand” thesis.

The Nvidia Shot: On October 28, 2025, Nvidia announced a $1.0B investment in Nokia, buying ~166.4M new shares at $6.01 per share. This is not just a portfolio allocation; it’s a 2.9% stake paired with a “strategic partnership to co-build an AI-RAN platform” and accelerate Nokia’s software on Nvidia’s stack.

The Narrative Shift: The joint release is explicitly geopolitical, calling out “powering America’s return to telecommunications leadership”. This is a U.S.-backed coalition.

The Partners: This isn’t vaporware. T-Mobile US is the named operator for collaboration, with field trials beginning in 2026. Dell is the named server partner, providing the PowerEdge compute platform.

The Tech: Nvidia is introducing ARC-Pro, a 6G-ready “Aerial RAN Computer” reference platform. This is an AI-native RAN built on Nvidia’s CUDA and Blackwell-era GPUs, with an Aerial SDK for GPU-accelerated PHY (the complex radio processing layer) and a digital twin toolchain.

The Reshaping (Pre-Deal Context): This did not happen in a vacuum.

Infinera: Nokia completed its acquisition of Infinera on Feb 28, 2025. This was a major move to build scale in optical networks, specifically targeting AI-era data center optics and webscale customers, with a target of over EUR 200M of operating synergies by 2027.

ASN Sale: Nokia completed the sale of its submarine cable unit (ASN) to the French state (at a EUR 350M enterprise value), with the deal closing in early 2025. This was a deliberate refocusing, trimming a lower-margin, project-heavy business to concentrate on the high-growth IP, Optical, and Fixed networks.

The Backdrop (Q3 2025 Print): The Q3 earnings, released just days before the deal (Oct 23, 2025), set the stage. Net sales rose, and profits beat. The growth was already coming from “optical and AI-cloud demand”. The new CEO, Justin Hotard, who joined in April 2025 from Intel’s Data Center & AI Group, noted that AI & Cloud customers already accounted for 14% of Network Infrastructure’s sales.

The Price Context: The market is pricing this pivot. Nokia’s ADR closed around $6.91 on Oct 31, 2025. This was after spiking to $7.77 on Oct 28 when the Nvidia news hit. A week earlier (Oct 22) it was $5.55, and it was in the $4.80 range back in February.

The Nvidia deal is not the start of a new strategy. It is the capstone of a 9-month strategic pivot executed by a new, specialist CEO. Hotard is pivoting Nokia from a telco-first vendor to a telco and webscale vendor. The AI-RAN deal is the vehicle.

What exactly is AI-RAN?

The market is throwing this term around. Let’s define it, as we would to any other buzzword. It’s not one thing; it’s a three-layer stack.

AI-for-RAN: Using Machine Learning to optimize the existing radio network (e.g., better beamforming, radio scheduling, or power-saving). This is “smarter 5G.”

AI-on-RAN: Using the compute hardware at the cell site (under the tower or at an aggregation point) to run other edge AI applications (e.g., an inference workload for a local factory, smart city, or retail analytics). This creates a new revenue stream for telcos.

AI-native RAN: This is the real thesis. This is the convergence. The same hardware (Nvidia GPUs on a Dell server) handles both the radio baseband processing (the core telco job) and the edge AI services. This is what Nvidia’s ARC platform and Aerial SDK are built for.

The pitch from Nokia and Nvidia is that this provides a software-defined path from 5G-Advanced to 6G, without a “forklift swap” of hardware. This is highly appealing to capex-shy telcos. Omdia pegs this as a cumulative >$200B opportunity within the broader RAN market by 2030. This is the “gold rush” number.

Timeline Reality Check: Let’s ground this. 6G is not tomorrow. 3GPP (the standards body) is still working on 5G-Advanced (Release 18, 19). 6G work (Rel 20/21) is just starting. Commercial 6G is a 2030 story.

Nokia’s CEO was specific on the analyst call: trials are in 2026, and commercial production is by “late ‘27”. That means the 2026–2029 monetization cycle is not 6G. It’s 5G-Advanced. The T-Mobile trials will be focused on proving Layer 1 (opex/energy savings) and enabling Layer 2 (new services). The “6G” label is future-proofing, but the money is in 5G-Advanced.

Does 6G make a 1 hour 4K file transfer “instant”?

Let’s test the marketing claims. “Instant” is a marketing word. We use physics.

A one-hour 4K stream (encoded at 15-20 Mbps) is roughly 6.7–9 GB. “Instant” (one second) would require a ~60-70 Gbps sustained user-experienced rate.

The binding constraint is not the peak theoretical rate of 6G; it’s the realistic, loaded, user-experienced rate.

Will carrier capex reach 2.5% of world GDP?

This is a “wording matters” check, just as a legal expert would perform.

The Omdia forecast (Oct 29, 2025) being passed around is being misquoted.

It does not say 2.5% of gross output (GDP).

It says 2.5% of global GDP investment. This is a much smaller, different denominator.

Still, the underlying data is bullish. Omdia forecasts communications provider (CP) capex per person rising from $74 (2024) to $116 (2030). It does signal a new upcycle, with a specific mobile-centric shift starting in 2028 for 6G prep.

Verdict: Partly-true, needs precise wording. It signals an upcycle, which is the investable part, but the headline number is misleading.

What Nokia actually owns now

Let’s look at the mundane assets to understand the real value.

Mobile Networks: The “boring” part. AirScale RAN portfolio (the radios), ReefShark SoCs (their custom silicon), and the Cloud/anyRAN software. This is the division that will now integrate the Nvidia stack, creating a high-margin, software-defined alternative to its own hardware.

Network Infrastructure (The Crown Jewel):

IP Networks: Custom FP5 routing silicon and 7750 SR platforms. This is high-end, high-margin gear that competes with Juniper/Arista. Q3 saw strong order intake.

Optical Networks: Now combined with Infinera. This is a beast. It has Nokia’s PSE-6s coherent engines (for 800G) and Infinera’s webscale-focused portfolio. This is the division that was already growing 19% Y/Y from AI & Cloud demand before the Nvidia deal.

Fixed Networks: XGS-PON, 25G PON. Solid, steady broadband access.

Data Center Switching: This is the hidden asset. The 7220 IXR and SR Linux (a modern, open Network OS). This is a direct competitor to Arista/Cisco in the data center, and it is validated for Ultra Ethernet and poised to integrate with Nvidia Spectrum-X.

Cloud and Network Services: 5G Core, automation, security, and private wireless. This is the “brain” of the network, and it grew 13% in Q3, gaining share in Voice Core.

Nokia Bell Labs: The U.S.-based research powerhouse. 10 Nobel Prizes. This is where the 6G research and intellectual property are born.

Nokia Technologies (The Cash Engine): ~20,000 patent families, with 7,000 declared essential to 5G. Apple, Samsung, and others are licensees. This is a high-margin, recurring-revenue cash-flow engine with an annual run-rate of ~EUR 1.4 billion.

The AI-RAN story (Mobile Networks) is getting all the press, but the Network Infrastructure (Optical + IP + DC Switching) division is already executing a massive pivot to data centers. The Q3 results prove it. The AI-RAN deal may be a “Trojan Horse,” using Nvidia’s brand to accelerate Nokia’s push into the data center.

Nokia’s near-term expansion plan, as disclosed

This is management’s stated plan, straight from the filings and calls.

AI-RAN Path: Commercialization with Nvidia hardware and Nokia’s anyRAN software, with the T-Mobile US 2026 field trials as the key proof point. CEO Hotard set a target of “late ‘27” for commercial production.

Data Center Push: Drive SR Linux adoption on Nvidia Spectrum-X and win Ultra Ethernet validation.

Optical Scale: Integrate Infinera and hit the EUR 200M operating synergy target by 2027.

Private Wireless: Continue to lead the market (ex-China) for enterprise edge deployments.

The Capital Markets Day on Nov 19, 2025. This is where Hotard must quantify the Nvidia partnership, detail the data center strategy, and provide 2026-2028 targets.

Competitive and geopolitical context

The “humble sand” of the RAN market is an oligopoly: Huawei (banned in US), Ericsson, Nokia, ZTE (banned), Samsung. In North America, it’s a duopoly: Ericsson and Nokia.

The “Bringing telecom leadership back to the U.S.” quote is the key. Nokia is Finnish, Ericsson is Swedish.

This “U.S. leadership” narrative doesn’t mean a U.S. company will suddenly build radios. It means the value capture is shifting. It means the compute (Nvidia, a U.S. company), the servers (Dell, a U.S. company), and the research (Nokia Bell Labs, a U.S. entity) will capture the margin from the RAN software (Nokia).

This is a U.S. silicon and software play, not a U.S. radio play. It’s a fundamental shift in the value chain, moving margin from proprietary foreign-designed ASICs to U.S.-designed GPUs.

Valuation sanity check and scenarios

This is the core financial analysis.

The Math:

Nokia’s shares outstanding (from the Oct 31, 2025 data) were 5,426,458,407.

Nvidia is buying ~166.4M new shares.

The post-issuance share count will be ~5.742 Billion shares (using the framework’s full float assumption).

A $300 share price implies a $1.72 Trillion market cap.

From a ~$7 price today, that requires a ~45.6% CAGR for 10 years.

For a mature, cyclical, capital-intensive hardware vendor to achieve this, it would require a complete transformation into a high-margin software/licensing company, extraordinary market share gains in data centers, and a “Hail Mary” scenario.

Plausible Drivers for Multiple Expansion:

Real, high-margin AI-RAN software revenues ramping from 2027-2032.

The patent engine (Nokia Technologies) sustains its high-margin licensing with 6G and IoT.

The Infinera-boosted Optical division and the SR Linux data center switch business take significant, high-margin share from Arista and Cisco.

Data center switching share gains if SR Linux plus Spectrum-X lands broad adoption.

The $300 number is a “narrative device,” not a forecast. Its purpose is to make you check the math. In checking, you are forced to evaluate the plausible bull cases (e.g., $30-$60) which, from a $7 start, are still exceptional.

The bull thesis in one paragraph

Nokia, under a new data-center-native CEO , has repositioned itself across the entire AI-era network. It has high-margin IP routing (FP5), a newly acquired webscale-focused Optical division (Infinera) that is already growing 19% from AI & Cloud demand, its own data center switching products (SR Linux), and a durable patent cash engine. Now, the $1B Nvidia partnership and T-Mobile trials give it a funded, credible, and U.S.-backed path to owning the next major network-edge TAM: AI-RAN. If AI shifts from centralized to distributed, Nokia owns the programmable edge.

The bear case in one paragraph

AI-RAN is a science project. It has to beat purpose-built, highly-optimized ASICs on cost-per-bit and energy-per-bit at scale, not just in a lab. Operators are broke and will not pay a “GPU premium” for a “software-defined” network if the TCO doesn’t work. Ericsson (in the US) and Huawei (globally) will defend their turf. The 6G revenue is a 2030+ story. By then, the AI-RAN architecture might be commoditized. The real growth remains in Optical/IP , and the AI-RAN story is just a temporary narrative to get the stock multiple to expand.

What to watch next

Nov 19, 2025 Capital Markets Day : This is the big one. Hotard must provide hard revenue bridges, margin targets for AI-RAN and data center, and pilots beyond T-Mobile US.

Early 2026: T-Mobile Trial Results : We need hard data on spectral efficiency, energy savings (opex), and TCO deltas.

Webscale Logos: Look for new logos in Optical (post-Infinera) and Data Center Switching (SR Linux on Spectrum-X).

Bottom line

The Nvidia–Nokia deal is not random. It is the capstone of a 9-month pivot by a new, data-center-focused CEO to remake Nokia.

It is a real, U.S.-backed attempt to shift the RAN value chain from proprietary foreign hardware to U.S. compute (Nvidia, Dell) and U.S.-based R&D (Bell Labs).

The “AI-RAN” revenue is a 2027-2032 story. The immediate growth story is Nokia’s (Infinera-powered) Optical and IP divisions selling into the data center AI gold rush today.

The $300 call is a narrative device. The investable thesis is that Nokia is on a path to becoming a higher-growth, higher-margin, software-like networks company, and its current ~$7 valuation does not reflect this pivot.
