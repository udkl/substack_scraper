Business Description

Ivanhoe Mines Ltd. (TSX: IVN), incorporated in 1993 and renamed from Ivanplats Limited in 2013, is a Canadian mining company focused on the exploration, development, and production of metals critical to the energy transition. The company’s strategy centers on discovering and developing Tier-1 mineral deposits, characterized by high grades, large scale, and long mine lives.

Ivanhoe’s operations are geographically concentrated in Southern and Central Africa, specifically South Africa and the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC). The company operates through three principal segments:

  1. Kamoa Holding Joint Venture (DRC): Focused on the exploration and production of high-grade copper at the Kamoa-Kakula complex. This is the company’s flagship asset.

  2. Platreef Properties (South Africa): Developing a large-scale, mechanized mine focused on Platinum Group Metals (PGMs), nickel, copper, and gold.

  3. Kipushi Properties (DRC): Redeveloping the historic Kipushi mine, focusing on ultra-high-grade zinc production.

The company sells its commodities (copper concentrate, zinc concentrate, and soon PGM concentrate) to industrial manufacturers, utilities, and the automotive sector via long-term contracts and spot market engagements. The commissioning of an onsite smelter at Kamoa-Kakula is transforming its copper output into high-purity blister copper, altering its distribution and customer profile.

While Q3 2025 marked the operational trough and liquidity is secure (bolstered by a $500M QIA investment), the Q1 2026 revised Life of Mine (LOM) plan is the defining catalyst.

Operational Overview

Asset Portfolio Snapshot (as of Q3 2025)

Kamoa-Kakula (KK) – The Geotechnical Crisis

Root Cause: The May 2025 seismic event resulted from flawed geotechnical assumptions. As per the June 12 Special Call (COO Farren), the backfill failed to take stress redistribution, overloading support pillars and causing failure/flooding. This invalidates the previous ~90% extraction ratio assumption.

Q3 Impact (The Trough): Operations relied on stockpiles, causing grade starvation (2.47% Cu vs. 4.10% Q1) and cost inflation (C1 $2.62/lb). Analysis of the C1 breakdown (Financial Supplement, Table 2) confirms ‘Mining’ costs ($1.22/lb) drove the increase due to high-cost ‘development tonnes’.

Recovery & Risk: Dewatering is progressing; access to higher-grade Western stopes expected Nov 2025. The key risk is the Stockpile Transition Risk: stockpiles deplete by Q1 2026; any delay in underground ramp-up will cause a sharp drop in utilization.

Smelter Catalyst: The 500ktpa smelter (Dec 2025) is vital for margin protection, unlocking 59kt inventory and capturing high-value acid credits (>$500/t).

Platreef – Timely Commissioning

First ore fed Oct 29, 2025, coinciding with a strong PGM rally (Pt +78% YTD). Structurally superior (mechanized, thick deposit) to peers. Phase 2 target C1 cost ~$600/oz (net of byproducts), placing it at the bottom of the global cost curve.

Kipushi – Flawless Execution

Debottlenecking complete. Record Q3 production at 37.8% Zn. Targeting 250-300ktpa from 2026 (Top-3 global zinc mine).

Geographies: Highly concentrated in Central/Southern Africa. ~90% of NAV derived from DRC and South Africa.

Segments: Revenue currently dominated by Kamoa-Kakula (copper) and Kipushi (zinc). Platreef (PGMs/Base Metals) will begin contributing in Q4 2025.

Q3 2025 Contribution (Trough Quarter): Kamoa-Kakula (IVN Share of EBITDA): $76.3M; Kipushi (EBITDA): $27M.

Profitability Illusion - Revenue, Margins, & Accounting

Revenue Trends:

Overall revenue growth has been driven by the Kamoa-Kakula ramp-up, until the wheels came off.

Q3 2025 saw a sharp reversal due to the seismic event. Kamoa-Kakula revenue fell to $566M (vs. $973M in Q1 2025).

The Q3 2025 decline was due to lower volumes (61.5kt Cu sold vs 110kt in Q1) and lower grades processed (2.47% vs 4.10%). Realized copper prices were supportive ($4.42/lb in Q3).

Kipushi provided a crucial offset in Q3, delivering record revenue of $129M (Zinc).

Margins and ROCE Analysis:

Do the math on the cost structure. The “Mining” component exploded.

  • Mining: 46.6% ($1.22/lb) - Abnormally high due to recovery efforts/development tonnes.

  • Processing: 19.1% ($0.50/lb)

  • Logistics/TC/RC: 22.5% ($0.59/lb)

  • G&A: 11.8% ($0.31/lb)

Kamoa-Kakula EBITDA margin compressed significantly to 35% in Q3 2025, down from 61% in Q1 2025.

Margins are expected to recover strongly from Q1 2026 as grades improve (access to Western side re-established Nov 2025) and the smelter ramps up, reducing logistics costs and adding significant acid byproduct credits.

Traditional group ROCE is difficult to calculate meaningfully due to the JV accounting structure and the high level of capital currently being deployed for growth (>$6B invested at KK). The underlying asset quality suggests the potential for sector-leading ROCE once operations stabilize, contingent on the revised LOM plan.

Verdict - Valuation & Conclusion

Valuation & Margin of Safety:

Valuation is best approached via a Sum-of-the-Parts (SOTP) Net Asset Value (NAV) analysis.

IVN typically trades at a premium to NAV (~2.1x P/BV per S&P CIQ peer sheet).

Analysis of peer data shows IVN trades at a massive premium on TEV/Revenue (NTM) (15.60x vs 3.57x median), reflecting the exceptionally high margins expected from its assets.

On TEV/EBITDA (NTM), IVN appears cheaper (12.85x vs 20.41x median), likely reflecting depressed NTM EBITDA estimates post-seismic event. The valuation remains contingent on the successful restoration of the Kamoa-Kakula production profile.

The valuation is highly sensitive to the outcome of the Kamoa-Kakula recovery. The critical variables are the long-term extraction ratio and sustainable mining rate defined in the Q1 2026 LOM plan.

The margin of safety rests on the diversification (Platreef and Kipushi) and the inherent high-grade nature of Kamoa-Kakula. The current uncertainty surrounding the Kamoa recovery likely compresses the typical premium, offering a potential entry point if the revised LOM plan exceeds cautious expectations.
