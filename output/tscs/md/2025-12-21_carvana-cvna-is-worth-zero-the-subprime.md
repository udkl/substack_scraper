## The Legal Aspect

**The Legal Risks That Could Sink Carvana**

Carvana’s recovery story looks compelling on paper – until you examine what’s happening in courtrooms and state regulatory offices across America.

The legal exposure here isn’t peripheral noise. It targets both the operational model and the financial engineering simultaneously.

**When Your Business Model Becomes Your Legal Problem**

Carvana sells more than used cars online. They sell financing attached to those cars. Then they sell those loans to someone else. Repeat.

That last bit – the loan sales – generated $755 million in gains during 2024. For context, that’s a material chunk of how the “recovery” narrative works.

But when short seller Hindenburg Research published their report in January 2025, they asked a simple question: who exactly is buying nearly $800 million worth of these loans?

The allegation? An undisclosed related party.

If Hindenburg’s related-party allegations are proven in discovery or by regulators, this isn’t creative accounting. It’s the accounting equivalent of selling your watch to yourself and booking the profit.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Z0p3!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fa9c82069-986c-47e3-aae4-e95f32531185_1600x700.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Z0p3!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fa9c82069-986c-47e3-aae4-e95f32531185_1600x700.png)

**The Legal Threat Map**

Carvana faces three distinct categories of legal exposure, each with different timelines and damage potential.

**State Regulatory Actions: Death by a Thousand Licences**

Connecticut Attorney General William Tong announced a $1.5 million settlement with Carvana in January 2025 – $1 million for consumer restitution, $500,000 in penalties.

The violations? Delays in title registration, dodgy temporary licence plates, misrepresenting vehicle condition.

Connecticut isn’t unique. Illinois revoked Carvana’s dealer licence twice in 2022. Michigan suspended operations over “imminent harm” to consumers in 2022. California extracted settlements in prior years.

Here’s why this matters: state regulators can simply stop you selling cars in their jurisdiction. Revenue goes to zero in that state until you fix the mess.

For a national online model, getting locked out of major states isn’t a compliance headache. It’s a business model crisis.

The securities class action complaint alleges Carvana faced “imminent and ongoing regulatory actions including license suspensions, business cessation, and probation in several states and counties including in Arizona, Illinois, Pennsylvania, Michigan, and North Carolina” – but failed to disclose the severity of these problems to investors.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!WZcs!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fced58c67-915c-4557-8508-d8c2370e58e6_1600x864.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!WZcs!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fced58c67-915c-4557-8508-d8c2370e58e6_1600x864.png)

**Securities Litigation: Discovery is Where Reputations Die**

Here’s where things get serious.

Robbins Geller Rudman & Dowd – one of the world’s leading securities litigation firms – has survived not one but two dismissal attempts. In February 2025, they defeated Carvana’s motion for reconsideration after already defeating the initial motion to dismiss in 2024.

The case is now in discovery.

The lead plaintiffs are the United Association National Pension Fund and Saskatchewan Healthcare Employees’ Pension Plan. These aren’t retail investors; they’re sophisticated institutional shareholders representing a class of investors who bought between May 2020 and June 2022.

The underwriters – Citigroup and J.P. Morgan Securities – are also defendants.

Discovery – that’s legal jargon for “we get to subpoena your internal documents and put executives under oath asking uncomfortable questions about emails they wish they’d never written.”

The complaint alleges Carvana misled investors about:

* Serious, ongoing issues with documentation, registration, and title
* Unusually frequent issuance of temporary plates
* Violations of laws and regulations in existing markets
* Discussions with state and local authorities about these violations
* The risk to ability to continue or expand business

If Carvana did sell $800 million in loans to a Garcia family affiliate while booking the gains as arm’s length transactions, discovery will likely find the paperwork.

Lead counsel Daniel Drosman told the press: “We’re pleased with the court’s rulings, and we’re eager to advance the class’s claims in discovery and ultimately towards a trial on the merits.”

That’s lawyer-speak for “we’ve won the preliminary rounds and now we get to see the documents.”

Securities settlements in the US routinely hit nine figures for companies this size. Even a “modest” $200-300 million settlement would consume a meaningful portion of Carvana’s $1.7 billion cash balance.

**Consumer Class Actions: Volume Creates Exposure**

Carvana sold hundreds of thousands of vehicles. If even a small percentage had title problems, the aggregate exposure gets interesting.

A $1,000 average resolution multiplied by 50,000 affected customers is $50 million. Scale that to 150,000 customers across multiple state actions and you’re looking at material cash outflows.

Unlike securities litigation, consumer claims also generate ongoing reputational damage. Every news story about customers unable to register their Carvana purchase is free negative advertising.

The proliferation of consumer complaints has spawned an entire cottage industry of personal injury lawyers advertising their services to disgruntled Carvana customers – a visible symptom of systemic operational problems:

**The Forensic Accounting Question**

Strip away the legal complexity and you’re left with a simple question: did Carvana sell loans to itself?

Hindenburg alleges that Carvana generated $145 million in “other revenue” from related parties in 2023, representing 8.4% of total gross profit. This included $138 million in commissions on extended warranties administered through DriveTime – the car dealership run by Ernest Garcia II.

If the company sold receivables to an entity controlled by the Garcia family at prices that maximised reported gains, those loan sale gains would represent transfers between related parties rather than genuine profit realisation.

The numbers are concerning: gain on loan sales represented 2.2 times Carvana’s net income in the nine months through Q3 2024.

Investors should demand answers:

* Who purchased the loans Carvana sold in 2023-2024?
* What recourse provisions exist?
* Are any counterparties affiliated with insiders?
* Why has Carvana told investors for six years it’s seeking to diversify beyond Ally Financial but hasn’t announced new financing partners?

If management refuses counterparty-level disclosure, that refusal itself tells you something.

**My Take**

The legal special situations I typically write about offer asymmetric upside – small companies with credible claims against deep-pocketed defendants where even partial success delivers multibagger returns.

Carvana represents the mirror image: asymmetric downside.

The company faces simultaneous pressure from state regulators who can shut down operations, securities plaintiffs with discovery rights who’ve already survived two dismissal attempts, and thousands of consumers with legitimate grievances.

The operational compliance problems aren’t alleged – they’re documented. States suspended licences in 2022. Connecticut just settled in 2025. The pattern is established.

The insider selling creates terrible optics. When the CEO’s father dumps $5 billion in stock during a supposed recovery while only one other insider buys $200,000 worth, that’s not confidence.

And crucially, the “recovery” narrative depends entirely on continued loan sale gains that represent 2.2x net income. If Hindenburg’s related-party allegations prove accurate in discovery, or if counterparties simply become more cautious, that revenue line comes under immediate pressure.

Even in optimistic scenarios, legal costs consume hundreds of millions in cash and management attention over the next 2-3 years. In pessimistic scenarios, discovery reveals accounting practices that fundamentally change how investors value the business.

The math doesn’t work. This is asymmetric risk in the wrong direction.

### **Conclusion**

Carvana is a case study in fragility. It is built on subprime credit, leveraged to the max, and run by a family with a history of fraud who are actively dumping their shares.

It exhibits every single trait of the “Fragilista” error: mistaking the absence of immediate volatility for stability. They are picking up pennies in front of a steamroller, and they are using your money to do it.

The “scale economics shared” narrative is a lie. This is **“losses socialized, profits privatized.”**

The fundamentals are deteriorating.

The cash flow is negative.

The debt is compounding.
