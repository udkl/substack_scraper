## Why nobody showed up to defend the Russians

The single most important procedural fact in this case isn’t in any sell-side note I’ve read. Commerce’s preliminary AD margin of 132.83% was calculated on Adverse Facts Available because no Russian producer participated in the investigation. Commerce’s preliminary CVD rate of 109.10% was assigned on the same basis to JSC Urals Innovative Technologies and the Prioksky Plant of Non Ferrous Metals, the two named mandatory respondents, because neither responded to the subsidy questionnaire either.

AFA in practice: Commerce takes the petitioner’s calculation, applies it directly, moves on. No factual record to contest. No rebuttal filings on dumping margins. No surrogate-country cost methodology dispute. No benchmarking fight on Nornickel’s Talnakh ore body cash costs. The petitioner writes the number, Commerce applies the number, the number lands at the border.

One trading subsidiary through counsel, zero producers responding to the actual investigation. The USITC in Publication 5671 classified only two “respondent entities” in preliminary phase. ## The one US consumer who tried to stop this, and what it tells you

BASF Metals LLC filed comments at Commerce on August 12, 2025, before the investigation was even a month old, challenging industry support and standing. BASF entered an appearance at the ITC on August 8 (EDIS 859104) and filed a postconference brief on August 25 (EDIS 860368).

The Commission classified BASF as a “respondent entity” in its preliminary-phase publication alongside Metal Trade Overseas. Commerce’s initiation notice at 90 FR 41032 records BASF filing “a statement of opposition.” Petitioners filed rebuttal two days later. BASF is the only US downstream consumer who formally opposed the petition at either Commerce or the ITC during preliminary phase.

No other US downstream party filed at Commerce or the ITC during preliminary phase. The Alliance for Automotive Innovation, US OEMs, Umicore, Heraeus Metals New York, Tanaka — all absent from the EDIS docket. Johnson Matthey participated as a questionnaire respondent providing data, not arguments. Commerce rejected BASF’s standing challenge, the ITC voted 3-0 affirmative at preliminary, and footnote 30 of the Views of the Commission records that “BASF did not make any arguments regarding domestic like product.” BASF fought on procedural grounds because it didn’t have a substantive injury case to make.

The signal isn’t that the petition was uncontested. It’s that the petition was uncontested by any party who had to pass through the Pd cost in a vehicle price. The AAI, every US OEM, Umicore, Heraeus, Tanaka — all stayed silent in a case that mechanically raises the input cost of every US-built gasoline vehicle. That silence is data. It has to mean something. Three candidates:

**Candidate (a), 40%**: US OEMs and tier-one autocat manufacturers have partially substituted out of direct Russian-origin imports through Swiss bonded warehouses, Belgian refining, and Hong Kong intermediaries. The 44.4% Russian-origin share of US consumption in the twelve months ending June 2025 is the direct-import figure and doesn’t capture metal already processed through third-country intermediaries before US entry. If the true Russian-origin economic share (direct plus intermediary-processed) is closer to 55-60%, candidate (a) explains roughly half of AAI silence: existing intermediary chains absorb the marginal flow but can’t absorb the full displaced volume without margin compression getting passed through. They didn’t file because their rerouting partially insulated them, not because the order is non-binding.

The near-complete-rerouting version of (a) is implausible against the consumption-share data. Partial rerouting is consistent and is what I’m underwriting. This tightens the basis-premium implication: if (a) is partial rather than complete, Stillwater captures more spread than the worst-case (a) implies, because the marginal substitution flow into US-origin and clean third-country metal is larger.

**Candidate (b), 40%**: the political cost of publicly defending Russian palladium imports in 2026 was higher than the absorbed cost of the order. AAI’s government relations apparatus made a calculated decision that the headline risk of being the trade group defending Nornickel exceeded the dollar cost of the tariff. They didn’t file because they couldn’t file. Given the partial-rerouting reading of (a), this is the single most likely explanation.

**Candidate (c), 20%**: cost pass-through at $1,572 Pd is absorbable inside existing vehicle margins. Pd content per gasoline vehicle runs $250-400 at current prices. A 50% effective cost increase on the Russian-sourced fraction translates to single-digit dollars per vehicle. They didn’t file because the math didn’t justify the legal spend.

The decomposition matters because (a) and (c) are bearish for the basis-premium thesis and (b) is neutral. If (a) dominates, the rerouted volume reduces the scarcity pressure that sets the US clearing premium, compressing basis across the entire US market. Stillwater still captures 100% of that clearing premium on its own production (intermediaries earn arbitrage on rerouted flows, not margin from Stillwater), but the clearing premium itself is lower than in a clean-enforcement scenario.

Weighting through the rebalanced (a) at 40% partial rerouting rather than 55% near-complete: the probability-weighted year-one US basis premium is 8-11%, not the 10-20% the headline math suggests. I’m running 9% as the planning number. On 223,000 Pd ounces at $1,572 spot, that’s roughly $31.5M of incremental annualized EBITDA from basis. It’s smaller than the bull modeling range would imply and larger than near-complete-rerouting scenarios would produce, though I have not recast the bull and bear anchor figures on the corrected denominator.

Mitigating factor on (a): Sibanye-Stillwater, as sole US petitioner, has standing and incentive to file circumvention petitions the moment suspicious import patterns appear. Commerce’s AFA-trained posture suggests responsiveness. Circumvention inquiries on refined PGMs run 12-18 months per case. Year one is the highest-spread year regardless. Years two and three depend on enforcement.

## Deriving the planning number

The 9% is load-bearing. It produces the $31.5M of basis EBITDA stacking on top of $108-114M from the Pd price lift, and it drives the SBSW $14.50-15.50 base case. The arithmetic has to be visible.

*The theoretical maximum*. In a clean-enforcement world with 100% of Russian volume walled off and zero rerouting, the US basis premium is set by the cost of pulling the marginal non-Russian ton into the US market. Two anchors triangulate the ceiling. USGS Open File Report 2025-1047 (Nassar et al., 2025), the methodology paper underpinning the 2025 US List of Critical Minerals, models a complete one-year restriction of US net imports of Russian Pd and concludes US domestic prices would lift by up to 24%.

Upper bound; the AD/CVD case is strictly less severe because Russian metal remains in the global market and can reroute through third countries over multi-year horizons. The Section 232 aluminum analog gives the lower bound: a 50% tariff on 85% of US supply produced a Midwest Premium widening of $200-300/ton over LME at baseline, or 10-15% basis. Palladium has a higher duty wall (241.93% combined before offsets) but a smaller tariffed supply share (44.4% vs aluminum’s 85%).

The two effects partially offset. Calibrating against both anchors, 15% is the planning number for theoretical maximum. Conservative; a reader wanting the upper case can run 18-20% and scale linearly.

*Realized premium by candidate*. Each AAI candidate produces a different discount to the theoretical maximum through a different mechanism.

Candidate (a), partial rerouting at 40% weight. Rerouting 30-40% of displaced Russian volume through Swiss bonded warehouses, Belgian refineries, Armenian intermediaries, and Hong Kong relieves scarcity pressure setting the US clearing premium. Stillwater still realizes the full basis on every ounce sold (the premium accrues to whoever sells at the US clearing price, not to whoever controls the intermediary chain), but the clearing premium compresses to 60-70% of theoretical max. Realized: 15% × 65% = 9.75%. The 30-40% rerouting range is a judgment call against directional evidence, not a quantified input.

Direct US Russian Pd imports rose 34% from 2021 to 2024 and reached 44.4% of US imports in H1 2025, inconsistent with near-complete rerouting having already occurred; NYMEX warehouse stocks are up 169% since petition filing, indicating end-user inventory hedging rather than confident reliance on rerouting.

Candidate (b), political cost at 40% weight. No rerouting beyond baseline circumvention of 10-15% in year one, because the mechanism (trade associations declining to defend Russian metal publicly) doesn’t change physical flows. Realized: 15% × 85% = 12.75%.

Candidate (c), margin absorption at 20% weight. Absorption explains why US downstream didn’t file opposition, not whether OEMs keep buying Russian metal at tariff-inclusive prices. Even under absorption, OEMs substitute away from Russian supply because tariff-inclusive Russian exceeds non-Russian clearing. Mechanically similar to (b). Realized: 15% × 85% = 12.75%.

Weighted year-one US basis premium: 9.75% × 0.40 + 12.75% × 0.40 + 12.75% × 0.20 = 11.55%.

*Execution risk discount*. Circumvention enforcement runs 12-18 months per case at Commerce. CBP rules-of-origin policing at the border on refined PGMs has historically required petitioner-driven inquiries. Apply a 15% execution haircut: 11.55% × 0.85 = 9.8%, rounded to 10%.

I’m carrying 9% rather than 10% to leave margin for unmodeled frictions (Nornickel-side pricing behavior, OFAC supersession risk already inside kill switch 6, residual uncertainty on the AAI weights). A reader running the less conservative case at 10-11% should expect SBSW at $15.50-$16.50 rather than $14.50-$15.50, still below the sell-side range. The choice between 9% and 11% is a conviction question on execution, not a quantification question on setup.

*Sensitivity. At $1,572 Pd spot and 223,000 Pd ounces of US production, each percentage point of basis premium contributes $3.50M of annualized US PGM segment EBITDA.*

**The closest recent precedent, and what it complicates**

The Russian uranium ban is the nearest structural analog to this trade in the last three years. H.R. 1042 (Prohibiting Russian Uranium Imports Act) was signed May 13, 2024, effective August 11, 2024, with a DOE waiver system running through January 1, 2028 and statutory sunset on December 31, 2040.

The House passed on December 11, 2023. Russia retaliated with a TENEX export license revocation in November 2024. Close enough in structure (Russian dominant supplier into thin Western market, US statutory action, 8-18 month run-up from House vote through effective date) to serve as a precedent. Different enough in mechanism (statutory ban with administrative waivers versus AD/CVD calculated on AFA) that the read cuts in multiple directions. Worth walking through honestly.

The restricted service premium materialized and persisted. TradeTech’s SWU spot indicator ran from approximately $152/SWU at House passage in December 2023 to $174/SWU by June 2024 (roughly +14% through the signing window), with 2024 spot average at $172.67/SWU (+20% year-on-year per UxC) and long-term SWU at $160.08 (+10%). This is the direct analog to the US palladium basis premium, and at 20% it sits above my 9% planning number and inside the 15% theoretical maximum. The uranium case supports the basis premium framework and suggests the 9% may be conservative.

The spot commodity underlying the restricted service went the other way. U3O8 peaked at approximately $106/lb in January 2024, four months before the May signing and seven months before the August effective date, and faded to $73.50/lb by year-end 2024 and $63/lb by March 2025. Textbook sell-the-news on the spot. If palladium spot follows the same pattern, Pd at $1,572/oz on the April 17 close is closer to U3O8 at its January 2024 peak than to U3O8 after the ban took effect.

That is the uncomfortable read for the operating leverage thesis: the $108-114M from price alone may be front-loaded rather than back-loaded, and what I am modeling as steady-state EBITDA at current spot could be the peak year.

Centrus Energy (LEU), as the closest equity analog to SBSW (sole US domestic producer of the restricted service), traded in a 52-week range of $60.52 to $464.25 and closed at $202.88 on April 18, 2026. The equity run was real, but happened largely through 2025, 12-24 months after the legislative catalysts, and was driven by HALEU production milestones, $2.7B in appropriated federal funding, DOE contract wins, the Trump administration’s nuclear push, and Palantir/Oklo partnerships, with the Russian ban as one tailwind among several.

Attributing the bulk of the Centrus run to the tariff catalyst is causal inference the evidence does not support. The read for SBSW is that the equity leg has more duration than the 8-12 week window suggests, but the drivers that carry it past the catalyst are operating leverage on Pd and Pt spot, the 45X credit intact period through 2030, Sibanye’s strategic repositioning, and the gold exposure, not the tariff itself.

Data I’m working around. My Centrus pre-House-passage price is inferred from surrounding reference points (52-week low of $60.52, “nearly quadrupled over 12 months” framing from January 2026 at $286), not a directly pulled close, and should be treated as approximate. I have not separated UF6 conversion pricing from SWU pricing, and conversion may be the tighter physical-throughput analog to palladium basis than SWU (which is services). I have not surveyed subsequent uranium trade actions post-ban for whether they sharpen or weaken this read. None of these caveats change the directional conclusions, but a reader wanting to stress-test the analog further has three places to push.

Three things the analog does not test. No futures positioning analog exists because uranium enrichment has no COT report; the positioning squeeze half of this thesis cannot be precedent-tested from the uranium case at all. The waiver mechanism in uranium provided an administrative off-ramp (DOE granted Centrus waivers for 2024, 2025, 2026, and 2027 committed deliveries) that palladium’s AFA architecture does not provide, and the SWU premium still materialized with waivers in place; this weakens my framing of AFA as categorically different from a waiver regime, though AFA likely provides tighter enforcement at the margin. And the Centrus equity move happened 12-24 months after catalysts, not around them, which counter-argues the “hard expiry on the combined book” framing.

The honest version. The uranium precedent supports the basis premium thesis, complicates the commodity price thesis, weakens the AFA-as-unique-edge argument, and is silent on the futures squeeze. Sizing does not change from what I’ve already committed to (2-3% PA futures, 4% SBSW core scaling to 6%), but the equity leg probably carries more duration than the futures leg implies, which aligns with how the kill switches are structured. One confirming precedent element (basis premium), one disconfirming (sell-the-news on the commodity), two precedent-null (positioning squeeze, AFA edge). That’s a mixed signal, not a clean confirmation, and I’d rather publish it mixed than pretend it clean.

## The J-M Reef: what it is and why there’s exactly one in the United States

The Stillwater Complex is a 2.7-billion-year-old layered mafic intrusion outcropping along a 28-mile band of the Beartooth Mountains, 130 km southwest of Billings. Johns-Manville geologists discovered the PGM-bearing horizon within the Complex in 1974 through geochemical surveys (Sibanye-Stillwater’s current disclosure; peer-reviewed literature gives “early 1970s”), though the underlying sulfide mineralization had been known since the 1930s. They named it the J-M Reef, and until a comparable deposit turns up somewhere else, it’s the only known significant source of PGMs in the United States and the highest-grade PGM deposit in the world.

Geologically, it’s a 2.4-meter-thick layer of pyrrhotite-chalcopyrite-pentlandite sulfides near the base of the Banded Series, carrying PGM mineralization at a palladium-to-platinum ratio of roughly 3.4:1 to 3.6:1. The J-M Reef is what makes Stillwater’s production 77% palladium and 23% platinum, effectively inverted from the Bushveld Complex’s Merensky and UG2 reefs in South Africa where Pt:Pd typically runs in platinum’s favor. It’s also what gives the US deposit its unusual relevance to the Russian palladium trade: Nornickel’s Norilsk-Taimyr ore body is similarly palladium-rich, and the two deposits are economic substitutes in the autocatalyst market in a way South African supply isn’t.

[![](../assets/c2856dc335545ed3a0ecbd0ba0a6aa75.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!BVNO!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F62cdbebb-3c95-49d7-b1d1-fecc40b1cc40_1405x763.png)

Sibanye-Stillwater reported US PGM total 2E Mineral Reserves of 19 million ounces and total 2E Mineral Resources of 79.1 million ounces as at December 31, 2024, on the J-M Reef. At the 2026 guidance midpoint of 290,000 2Eoz per year, those reserves carry a nominal mine life of approximately 65 years at current cut-off grade.

The point isn’t the headline number but that the resource constraint on this operation unambiguously isn’t the rock. It’s the capital cost to develop it. Continuous mineralization has been confirmed along the full 28-mile extent of the Complex through historical surface drilling, with extensive portions still awaiting detailed delineation.

## Stillwater (the mine) versus East Boulder: two different cost structures inside one franchise

The J-M Reef is worked by two separate mines. The distinction matters because they aren’t the same business.

Stillwater mine sits at the eastern end of the Complex near the town of Nye. Production since 1986, operating through two principal mining sections:

Stillwater West: in operation from 1986 until Q4 2024 when it was placed on care and maintenance as part of the restructuring. Accessed through a 580-meter-deep shaft and five surface portals.

Stillwater East: began ore production in 2017, accessed via three portal drives. Higher-grade than Stillwater West in the current mining sequence.

East Boulder sits 20 km west of Stillwater, developed at an original cost of $370 million and accessed via twin 5,800-meter tunnel-bore-machine portal drives. First production in 2002. Different mining dynamics: lower in-situ grade than Stillwater (historically about 14.7 g/t Pt+Pd reserve grade at East Boulder vs about 19.4 g/t at Stillwater per the 2019 technical report), but much more mechanized access via the TBM drives, which changes the labor-to-ore ratio and the marginal mining cost profile.

The distinction is the first thing sell-side models generally miss. When analysts model “Stillwater AISC at $1,203/oz” and apply it uniformly, they’re averaging across three underground operations (West, East, and East Boulder) with genuinely different unit economics. The restructuring focused 2025 mining on Stillwater East’s higher-grade material plus targeted East Boulder stopes where grade and productivity justified the cost. The 2025 Q1 production mix reflects this: Stillwater produced 40,063 2Eoz, East Boulder produced 31,928 2Eoz, and Q1 plant head grade ran at 13.91 g/t, the highest level since Q2 2020, explicitly because of the mix shift toward higher-grade Stillwater East feed.

The 45-million-tonne combined reserve base at the two active operations isn’t a peer to most PGM deposits globally. It’s an outlier, and the outlier is what makes the operating leverage extraordinary through a price cycle.

## Terminal value under the 45X phase-out

At $110/oz on 290k oz, 45X contributes approximately $32M of annualized segment EBITDA at current scale. PV of the full phase-out stream at 10% discount is approximately $147M, or $0.20 per SBSW ADR. Against a counterfactual permanent-45X world, the phase-out costs approximately $173M in present value, or $0.24 per ADR. Small numbers because the R3.8B Sibanye impairment already reflects the write-down in reported book value, and the sell-side DCFs anchoring the $17-$18 target already embed the phase-out. The catalyst trade doesn’t need to adjust.

The offsetting lever is Sibanye’s January 2026 strategy update commitment to a $1,000/2Eoz AISC target in 2024 real terms, against 2026 guidance of $1,520-$1,580. Adjusting the target for expected inflation to the delivery year (likely 2028-2030) puts the nominal target at approximately $1,190/oz, producing a runway of $330-$390/oz on 290k oz, or $96-$113M of annualized EBITDA uplift at full realization. At 50% realization the phase-out is absorbed. At 25% realization it’s roughly neutral. Below 25% realization the long-term holder faces real terminal value compression.

Timing is the residual risk. The 45X phase-out bites 2031-2033. The AISC target has no announced delivery date. If Sibanye delivers the AISC program by 2030, the offset is in place when the credit rolls off. If the program slips to 2035 or beyond (typical for mining cost initiatives), there’s a 1-3 year gap worth $1-$2/ADR of compressed terminal value. The trigger to recalibrate is the 2027 Sibanye investor day and the 2026-2028 operating results, not the ITC vote. The 45X phase-out isn’t a reason to shorten the equity leg. It’s a reason to underwrite AISC execution explicitly for any holder past 2027.

**The operating leverage that every model is undersizing**

The US PGM segment generates roughly $18-19M of annualized segment EBITDA per $100 move in the palladium price at current production scale. The arithmetic: 290,000 2Eoz at guidance midpoint is a basket, not a palladium count. At the 77/23 Pd/Pt reef ratio, that’s 223,000 Pd ounces and 67,000 Pt ounces. A $100 move in Pd alone (holding Pt flat) produces $22.3M of incremental gross revenue on the Pd ounces only, since Pt is priced off its own benchmark. At 80-85% flow-through to EBITDA after Montana royalty and effective tax (21% federal plus single-digit state, partially offset by the 45X credit), that’s $18-19M per $100 Pd move. At $1,572 spot vs the $900-1,000 levels that triggered the 2024 restructuring, the segment is generating approximately $108-121M of incremental annualized EBITDA purely on the palladium price alone, before any US-basis premium from the ITC-affirmed order.

That’s the pre-catalyst baseline. Catalyst premium stacks on top.

Once the AD/CVD order publishes, domestic producers sell into a bifurcated market. Russian metal still clears to Asia, likely at a yuan-denominated discount to LBMA, because Nornickel won’t stockpile the 800,000 ounces a year previously clearing to US end-users. It’ll move to Shanghai. US refiners, autocat manufacturers, and OEMs buying Russian-origin metal face the combined cash-deposit wall (132.83% AD plus 109.10% CVD before statutory export-subsidy offsets) on every entry, so in practice they don’t buy Russian-origin metal at the border. What they buy is Stillwater, South African, Canadian, recycled, and third-country-processed material at a premium to the global clearing price.

The probability-weighted basis premium is 9% in year one, declining to 4-6% in years two and three as third-country leakage stabilizes. On 223,000 Pd ounces at $1,572 spot, that’s $31.5M of incremental EBITDA in year one, $14-21M in years two through three. This is the number that flows into the price target. Smaller than the bull modeling range, and the downward revision from the headline 10-20% is what separates this thesis from the sell-side notes.

The operating leverage on the metal price itself is the larger lever. At $1,572 vs $900-1,000, the segment is generating $108-114M of incremental EBITDA purely on price, before any basis premium. The basis premium is the kicker. The Pd price is the thesis.

Risk is consensus reading the AD final as catalyst-complete and selling through the May/June window before the ITC affirms; protection is sequencing exits to the cover rather than the publication.

*Russia reroutes and the order leaks*. Scope language in the prelim determinations covers HTSUS 7110.21.0000 (unwrought palladium and powder) and 7110.29.0000 (other unwrought forms), and the CVD scope includes third-country processing language. Commerce anticipated Russian material moving through Armenia, Swiss bonded warehouses, Hong Kong, Dubai. Anticipating isn’t catching. CBP enforcement against rules-of-origin gaming on refined PGMs has historically required affirmative petitioner-driven circumvention inquiries, each 12-18 months. If transshipment handles 30-40% of displaced Russian tonnes in the first 18 months, the US-basis premium compresses. Stillwater still captures the rest, but the trade doesn’t return what it returns in clean enforcement. Mitigating factor: Sibanye-Stillwater has standing and incentive to file circumvention petitions the moment suspicious patterns appear, and Commerce’s AFA-trained posture suggests responsiveness. Unmitigated version: OFAC separately acts on Russian PGMs via sanctions (making AD/CVD redundant), which could happen as part of broader Russia sanctions tightening and changes the legal basis for the US premium.

*The ITC walks at final*. ITC’s final injury determination applies a stricter standard than the preliminary “reasonable indication” finding, and the Commission makes affirmative final determinations in roughly half of contested cases. A negative ITC final vote vacates the case, releases all cash deposits collected since February 19, and ends the trade-remedy path for at least two years. Cases flipping affirmative preliminary to negative final are usually ones where the domestic industry recovers during the investigation, where imports fall for reasons unrelated to dumping, or where injury turns out attributable to demand factors rather than subject imports.

None applies here: Russian imports rose 50% interim 2025 vs interim 2024, subject imports reached 44.4% in the twelve months ending June 2025, Stillwater was on care-and-maintenance curtailment for part of 2025. Strong injury record. But the ITC is an independent agency, commissioner voting patterns can shift, and a political intervention (OFAC sanctions superseding AD/CVD, a Commerce-negotiated suspension agreement under 19 USC § 1673c, or a broader US-Russia diplomatic resolution) can’t be ruled out to zero.

*Demand destruction from the vehicle mix*. Palladium is a gasoline-ICE autocatalyst metal. EV penetration continues. On substitution, the current spread points the other way: platinum is trading at approximately $2,684 vs palladium at $1,572, a Pt premium of roughly $1,112 and a Pt-Pd ratio of 1.71 vs the historical average of 2.8. At this spread, the economic incentive is reverse substitution (Pt back to Pd) in gasoline autocat, which is a structural Pd demand tailwind, not a headwind. WPIC forecast the reverse-substitution cycle for 2026 in its mid-2024 outlook, and industry reports through early 2026 show incremental movement in that direction. The bear case on this axis is not substitution but rather EV penetration itself, which continues to erode the gasoline-ICE share of new fleet regardless of Pt-Pd economics. Demand destruction risk on the piece’s 18-36 month window is therefore bounded by the EV transition pace, which is slower in the US than in China or Europe and which multiple OEMs have publicly extended timelines on through 2025-2026.

*The Swap Dealer composition is wrong*. The 70/22/8 probability split is informed by three triangulating strands but not decisive. If the actual decomposition is closer to 30% end-user / 70% producer hedging, the squeeze dynamic compresses by 40-50% from the headline asymmetry. Position sizing already discounts for this, but a reader running larger size than 2-3% notional on the futures leg should reweight. Single piece of evidence that would update the prior: a Sibanye disclosure on US PGM forward sales in the Q1 2026 production report (expected late April / early May), or a Nornickel statement on Western hedge counterparty unwinds. Neither is guaranteed. Worth flagging: AAI partial-rerouting reading and the Swap Dealer end-user reading move together; if AAI silence turns out primarily political (candidate (b)) rather than partial-rerouting (candidate (a)), the third triangulating strand weakens and the prior compresses back toward 65/25/10.

*“This is a trade not an investment” risk*. Thesis runs on an 8-12 week window. Operating leverage and US-basis premium arguments for SBSW as a longer-term holding depend on palladium staying above $1,500/oz structurally, which requires resolution of EV transition timing and Chinese demand absorption that’s genuinely uncertain past 2027. Positioning trade, not an investment thesis. Confusing the two is how good trades become bad holds.

## Pricing the options structure

The call spread plus put structure needs pricing against the vol surface. Numbers below are illustrative estimates against a modeled vol surface (ATM IV 58% May, 62% June, 68% July, with typical catalyst-stock skew). Readers should confirm against live bid-ask before entry; SBSW 30-day IV has ranged from 45% to 75% over the past twelve months and the structure economics shift inside that range.

At SBSW spot $13.19 and April 17 close:

May 13/16 call spread: approximately $0.78 net debit per share, $2.22 max payoff if SBSW finishes at or above $16, breakeven $13.78. Reward-to-risk 2.85x.

June 14/17 call spread: approximately $0.64 net debit, $2.36 max payoff, breakeven $14.64. June 19 expiry coincides with the earliest statutory ITC final vote date, so this is the cleanest catalyst expression. Reward-to-risk 3.69x.

July $10 put sale: approximately $0.44 credit, offsetting part of the call spread debit. If SBSW stays above $10 through July 17 the credit is retained; if SBSW breaks $10 the sold put generates open-risk loss (capped only by the separate long $9 put hedge).

July $9 put purchase at 10-15% of equity delta: approximately $0.28 debit per share. ITC termination hedge, separate from the call spread financing. Together with the $10 short put it forms a bull put spread with $0.84/share max loss.

Full structure net cost: approximately $1.26/share ($1.42 call spread debit, minus $0.44 put credit, plus $0.28 hedge debit). Base case payoff at SBSW $15.50 at June expiry with stock above $10 at July: approximately $3.22/share. Reward-to-risk at base case 2.56x. Worst case drawdown (SBSW to $8, ITC walks): approximately $2.70/share loss, 2.14x initial outlay.

What moves with higher IV is the probability distribution, not the spread cost: wider distributions reduce the probability of finishing at max payoff, while put sale economics improve (more credit), partially offsetting.

Trigger to stop adding: SBSW trading above $14 before entry (compresses May spread asymmetry below 2x) or SBSW 30-day IV above 75% (probability of max payoff falls below 12%). The “stop above 65% IV” rule is directionally right but mechanically imprecise; the binding constraint is distribution width, not spread cost.

Sizing: at $1.26/share against a 4-6% equity position, a 1-2% options notional overlay funds 13-20 contract sets per $1M of equity, contributing 1-3% of effective equity delta through the window.

**Paired trade** for position-flat institutional readers: long SBSW / short auto OEM cost basket (F, GM, STLA). Autocat cost pass-through squeeze. The OEMs eat a fraction of the Pd cost rise, Stillwater captures a fraction of the basis spread, and the pair removes most of the macro tape risk. Beta-neutral sizing at 1:1 dollar notional. The more capital-efficient expression if outright SBSW is already crowded in the book, or if the macro environment in late April forces equity net exposure lower.

**ITC termination hedge**: SBSW July 2026 $9 puts at 10-15% of long-equity delta. Drag on returns if the order publishes cleanly. Capital preservation if the ITC vote surprises negative. Priced cheaply because the market assigns low probability to the negative outcome, which is exactly why the hedge is worth carrying. If the April 27 hearing produces evidence of Commissioner skepticism toward the injury finding, I increase the hedge to 25-30% of delta.
