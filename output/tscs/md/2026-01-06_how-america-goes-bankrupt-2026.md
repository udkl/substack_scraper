### Illusion of “Have It All” Economics

For the past fifty years, since the unilateral closing of the gold window in 1971 (thanks Nixon), the United States has operated under a specific, albeit artificial economic operating system.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!cYrf!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fdcbdecaf-0932-45f3-a8c5-ce5818406d32_1405x750.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!cYrf!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fdcbdecaf-0932-45f3-a8c5-ce5818406d32_1405x750.png)

This system, which was significantly accelerated by the entrance of China into the World Trade Organization, relied on a mechanism so simple it borders elegant, provided one ignores the long-term rot it induces.

The United States exported dollars and imported deflation.

By offshoring its industrial base to low-cost jurisdictions, primarily China, the US enjoyed cheap goods, low inflation, and a strong bond market.

The “deal” was straightforward: We send them pieces of green paper; they send us toaster ovens, iPhones, and fentanyl precursors. They then take those green pieces of paper and recycle them back into US Treasuries, suppressing US interest rates and allowing the American government to run deficits that would make a banana republic blush.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!tvD5!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F5d2367f5-aaff-4244-be15-2f497040d164_1404x747.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!tvD5!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F5d2367f5-aaff-4244-be15-2f497040d164_1404x747.png)

“We send them dollars for the stuff, they recycle the dollars into our capital markets... fundamentally there is... I get accused a lot of being anti-American or non-patriotic. All I’m doing is double entry bookkeeping and double entry bookkeeping doesn’t care about your political views”. - Luke Gromen

This mechanism worked brilliantly for a specific triad of beneficiaries: Global Capital, Washington DC, and the “Too Big to Fail” banks. It was, however, an absolute catastrophe for the American working class and the defense industrial base, which were hollowed out via debt-based consumption. The US traded its factories for cheap credit, its sovereignty for cheap goods.

But now, the geopolitical architecture has completely changed.

The US government, bridging the divide between the Biden and Trump administrations, has decided that re-shoring (bringing the factories back home) is now a matter of national survival.

The rise of China as a peer competitor, the fragility of supply chains exposed by COVID-19, and the dawn of the AI arms race have made “industrial policy” the new state religion in Washington.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!i38a!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F49d788c8-61e4-4e5e-a013-b339c54f4821_1405x773.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!i38a!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F49d788c8-61e4-4e5e-a013-b339c54f4821_1405x773.png)

Herein lies the fatal flaw in the plan that Wall Street has yet to price in: **You cannot reshore the industrial base and maintain the real value of the Treasury market simultaneously.**

The two goals are diametrically opposed in the balance of payments. But it seems like everyone is too stupid to understand this.

### The Accounting Identity Crisis

The logic is rooted in the inescapable laws of the balance of payments. If the US wishes to reshore, it must stop sending net dollars abroad to purchase foreign goods.

If the US stops sending dollars abroad, foreigners cease to accumulate the dollars necessary to buy US Treasuries.

If foreigners stop buying US Treasuries, who finances the US government’s deficit?

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!ZHWm!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F20f82d8c-9309-42fc-9129-8424de20d554_1405x773.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!ZHWm!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F20f82d8c-9309-42fc-9129-8424de20d554_1405x773.png)

This is double-entry bookkeeping acting as the grim reaper. If capital moves from the financial account (buying bonds) to the current account (building factories), interest rates in the US must rise to attract the remaining scarce capital. In a normal environment, with low debt levels, say, the 20% debt-to-GDP of 1980, this would be a healthy, albeit painful, adjustment.

But the US is not in a normal environment. It is swimming in a debt-to-GDP ratio of roughly 120%. The economy, financialized to its eyeballs, cannot handle positive real interest rates. The private sector, levered to the hilt on the assumption of “lower for longer,” would implode.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!OTft!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fbad37603-b231-4f00-a1f2-ad98443fe031_1405x786.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!OTft!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fbad37603-b231-4f00-a1f2-ad98443fe031_1405x786.png)

“We’ve seen that the US equity markets economy doesn’t handle 4.8%. 8% in the 10-year very well. So you’re going to have to put a ceiling on that 10-year.” - Luke Gromen

This is the “Mother of All Crises.” It is not a crisis of liquidity, which the Fed can print its way out of. It is a crisis of solvency and physical reality.

The US government is like a homeowner who wants to renovate their kitchen (reshore) but relies on the contractor (China) to lend them the money for the renovation. If you tell the contractor to get lost, you also lose the loan.

The US wants to fight a trade war with its banker.

### The “Notebook”

This can all be visualised in the analogy of the film The Notebook (credits to Luke for this), where the protagonist is asked repeatedly, screaming in the rain, “What do you want?” This is the question the bond market is currently asking the Federal Reserve and the US Treasury.

It is the question that defines the next decade of asset allocation.

Do you want to beat China in the AI and industrial arms race?

* **The Cost:** You must print trillions of dollars to build the grid, subsidize factories, and secure resources. You must run deficits that make the COVID stimulus look like a rounding error.
* **The Consequence:** Inflation roars back. The bond market (Treasuries) gets incinerated in real terms as yields are capped below the rate of inflation to keep the government solvent. The dollar loses its purchasing power, but the factories get built, and the missiles get made in America.

Do you want to save the Bond Market and the Dollar’s Reserve Status?

* **The Cost:** You must maintain high real interest rates to attract global capital. You must impose austerity.
* **The Consequence:** You crush domestic demand. You make industrial investment impossible because the cost of capital is too high. You concede the industrial and AI race to China. You maintain the “value” of your currency, but you have no factories, no grid, and eventually, no security. You become the United Kingdom of 1940, but without a benevolent America to bail you out.

The market is currently betting that the US can look for a middle path, a “soft landing” where we reshore without inflation and without crushing the bond market.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!0EiG!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F1422bdc0-645b-4e8d-bb19-7a5e9c0feaf6_1405x766.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!0EiG!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F1422bdc0-645b-4e8d-bb19-7a5e9c0feaf6_1405x766.png)

This is a fantasy.

The physics of the situation dictates that you cannot have a manufacturing boom funded by a sovereign that is already fiscally incontinent without destroying the currency or the bond market. The “middle path” is simply a delay tactic, a form of bargaining before the inevitable acceptance.

## The Trigger – Physics vs. Finance

If the balance of payments is the gunpowder, Artificial Intelligence (AI) and the electrical grid are the spark.

For decades, Western economists have operated under the assumption that financial capital is the scarce resource. If you have money (or credit), you can buy anything. This “financialization” of the mind has led to a neglect of the physical reality that underpins the economy.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!jRkC!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F5a06a1b1-b95e-43a2-a7fe-dc2236c0b2b0_1405x780.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!jRkC!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F5a06a1b1-b95e-43a2-a7fe-dc2236c0b2b0_1405x780.png)

We forgot that you cannot eat Bitcoin, and you cannot build a tank with a credit default swap.

Gromen argues that we are transitioning from a world where finance constrains physics to a world where physics constrains finance.

You can print dollars, but you cannot print a gigawatt of electricity. You cannot print rare earth minerals. You cannot print a transformer.

### The AI Energy Cliff

The current hype cycle around AI focuses on Large Language Models (LLMs) and GPU chips, the domain of Nvidia and Microsoft. However, the bottleneck has shifted. The constraint isn’t chips. The constraint is grid. The constraint is rare earths. And these are not things the Fed can print. They have to actually get them.

The data processing requirements for AI are not incremental; they are exponential. We are attempting to layer a massive new energy demand on top of an electrical grid that has essentially flatlined in capacity for two decades. The US grid is aging, balkanized, and regulatory-constrained. It is a third-world grid with a first-world stock market attached to it.

Contrast this with China.

### The Great Divergence

While the US spent the last twenty years “singing away the summer,” focused on software, financial engineering, and consumer apps, China was the ant, relentlessly building physical infrastructure. The disparity in grid capacity is terrifying.

“The Chinese grid passed ours and in the last 10 years the Chinese have installed grid capacity equal to the entirety of the US grid,” - Luke Gromen

China has built the “base layer” of the 21st-century economy while the US was busy optimizing ad-click algorithms.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!7iAP!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F7f0ad243-07d6-458c-b189-31440a98a98a_1405x789.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!7iAP!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F7f0ad243-07d6-458c-b189-31440a98a98a_1405x789.png)

If the US wants to compete in AI, it needs electricity. To get electricity, it needs to build power plants and transmission lines. To build those, it needs raw materials and skilled labor.

* **Labor Constraint:** The US unemployment rate for skilled trades is effectively zero. To attract workers to build the grid, wages must rise.
* **Material Constraint:** The US lacks domestic supply of key components (large power transformers, rare earths), many of which are controlled by China.

“And to get them, they actually have to pay people to get them. And to pay people to get them, you’ve got to pay a positive real rate. If you pay a positive real rate, you’re going to have wage inflation.” - Luke Gromen

This wage inflation feeds back into the bond market. Higher wages mean higher inflation, which pushes long-term yields higher. Higher yields crash the economy (as discussed in Section 1). Thus, the physical necessity of building the grid triggers the financial crisis.

### The Nuclear Option (and its Cost)

Nuclear energy is often seen as the savior. However, the cost differential between the US and China in nuclear construction highlights the depth of the US “competitiveness crisis.”

The data provided by venture capitalist Josh Wolfe in his congressional testimony is nothing short of shocking.

The cost per gigawatt of nuclear power in China is roughly **1/6th** the price of a gigawatt in the United States. Specifically, China is producing nuclear energy for approximately **$2 billion per gigawatt**, while recent US projects (like the Vogtle plant in Georgia) have cost upwards of **$11.5 billion per gigawatt**, and that’s if they finish at all.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!5OEj!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fb7e5a2df-0b00-4d45-a511-f6b1a996d82b_1405x787.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!5OEj!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fb7e5a2df-0b00-4d45-a511-f6b1a996d82b_1405x787.png)

This is not a 10% or 20% gap. It is a **600%** gap. “A gigawatt is a gigawatt... It’s not like it’s a magic yuan gigawatt versus a magic dollar gigawatt,” - Gromen.

This implies that for the US to be competitive with China in the foundational cost of energy, the US dollar would need to devalue by roughly **87%** against the yuan. Alternatively, the US would need to impose tariffs of 300% to 500% to equalize the cost basis.

* **Scenario A:** Devalue the dollar 87%. The bond market is vaporized.
* **Scenario B:** Impose 400% tariffs. Inflation skyrockets. The bond market is vaporized.

The trigger is the physical reality that the US industrial base is uncompetitive at the current exchange rate, and the investment required to fix it (the grid) is inflationary by definition. The “elemental energy” advantage that China possesses is forcing the US into a corner where it must choose between its currency and its power plants.

## Math Doesn’t Lie (Data Analysis)

To understand the severity of the crisis, one must look beyond the narratives and stare directly at the “hard data.” The numbers describe a sovereign entity that has lost control of its fiscal destiny. This is a matter of arithmetic (opinions aren’t always facts my friends).

### The Debt Spiral and True Interest Expense

The United States debt-to-GDP ratio stands at approximately **120%**. This is a level historically associated with default or massive currency devaluation. But the stock of debt is less concerning than the flow of interest payments relative to income.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!_o61!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Ff8414cd8-0461-445a-a2cb-d3ed8b3c2397_1406x789.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!_o61!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Ff8414cd8-0461-445a-a2cb-d3ed8b3c2397_1406x789.png)

“US true interest expense, even with record tariffs, even with record receipts... is still **96%** of trailing 12 months receipts.” - Gromen

**The Implication:** 96 cents of every dollar the US government collects in tax revenue is immediately walked out the door to pay for past promises. This leaves only **4 cents** for the entire rest of the government, the military, the Department of Justice, the National Parks, the grid, and those AI subsidies we discussed.

### “Basis Trade” Time Bomb

A critical vulnerability in the Treasury market is who is currently buying the debt. It is not foreign central banks (who have been net sellers or flat for a decade). It is not the Federal Reserve (currently in Quantitative Tightening). It is the **Hedge Fund Basis Trade**.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!WwyB!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F88ec5ecd-cb1d-4899-98e5-f5b797ceac44_1406x789.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!WwyB!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F88ec5ecd-cb1d-4899-98e5-f5b797ceac44_1406x789.png)

According to a Federal Reserve white paper, this trade has become the marginal buyer of US sovereign debt. “That creates a riskoff amongst the levered hedge fund basis trade that has been responsible per the Fed that of **37%** of long-term Treasury issuance since January 2022” (ok I’m not going to quote Luke Gromen all the time, its safe to assume that all the quotation marks are his words).

How the Trade Works:

Hedge funds exploit a tiny price discrepancy between the cash Treasury bond and the Treasury futures contract. They buy the cash bond and short the future. Because the spread is miniscule, they must use massive leverage (often 50x or 100x) funded in the repo market to make the trade profitable.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!RE0r!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fc4ab8acf-3211-49dc-abd1-0c568a21e68a_1404x791.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!RE0r!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fc4ab8acf-3211-49dc-abd1-0c568a21e68a_1404x791.png)

The Systemic Risk:

This is “hot money” in its purest form. It is not an investment based on the belief that the US is a creditworthy borrower. To put it simply, you can look at it as an arbitrage trade based on market structure.

* **The Trigger:** If volatility spikes (e.g., due to an inflation scare or a geopolitical shock), the margin requirements on the futures short increase.
* **The Unwind:** Hedge funds are forced to de-lever. To do so, they must sell the cash Treasuries they hold.
* **The Feedback Loop:** Selling Treasuries spikes yields. Spiking yields increases volatility. Higher volatility forces more margin calls and more selling.

We saw a preview of this in March 2020, when the Treasury market went “no bid.” The Fed had to step in with trillions of dollars to save it. With hedge funds now absorbing nearly 40% of issuance, the next unwind will be catastrophic. It creates a mechanism for a sudden, discontinuous crash in the bond market; a “Treasury Market Rupture” that would force the Fed to intervene immediately, ending the illusion of a free market in bonds.

### Net International Investment Position (NIIP) Collapse

The US Net International Investment Position (NIIP) tracks the difference between US assets owned by foreigners and foreign assets owned by US entities. It is the ultimate scorecard of a nation’s wealth relative to the world.

In the early 1980s, when George Soros wrote about the “Imperial Dollar,” the US NIIP was a **positive 10%** of GDP. The US was a creditor nation. Today, the US NIIP is **negative 65% to 85%** of GDP.

* **Meaning:** Foreigners own roughly **$63 trillion** of US assets gross, and $26 trillion net more than we own of theirs.
* **The Threat:** In a crisis where the US attempts to devalue or cap yields to save itself, this capital can flee. “That money can’t be in two places at once... It can own treasuries... or that capital can finance new factories... but it can’t do both”.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!88K4!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fa2e820ab-0f6b-4b48-a11c-8247e1654b4c_1404x790.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!88K4!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fa2e820ab-0f6b-4b48-a11c-8247e1654b4c_1404x790.png)

This massive overhang of foreign ownership is a Sword of Damocles (this is a Greek metaphor for the constant, imminent peril faced by those in positions of power, wealth, for those who aren’t ancient Greek aficionados).

## The Timeline – BC (Before Capping) vs. AD (After Decision)

Timing the apocalypse is notoriously difficult; the market can remain irrational longer than you can remain solvent. However, Gromen offers a useful heuristic framework to navigate the coming chaos: **BC (Before Capping)** and **AD (After Decision)**.

### The BC Era - Volatility and “The Death Spiral”

We are currently in the late stages of the BC era. This period is characterized by the US government and the Fed trying to “ride two horses with one rear end”. They are attempting to talk down inflation while quietly allowing liquidity to expand (via mechanisms like the Reverse Repo drain or Treasury General Account maneuvering) to prevent the system from seizing up.

However, the “Death Spiral” mechanic is now active. In a normal world, deflation (a recession, stock market crash) makes bonds attractive (Safe Haven). Investors sell stocks and buy bonds, lowering yields. In the current US fiscal setup, deflation is fatal.

### Year Zero - 2026

Gromen predicts that **2026** will likely be “Year Zero”.

* **The Catalyst:** The conflicting forces of AI capital expenditure demands, massive debt refinancing walls, and the physical grid bottleneck will likely come to a head.

* **The Action:** The Fed implements explicit or implicit **Yield Curve Control (YCC)**. They explicitly cap the 10-year yield (e.g., at 3% or 4%) and commit to printing infinite money to buy any bond that yields more than that.

## Criticism & The TSCS Opinion

We respect the “Mother of All Crises” thesis. It is seductive because it relies on the cold comfort of arithmetic. The debt is too high, the grid is too old, and the promises are too big. The math *seems* irrefutable.

However, in macro-investing, being “mathematically right” but “mechanically wrong” is the same thing as being broke. It is essential to see how this may be wrong.

While I agree with the diagnosis (the US is fiscally incontinent), I am skeptical with the prognosis (a binary choice between Hyperinflation or Default). The thesis suffers from a cognitive bias known as **Linear Extrapolation in a Complex Adaptive System.** It assumes the variables are static, but history shows that when a system faces an existential threat, the rules of the game almost always change.

Applying **First Principles**, here is where the logic could fail:

#### 1. The “True Interest” Sleight of Hand

Firstly, there’s the intimidating statistic of *“True Interest Expense is 96% of tax receipts.”* **The First Principle of Sovereignty:** *Not all liabilities are created equal.*

To get to that 96% figure, you have to combine **Bond Interest** (contractual law) with **Entitlements** (political law). It’s clearly assumed that these are identical obligations. They are not.

> **Bond Coupons:** If you stop paying these, the global financial system collapses.
>
> **Entitlements (Social Security/Medicare):** If you slash these, you get riots, but the banking system remains solvent.

When pushed against the wall, a Sovereign will **always** break its political promises to save its financial existence. The US government will means-test Social Security, raise the retirement age to 75, and cap Medicare prices long before they voluntarily incinerate the Treasury market. The “96%” is a roadmap for future austerity, it doesn’t HAVE to be a death sentence.

#### 2. The Productivity Denominator (The AI Counter-Force)

Is AI is purely an inflationary energy sink (”Physics constrains Finance”)?

This focuses entirely on the **Input** (Cost of Energy) and ignores the **Output** (Productivity).

The “Debt-to-GDP” ratio has a denominator: GDP. If the AI “Industrial Revolution” is real, it allows the economy to produce vastly more wealth per unit of labor/energy (but look, this is one of the most debated ideas in finance this year, we don’t actually know if AI will deliver the productivity everyone is hopeful for).

If we want to be objective, it must be noted that Gromen dismisses AI as just “consumer apps,” if AI optimizes the grid, collapses the cost of white-collar labor, and accelerates material science, we could see real GDP growth jump to 4-5%. If the economy grows faster than the debt pile due to radical efficiency, the debt becomes manageable without hyperinflation.

#### 3. The “Dirty Shirt” Theory of Relativity

*“If foreigners stop buying US Treasuries, who finances the US government’s deficit?”*

Money flows to the location of highest institutional trust and security.

We must ask ourselves, can investors simply “leave” the dollar? Leave for what?

> **China?** A nation facing a demographic collapse faster than the West and a property crisis larger than 2008 (again, debatable).
>
> **Europe?** An energy-poor museum relying on imported security ([Doomberg](https://open.substack.com/users/35017257-doomberg?utm_source=mentions) would probably agree).
>
> **Gold?** You cannot collateralize the $100 trillion global repo market with physical gold.

The US is exporting dollars AND security. In a chaotic world, capital doesn’t flee the US, it flees *to* the US. The bond market might not be “vaporized” because the US is the “cleanest dirty shirt” in the laundry basket.
