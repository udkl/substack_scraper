In our last letter, we invoked Benjamin Graham’s famous quote of Mr. Market. We noted that with our holding in Just Group Plc, Mr. Market was “in one of his moods,” marking down the price of a business that was, by all objective measures, executing its strategy flawlessly. We counseled patience, trusting that the short-term “voting machine” of the market would eventually give way to the long-term “weighing machine” of fundamental value.

We did not expect the weighing machine to render its verdict so quickly, or so decisively.

On July 31, 2025, **Bermuda based Brookfield Wealth Solutions** (BWS) announced a recommended all cash offer to acquire Just Group for 220 pence per share. The offer, which valued the company at approximately **£2.4 billion**, represented a premium of roughly 75% to the prior day's closing price. In a single session, our largest detractor (which was down 10.05% at our last update) became our single greatest contributor, delivering a total gain of **43.34% since inception**.

This single event was the primary driver of our portfolio's exceptional performance over the past two weeks (and why we have done such an early update). It is a powerful, real-world lesson in the strategy we preach: buy good businesses at prices you find attractive and wait. The waiting is the hardest part. But when a sophisticated, strategic buyer validates your thesis by acquiring the entire company at a massive premium, it is also the most rewarding. This was not luck. This was the inevitable proof of intrinsic value over poor market sentiment. Our thesis has spoken, and the judgment was overwhelmingly in our favor.

### **Our Just Group Thesis Validated**

To fully appreciate the significance of the Just Group acquisition, it is worth revisiting our argument from last month. We stated that the company was "**executing flawlessly**, announcing multiple wins in the UK pension de-risking market," yet the stock languished. People were focused on noise; we were focused on the fundamental health of the business.

The buyer, Brookfield Wealth Solutions, is not a flighty hedge fund. It is the wealth solutions division of Brookfield Corporation, one of the world's premier alternative asset managers. BWS's rationale for the acquisition reads like a point by point affirmation of our own thesis. BWS recently launched a UK insurance subsidiary, Blumont, and explicitly described the Just acquisition as "transformational" for its ambition to become a leader in the UK's massive and growing pension risk transfer market, a sector with over **£1 trillion in assets**.

This was not a financial buyer picking up a statistically cheap stock. It was a strategic buyer acquiring a platform for UK dominance. BWS saw what we saw: a high quality, market leading franchise with a strong brand, whose value was not being reflected in its public market quotation. The acquisition allows BWS to combine its formidable balance sheet with Just's established capabilities, creating a powerhouse in the UK annuity and life insurance space. The market's sentiment was negative for months, but the quality of the business (its cash flows, its market position, and its strategic value to a global player) was finally and decisively recognized. This is the very essence of our investment philosophy in action.

The Just Group saga was the headline event, but important developments occurred across our portfolio, illustrating the various ways our theses are playing out.

### **Positive Catalysts Realized**

**Weatherford International ($WFRD.NASDAQ)**

In our last update, we noted we were monitoring Weatherford ahead of its July 23 earnings report. The results were a success. The company reported second quarter earnings per share (EPS) of $1.87, obliterating the consensus forecast of $0.98—a **positive surprise of over 90%.** Revenue also came in ahead of expectations at $1.2 billion. The market reacted accordingly, and our position is now up 4.20% since inception.

While management offered a prudent and cautious outlook for the broader market, their operational execution was undeniable. The company generated $79 million in adjusted free cash flow and highlighted significant new contracts with supermajors like BP and Shell. This demonstrates a key part of our thesis: a resilient, well managed business that can execute and generate cash even in a challenging macro environment. The earnings beat is the signal; the market noise is the distraction.

**Alamos Gold ($AGI.TSE)**

We previously argued that the market was wrongly treating Alamos as a simple proxy for the daily fluctuations of gold, ignoring the quantifiable value being created at its Island Gold asset in Ontario. Since then, the stock has recovered from a slight loss to a 5.35% gain. On July 30, Alamos reported a strong second quarter, with production increasing 10% and all-in sustaining costs (AISC) falling 18% compared to the first quarter. The company generated record revenue and a robust $84.6 million in free cash flow.

Admittedly, the company did raise its full year cost guidance, citing external factors like higher royalty expenses linked to the stronger gold price. In the past, many people focused on this negative headline. However, on August 6, the company followed up by filing the official, 43-101 compliant technical report for the Island Gold District Base Case. This report substantiates the multi billion dollar net present value and long life, low-cost production profile we highlighted previously. By providing a concrete, audited document, Alamos gave long-term investors an anchor for their valuation, allowing them to look past the short-term cost revision.

### **The Quiet Compounders**

**Anicom Holdings ($8715.TYO)**

Japan's leading pet insurer continues its steady climb, now up **12.61%** since inception. The most compelling development has been the "smart money" echo validating our position. In our last update, we noted that telecom giant KDDI Corp. had taken a significant stake. Since then, the chorus of sophisticated buyers has grown louder. On July 29, Dalton Investments, a respected value oriented fund, reported increasing its stake to 6.31%. Just two days later, on July 31, Hikari Telecommunication reported that it had boosted its ownership to 10.48%.

It is one thing for us to believe a company is undervalued. It is quite another for three separate, sophisticated corporate and institutional investors to independently reach the same conclusion and deploy **significant capital**. This pattern of accumulation provides a powerful external endorsement of our thesis.

**AcadeMedia ($ACAD.STO)**

A model of stability, our Swedish education provider is holding its ground with a 7.16% gain. The preliminary fourth quarter results, released just before our last update, showed stable 5% sales growth and a 12% increase in adjusted operating profit. The company is performing its role in the portfolio perfectly: providing steady, non-correlated compounding from a defensive sector. We will be monitoring the full year end report, scheduled for August 28, for further details on margins and the execution of its international growth strategy.

**Perpetua Resources ($PPTA.TSE)**

Perpetua continues to show strength, now up **33.47%**. There has been no major company specific news since the transformative financing package we detailed last month. The stock's continued positive performance reflects the market continuing to digest the profound importance of that event. By securing the full financing for its Stibnite Gold Project, Perpetua eliminated the single largest risk to the investment case. The company is now being valued less on its financing risk and more on the immense strategic value of its future production of gold and antimony, the latter of which is a critical material for national defense with no current domestic mined source in the United States.

### **Conviction in the Face of Volatility**

**Lectra ($LSS.EURONEXT)**

Lectra, our French technology leader, has become our new "frustrating" holding, pulling back to a 5.07% gain. This presents a perfect opportunity to demonstrate our long-term conviction. The reason for the weakness is clear: in its first half results released on July 24, management stated that its "Annual objectives are no more relevant, in the absence of visibility".

Markets hate uncertainty, and withdrawing guidance is a cardinal sin in the eyes of short-term traders. The stock has been punished for this. However, this is short-term. Our thesis is not based on Lectra hitting its 2025 guidance; it is based on its multi year strategic change to a higher margin, more predictable Software as a Service **(SaaS) business model**. The key metric for this transition, Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR), continued to grow at a healthy 6% on a like for like basis, and the company's balance sheet remains very strong. This situation is creating an opportunity for patient investors to acquire more of a fine business at a more attractive price.

**Vetoquinol ($VETO.EURONEXT)**

After a strong run, our French animal health leader has pulled back to a 7.90% gain. The news flow has been quiet, and the stock is in a holding pattern ahead of its first half 2025 results, scheduled for September 11. The market is waiting for the next data point to confirm that the temporary US supplier issues are fully in the past. We remain confident in this family controlled, cash rich business and its portfolio of essential products.
