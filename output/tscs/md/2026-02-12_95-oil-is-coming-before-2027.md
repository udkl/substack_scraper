# **The Arithmetic of Scarcity**

Rick Rule is a 73-year-old billionaire who has spent 50 years in the natural resources sector. He made fortunes in uranium (twice), gold, and silver by applying a simple principle: buy what is hated, hold until it is loved.

His current thesis on oil and gas is not complicated. It is arithmetic.

**Fact 1: Fossil fuels still power 81% of global energy consumption.**

Despite $6-11 trillion in alternative energy investment over the past 40 years, the market share of fossil fuels has declined from 83% to 81%. That’s a 2% reduction. At this rate, the “energy transition” will be complete sometime around the year 2850.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!FYaf!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F1a50e112-36d2-4139-840b-12fa71836f3a_1405x786.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!FYaf!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F1a50e112-36d2-4139-840b-12fa71836f3a_1405x786.png)

The International Energy Agency, long the cheerleader for peak oil demand, has quietly revised its outlook. Under the “Current Policies Scenario” reintroduced in November 2025, oil demand rises to 113 million barrels per day by 2050, with no peak in sight.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!HBaf!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F22d77c54-ca3f-4a69-9f22-5de4adc6357c_1405x779.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!HBaf!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F22d77c54-ca3f-4a69-9f22-5de4adc6357c_1405x779.png)

**Fact 2: The US shale miracle is running out of miracle.**

At $60 oil with current interest rates, approximately 60-70% of Tier-1 drilling locations in the Permian and Eagle Ford have already been drilled. The “sweet spots” are gone. What remains is Tier-2 and Tier-3 acreage, where wells produce less, decline faster, and frequently lose money.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!IIM4!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F8683dcfb-a57a-43a5-97a6-31b566952839_1405x708.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!IIM4!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F8683dcfb-a57a-43a5-97a6-31b566952839_1405x708.png)

Enverus Intelligence Research projects that marginal breakeven costs will rise from ~$70/barrel today to $95/barrel by the mid-2030s. The industry isn’t dying. It’s just getting expensive.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Admi!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F927a3f1e-f179-4f52-83a1-52ff66501a32_1405x782.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Admi!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F927a3f1e-f179-4f52-83a1-52ff66501a32_1405x782.png)

*Note: Rule’s original claim of 85% Tier-1 depletion appears aggressive. Industry data from Wood Mackenzie and Enverus suggests 60-70% at current prices. The directional thesis holds, but the timeline may be longer than Rule implies.*

**Fact 3: The industry is cannibalising itself.**

Global upstream investment is running at approximately $570-600 billion annually. The International Energy Forum estimates that $640-738 billion is required by 2030 just to maintain current production levels. That’s an annual deficit of $60-140 billion.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!2Elh!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fab35d700-45bb-42ef-a216-7c73af9857a7_1405x789.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!2Elh!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fab35d700-45bb-42ef-a216-7c73af9857a7_1405x789.png)

Nearly 90% of upstream investment is now dedicated to offsetting production declines rather than meeting demand growth. The industry is running as fast as it can just to stay in place.

This is the Red Queen Effect applied to hydrocarbons.

# **Double-Entry Bookkeeping**

Rule’s framework is elegant because it relies on accounting identities, not opinions.

**The Net Present Value Illusion**

When Wall Street analysts value oil companies, they use discounted cash flow models. These models require an assumption about future oil demand. Most analysts are using peak demand assumptions circa 2030, per the IEA’s “Stated Policies” scenario.

Here’s the problem: if you discount cash flows at 8% and assume demand peaks in 2030, the value of production after 2037 approaches zero. You’ve effectively written off the “tail” of the asset.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!cS07!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fa1961d40-2184-410e-a9ec-84e97abb4b95_1405x788.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!cS07!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fa1961d40-2184-410e-a9ec-84e97abb4b95_1405x788.png)

But what if demand doesn’t peak in 2030? What if the IEA’s revised “Current Policies” scenario is closer to reality?

Then the “tail” that analysts have written off to zero is worth... everything.

Rule argues that Exxon is trading at 70-75% of its net present value at $60 oil. If oil goes to $85-90, and the tail gets re-priced, you’re looking at a company worth 2-3x today’s price.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!GKOM!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Feb36776b-5bd9-403a-9b38-6074b3e8a2c9_1405x797.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!GKOM!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Feb36776b-5bd9-403a-9b38-6074b3e8a2c9_1405x797.png)

This is not a bet on oil going to $150. This is a bet on oil staying relevant.

**The Dividend Arbitrage**

Here’s where it gets interesting. Unlike uranium or silver, where Rule had to eat 4.5 years of negative carry waiting for the thesis to play out, oil and gas companies pay you to wait.

The Canadian producers Rule recommends yield approximately 6%. If you’re using an 8% cost of capital, that 6% dividend advertises away most of your time value of money discount.

In other words: you get paid while the world figures out it needs what you own.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!9Ak5!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F4b4afb77-8f7c-420f-a410-6a8d588d8b9e_1405x779.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!9Ak5!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F4b4afb77-8f7c-420f-a410-6a8d588d8b9e_1405x779.png)

# **Shale Decline Math**

Here’s the secret the shale industry hopes you never learn: the average shale well loses 74% of its production in the first year.

Compare that to conventional Middle Eastern wells, which decline at 2-3% annually. Shale is a treadmill. You have to keep drilling just to stay flat.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!3tVM!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fca37c56a-e2dc-4864-a76d-fa04179624e5_1405x789.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!3tVM!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fca37c56a-e2dc-4864-a76d-fa04179624e5_1405x789.png)

At current drilling rates, with 60-70% of Tier-1 locations already drilled, the US shale machine is consuming its seed corn.

But the decline rates are only half the story. The other half is what happens when you run out of wells to complete.

# **DUC Depletion Math**

This is the section that matters. Everything above is thesis. This is arithmetic.

Drilled-but-uncompleted wells (DUCs) represent the shale industry’s strategic buffer, the inventory of wells that can be rapidly brought online without waiting for new drilling. Think of it as the industry’s savings account. When times are tough, you draw down savings. When times are good, you rebuild them.

The savings account is empty.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!v3cI!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F615be405-882d-49d9-9cce-20ac23d6a0dd_1405x783.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!v3cI!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F615be405-882d-49d9-9cce-20ac23d6a0dd_1405x783.png)

DUC inventory in the three primary oil-producing basins has collapsed by approximately 79% from the June 2020 peak. This is not a forecast. It is observed data from the EIA’s Drilling Productivity Report, supplemented by Kpler and Enverus analysis through late 2025.

At current activity levels, the Permian alone completes approximately 450-480 wells per month while drilling 430-450 new wells. The net effect: a steady drawdown of 20-30 DUCs monthly.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!TuTo!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fbe634dce-040b-443e-880b-a66a3b66762a_1405x783.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!TuTo!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fbe634dce-040b-443e-880b-a66a3b66762a_1405x783.png)

Here’s the critical point most analysts miss: the “normal” operational minimum for DUC inventory is approximately 2-3 months of completions. In the Permian, that translates to roughly 900-1,400 wells. This is the bare minimum needed for scheduling flexibility, equipment staging, and operational continuity. It’s not a luxury buffer. It’s operational necessity.

Current Permian DUC inventory: approximately 700 wells.

The buffer is not depleted. It is *below* the buffer.

### **Production Consequence**

When DUC inventory is healthy, production tracks completions with a 2-3 month lag. When DUCs are exhausted, production tracks drilling with a 6-9 month lag. The “cushion” that allowed operators to surge production without adding rigs is gone.

Operators are currently completing more wells than they are drilling. Production is being maintained by drawing down inventory that is already below operational minimum. This is not sustainable. It is arithmetic.

### **Decline Rate Overlay**

The average Permian shale well declines 70-75% in its first year. To maintain current Permian production of approximately 6.2 mb/d, operators must add roughly 4.3-4.6 mb/d of new production annually just to offset declines.

At current completion rates (approximately 475 wells/month at ~1.0 kbd initial production), new well production adds roughly 5.7 mb/d annually.

The math works. Barely. At current activity levels.

But current activity levels are falling. The Permian rig count has declined from approximately 300 at the start of 2025 to 244 as of January 2026. Every rig removed reduces drilling capacity by roughly 17 wells/month. Every well not drilled today is production that does not exist 6-9 months from now.

### **Shale Productivity Gains**

Average lateral lengths in the Permian have increased from approximately 7,000 feet in 2018 to over 11,000 feet in 2024. Permian well productivity (first-year production per well) has increased approximately 40% over five years despite Tier-1 depletion. Each rig now drills roughly 1.5 wells/month versus 1.3 wells/month in 2020.

*The bear case:* AI-optimised drilling, longer laterals, and better completion techniques continue extending the Tier-1 runway. Wood Mackenzie’s “aggressive technology” scenario shows breakevens falling $5-8/bbl through 2030.

*The honest rebuttal:* Productivity gains are decelerating. The easy improvements are done. Longer laterals require more capital per well, more proppant, more water, and more time. The 40% productivity gain over five years came from compounding roughly 7% annual improvements. Recent data suggests this has slowed to 3-4% annually. At that rate, productivity gains offset perhaps half of Tier-1 depletion, not all of it.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!B38C!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fabe53b1c-17f7-41e5-aff0-333d68430cca_1405x771.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!B38C!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fabe53b1c-17f7-41e5-aff0-333d68430cca_1405x771.png)

### **The Quality Filter**

Not all oil companies are created equal. Rule’s framework distinguishes between:

*Companies to Own:*

* Making required sustaining capital investments
* Maintaining production capacity for the eventual upturn
* Fortress balance sheets that survive the trough
* Management teams with track records of capital discipline

*Companies to Avoid:*

* “Cannibalising” future production to pay current dividends
* Depleting reserves without replacement
* Levered balance sheets vulnerable to prolonged low prices
* Management teams chasing short-term shareholder returns

The market is currently rewarding the cannibalisers (high dividends, stock buybacks) and punishing the investors (sustaining capex). This is precisely backwards if Rule’s thesis is correct.
