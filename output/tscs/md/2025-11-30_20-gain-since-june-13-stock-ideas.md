## To Our First 1,000 Partners: A Note on Objectivity

“The stock market is designed to transfer money from the Active to the Patient.”  
— Warren Buffett

We’ve been thinking about objectivity a lot lately.

It’s a fundamental pillar in not only our investment process, but daily thinking.

When you actively pursue objectivity, you realise how irrational the majority of your decisions are.

In the arena of investment, the most formidable opponent is often the one staring back from the mirror.

Our minds are wired for narrative, for quick judgments, for emotional conviction. These are survival traits, but in the world of capital allocation, they become liabilities.

The greatest danger is not a market crash or a bad quarterly report; it is the quiet, internal process of bending reality to fit a story we desperately want to believe.

Confronting this internal adversary is the first, most critical step toward a sound investment philosophy. It requires accepting an uncomfortable truth: our default settings are not suited for this work. We must actively build a defense against our own impulses.

The antidote is not superior intellect, but superior process.

It is the construction of a framework, a mental scaffolding, that forces us to confront our own assumptions, question our conviction, and remain tethered to facts when emotion threatens to take over. This framework acts as a shield against the biases that cloud judgment.

But a framework built on arbitrary rules is a trap.

A simple metric like "P/E ratio below 10" can be a siren song, luring an investor into a cyclical peak just before a collapse.

A truly effective process cannot be a blunt instrument.

It must be nuanced, adapting to the unique contours of each business, each industry, each competitive landscape, just as a master craftsman uses a different tool for a different task.

“Without models from multiple disciplines, you will fail in business and in life.”  
— Charlie Munger

Over time, as you use checklists more, they’ll become less laborious, they’ll become a part of your cognitive apparatus.

This relentless pursuit of objectivity and a disciplined, repeatable process is the bedrock of The Small Cap Strategist.
