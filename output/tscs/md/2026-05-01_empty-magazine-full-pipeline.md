## **Caveats**

Framework production targets that anchor the headline math (Tomahawk 1,000+ per year, AMRAAM 1,900+ per year, SM-6 500+ per year) are RTX press release figures from February 4, 2026. They are not contractually committed, not in the $271 billion backlog, and not in any DoD primary document I could locate. RTX’s spokesperson declined to comment to Breaking Defense on current annual production levels or contract definitization timing. Sell-side modeling that implies the dollar figures are independently DoD-attested overstates source quality.

The sell-side framing referenced in the introduction is anchored on secondary reporting. The $7 to $8 billion headline figure and the “underappreciated” framing from Morgan Stanley’s April 22 note are consistent across multiple financial news summaries and confirmed via Bloomberg News quotation. The underlying Wolfe Research February 8 note was sourced from descriptions on Investing.com, Yahoo Finance, and Insider Monkey rather than the primary note; the methodology is not verified. The magnitude is what matters for the thesis and the magnitude is not in dispute across sources, but I am not representing direct access to the sell-side research.

The PAC-3 revision pattern referenced as the analog for consensus model extension is directional. The exact magnitude and timing of the LMT FY26 and FY27 EPS revisions following framework conversion in early to mid-2026 should be charted from Bloomberg before publication; the directional pattern is what the trade depends on.

The supplier timeline analysis is grounded in publicly disclosed data: DPA awards, capacity expansion announcements, groundbreaking dates, qualification milestones. Williams International is private and its production capacity, current output rates, and Module 8 completion timing are not independently verifiable beyond the trade press and DoD press release record. Public records that would tighten the timeline meaningfully (Okaloosa County permits, USA Spending obligations, DPA Title III congressional reporting) are available but not pulled at time of writing. Holston AAP is government-owned and its expansion timeline is similarly tied to DoD disclosure rather than independent audit.

The SM-3 sustainment EPS contribution is built from publicly disclosed IDIQ ceiling figures and assumptions about utilization rate, margin, and recognition timing. The $0.11 to $0.15 per year EPS contribution is a directional estimate. RTX does not break out sustainment versus new-build margin in segment reporting, so the margin assumption is anchored on comparable structures across LMT and NOC sustainment work. Actual contribution may run higher or lower depending on task order timing and mix.

The L3Harris Missile Solutions IPO valuation framework uses EBITDA figures L3Harris has disclosed publicly but that have not been audited in a public S-1. The $620 million EBITDA figure may change when the public S-1 drops. Reuters reported on April 30, 2026 that L3Harris had confidentially submitted a draft registration statement, which is the standard procedural precursor to the public filing. Pentagon convertible-preferred terms have not been publicly detailed beyond the $1 billion headline. The trade is not actionable until the public S-1.

Operation Epic Fury munition expenditure figures (~850 Tomahawks of approximately 3,100, 50% of THAAD inventory, 50% of Patriot interceptor stockpile) are from CSIS’s April 21, 2026 reporting. State Department and White House releases confirm timing but do not publish exact stockpile figures.
