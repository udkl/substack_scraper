The more interesting story lies not in the portfolio’s aggregate return, but in the divergence of its constituent parts. We have been fortunate to see strong performance from our investments in **Perpetua Resources** (+32.31%) and **Vetoquinol** (+14.61%). We have also seen frustrating, though not unexpected, declines in **Just Group** (-10.05%) and **Alamos Gold** (-2.76%). This divergence is not random noise, it’s a near-perfect illustration of the market’s character and the central challenge of our work.

Benjamin Graham famously introduced the parable of "Mr. Market," an obliging business partner who offers to buy your shares or sell you his at a daily fluctuating price. Graham advised us to use Mr. Market’s mood swings to our advantage but never to be influenced by them. He wrote,

“In the short run, the market is a voting machine, but in the long run, it is a weighing machine.”

In our brief time together, we have already seen both of Mr. Market’s personalities. In the cases of Perpetua and Vetoquinol, he has been rational. He has seen a mining project significantly de-risked by a successful financing and an animal health company’s temporary operational issues fade into the past. He has “voted” for these stocks, and their prices have risen accordingly. In time, we expect the “weighing machine” will find them to be even more substantial.

But with Just Group and Alamos Gold, Mr. Market has been in one of his moods. He has seen Just Group announce a steady stream of new, profitable business, validating its strategy, and has responded by marking the price *down*. He was handed a detailed, 20-year mine plan from Alamos Gold that quantified immense future value, and he shrugged, choosing instead to worry about the daily price of gold bullion. Here, the voting machine is clearly broken.

This is the disconnect we seek to exploit. Our work is to buy good businesses at prices we find attractive and wait. The waiting is the hardest part. It requires the patience to sit still while Mr. Market has his tantrum, trusting that the weighing machine will eventually prevail. The recent price action in our laggards has not weakened our conviction; on the contrary, the opportunity to acquire more of these fine businesses at even lower prices has only increased their appeal. The fundamental health of these companies has improved, while their stock prices have declined. This is the very definition of an attractive investment.

### **Portfolio Discussion**

Our two best performers validate our thesis that great small-cap businesses can be found anywhere if one is willing to look.

**Perpetua Resources ($PPTA.TSE)** and **Vetoquinol ($VETO.EURONEXT)** have performed well because their underlying business realities improved. For Perpetua, the successful and oversubscribed raising of US$474 million in capital was a transformative event. For Vetoquinol, the story is one of quiet confidence. The absence of further negative news regarding the US supplier issue that had previously depressed the stock is allowing the company’s durable qualities (a strong balance sheet, a powerful portfolio of essential products, and family control) to shine through again.

The frustration, and therefore the opportunity, lies with **Just Group ($JUST.LSE)** and **Alamos Gold ($AGI.TSE)**. Just Group continues to execute flawlessly, announcing multiple wins in the UK pension de-risking market. The business is performing, yet the stock languishes. Alamos Gold published a life-of-mine plan for its Island Gold asset that can only be described as exceptional, outlining a top-tier Canadian mine with a multi-billion dollar net present value. The market completely ignored it, choosing to trade the stock as a simple proxy for the gold price. In both cases, the gap between the fundamental value of the business and the price quoted by Mr. Market has widened. We are content to wait for him to come to his senses.
