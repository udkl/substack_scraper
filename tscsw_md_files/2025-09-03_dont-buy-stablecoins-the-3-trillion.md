### Executive Summary

The original promise of cryptocurrency was clear: to create a financial system outside the control of governments and central banks and hedge against the debasement of fiat currencies like the U.S. dollar. Bitcoin and its successors were popularised by a deep distrust of a system that prints money at will, inflates away savings, and centralizes power in the hands of an unaccountable few. The government-regulated, dollar-pegged stablecoin is the ultimate corruption of this ideal. It adopts the *form* of crypto (using tokens, blockchains, digital wallets) while completely inverting its *function*. Instead of offering an escape from the dollar system, it secretly tethers you to it more tightly than ever before. This will transform your digital assets into a direct subsidy for the US Dollar.

This is no longer an accident, it is an engineered plan. We will try to dissect and expose this scam piece by piece. We will expose the motive for this grand deception: a national debt crisis so severe it threatens the foundations of the U.S. economy. We will talk about the mechanics of the GENIUS Act, a law bought and paid for by a lobbying machine of unprecedented scale. We will follow the money to the highest levels of power, there's so much corruption and self-dealing that makes a mockery of public service. And finally, we will explore the endgame scenarios of systemic collapse that the architects of this scheme are ignoring. The story of the GENIUS Act is the story of a heist.

### **No Solutions left: The Motive**

To understand why the U.S. government has resorted to such a desperate and deceptive financial gambit, one must first confront the terrifying reality of its balance sheet. The United States is on a collision course with insolvency, and the numbers are staggering. As of June 2025, the national debt has surged to an astronomical $36.2 trillion. The government is spending money it doesn't have at a breathtaking pace, with the fiscal year 2025 deficit hitting $1.3 trillion. This isn't a temporary problem to be solved by minor tweaks. The Congressional Budget Office (CBO) projects that federal debt will explode from 100% of GDP in 2025 to a record-shattering 156% of GDP by 2055, a level far beyond anything seen even in the aftermath of World War II.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!_3GT!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F293dd126-9dc7-43bc-a8d6-d767675797ab_1600x903.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!_3GT!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F293dd126-9dc7-43bc-a8d6-d767675797ab_1600x903.png)

The true crisis lies in the accelerating cost of servicing this mountain of debt. With rising interest rates, the bill for past profligacy is coming due. Net interest payments on the national debt are on track to break the $1 trillion mark annually, a figure that itself requires more borrowing to pay. The CBO projects that by 2055, interest costs alone will consume 5.4% of the entire U.S. economy, sparking the beginning of a vicious "debt spiral" where the nation must borrow ever-larger sums just to pay the interest on its old loans.

For decades, the U.S. has financed its deficits by selling Treasury bonds to the world. Foreign nations like China and Japan, along with global pension funds and central banks, reliably bought U.S. debt, keeping borrowing costs artificially low. But that era is ending. Lower confidence with the U.S. has caused foreign countries to de-dollarise their assets, and the appetite of foreign creditors for U.S. debt is disappearing. With the kind of painful spending cuts or tax hikes that would be required to balance the budget, the government faced a very scary question: Who will fund the deficit now?

The GENIUS Act is the answer to that question. It is strategic financial engineering designed to solve this. By creating a new, multi-trillion-dollar market and legally mandating that it hold U.S. government debt, Washington is creating a domestic buyer out of thin air. The scale of this new buyer is already large; in 2024, the largest stablecoin issuer, Tether, became the seventh-largest purchaser of U.S. Treasuries, with holdings now comparable to those of entire nations like Germany or Saudi Arabia.

The government can now "more reliably look to a committed market participant - stablecoin issuers - to lend it money."

It is insulating itself from the whims of foreign creditors and ensuring the deficit machine can keep running, for now.

### **"Stablecoin Solution": Engineering a Buyer**

The official sales pitch for the GENIUS Act is ‘genius’ in misdirection. Its proponents speak in terms of modernizing payments and protecting consumers. Deputy Treasury Secretary Michael Faulkender, one of the plan's key architects, let the mask slip when he admitted the goal was to create "trillions in additional demand" for U.S. treasury securities. Dante Disparte of Circle, a major stablecoin issuer, offers a more sanitized version, describing stablecoins as simply "a dollar uploaded onto the internet". This framing is a lie by omission.

It conveniently leaves out the most important detail: what, exactly, is backing that "dollar." **The answer is a mountain of government debt.**

The law's central provision is a strict reserve mandate, requiring every stablecoin to be backed one-to-one with "high-quality liquid assets". But the list of "permitted" assets is overwhelmingly dominated by U.S. government debt instruments: short-term Treasury bills, repurchase agreements backed by Treasury bills, and government money market funds. This provision legally compels the entire, fast-growing stablecoin industry, which is projected to be worth between $1.6 trillion and $2 trillion by 2030, to funnel its massive asset base directly into funding the U.S. government.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!BX6u!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Ff421864d-68a2-4bcb-bde2-3b6beb7df252_1834x1034.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!BX6u!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Ff421864d-68a2-4bcb-bde2-3b6beb7df252_1834x1034.png)

The most sinister provision, however, is the one that prohibits stablecoin issuers from paying any interest to their holders. This single rule exposes the entire "innovation" narrative as an act. As Representative Brad Sherman, a Harvard-trained CPA and one of the few lawmakers who understands the issue, has pointed out, **there is already a well-regulated, safe financial product that does exactly what stablecoins claim to do: money market funds**. These funds hold safe assets like Treasuries and pay their investors a healthy yield, around 4% in the current environment. While holders earn nothing, **issuers are making a fortune**. In its S-1 filing to go public, stablecoin issuer Circle reported earning a staggering $1.7 billion in revenue and reserve income in 2024 alone, citing the "favorable interest-rate environment" as a key source of profit.

> Why, then, would any "genius" choose to invest in a stablecoin that pays 0% interest while the issuer gets rich?

Sherman provides the answer: "hidden money". The only rational customer for a 0% yield financial product is someone who values its other attributes more than a return on their capital. For stablecoins, those attributes are speed, cross-border functionality, and a veneer of anonymity perfect for tax evasion, money laundering, and hiding assets. The government, in its desperation for funding, has created and legitimized a financial tool whose primary use case is facilitating illicit activity.

It is a devil's bargain: Washington will look the other way on financial crime in exchange for a captive market for its unpayable debt.

### **Endgame: Systemic Collapse**

Deputy Treasury Secretary Faulkender strangely claimed a mass redemption event poses no risk because Treasuries are "the most liquid asset in the world". This is a dangerously misleading half-truth. The liquidity of Treasuries is precisely what makes a catastrophic fire sale possible, and the GENIUS Act now has three doomsday scenarios.

#### **Scenario 1: The Run on the Digital Bank**

This is the most immediate danger. The history of finance is a string of bank runs, and digital banks are no exception. The collapse of the Terra/Luna "stablecoin" in 2022, which wiped out $40 billion in market value, was a warning. The system's supposed bedrock (the U.S. Treasury market) is itself subject to "concerning illiquidity," most shockingly during the COVID-19 crisis in March 2020 when it went to a virtual standstill it forced a multi-trillion-dollar Federal Reserve rescue. There is also a fundamental "temporal mismatch": stablecoins operate 24/7/365, but the liquidity needed is concentrated in New York and London trading hours, leaving the system highly exposed during nights and weekends. We have already seen a preview of this scenario. In March 2023, when Silicon Valley Bank was on the verge of collapse, its depositor Circle (issuer of the USDC stablecoin) faced a run that saw over $2 billion in redemptions in a single day.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!OYSX!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F3ef3c6bb-e192-4676-80fd-adf2e7e5188e_2085x1093.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!OYSX!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F3ef3c6bb-e192-4676-80fd-adf2e7e5188e_2085x1093.png)

When the next crisis of confidence hits (e.g. fraud, a market crash, or geopolitical risks) users will rush to redeem their stablecoins for real dollars. With the market projected to hold trillions of dollars in reserves, this will force issuers to dump their Treasury holdings onto the market at any price to meet withdrawal demands.

A May 2025 study by the Bank for International Settlements has already shown that stablecoin outflows have an asymmetric, destabilizing effect, causing Treasury yields to spike. **A mass fire sale of what is supposed to be the world's "risk-free" asset would instantly crash the bond market**, sending interest rates soaring and triggering a cascading collapse across the entire global financial system, a crisis that would make 2008 look like a minor correction. But, once the stablecoin market is sufficiently large and intertwined with the Treasury market, it becomes "too big to fail." Its collapse would be so catastrophic that the government would have no choice but to bail out the issuers, cementing the debt-monetization scheme in perpetuity.

#### **Scenario 2: Shrinking Real Economy**

Even if a sudden collapse is avoided, the long-term consequences are just as grim. By legally mandating that a multi-trillion-dollar industry must buy government debt, the GENIUS Act creates a capital sinkhole. Trillions of dollars that could have been invested in productive, wealth-creating private enterprises (funding new technologies, building factories, creating jobs) will instead be siphoned off to fund government consumption and service its ever-growing debt.

It is, as one analysis noted, "robbing private-sector Peter to pay public-sector Paul" on a national scale.

This misallocation of capital will starve the real economy of investment, leading to slower growth, lower productivity, and a long-term decline in American competitiveness. Worse, the scheme actively distorts the government's own fiscal policy. The need to support the stablecoin ecosystem "constrain[s] Treasury to favor short-term issuance over long-term bonds," forcing the government into a riskier, more volatile debt management strategy simply to produce the specific type of debt that stablecoin issuers prefer.

#### **Scenario 3: The State’s New Eyes**

The final, most insidious danger is the creation of the perfect tool for financial surveillance. Stablecoins are sold on the promise of "hidden money," but the GENIUS Act mandates that all issuers comply with Bank Secrecy Act and Anti-Money Laundering (AML/KYC) requirements. Every transaction is permanently recorded on an immutable public ledger. Once authorities link a person's identity to a wallet their entire financial history becomes known to the state. This is far more dangerous than an overt Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC). A CBDC would be an honest tool of state control. This stablecoin regime is dishonest and is the perfect infrastructure for total financial surveillance.

My article is an external critique of the direction system’s structure is heading toward. We don’t comment on the utility for the individual user, we comment on the implications for the state and the financial system.

For the clarity of the viewer, we will expand on my points where our perspectives may diverge.

**The 0% yield:** For the reader, the key question isn’t why crypto users should care about the yield. The question we tried to prompt is why the government would legally sanction a financial architecture where the issuer can capture billions in interest from the reserves while the holder gets 0%. The existence of high-yield money market funds proves it’s possible to pass this yield on. In our opinion, the GENIUS Act’s prohibition on paying interest is on purpose. It creates a massive incentive for issuers to grow as large as possible, which would take up the assets that must then be funneled into Treasuries.

**Illicit Finance:** Although there’s pure anonymity for certain crypto, this overlooks the issue of scale and liquidity. Laundering large sums of money requires a liquid market with reliable ties to the fiat system. So Tether for example would offer that (a 2024 UN report states that USDT has become a preferred vehicle for money launderers in Southeast Asia because of its speed). It should be seen in the perspective that the features are quite attractive for large-scale illicit finance (and in our opinion seems like the government has chose to tolerate it in exchange for creating a captive market for its debt).

**KYC:** To explain our narrative, we’d like to state the bigger picture: the regulatory perimeter. How does that money get into the stablecoin circulation in the first place? It is almost always through a centralized, KYC-compliant exchange like Coinbase. The GENIUS Act is meant to solidify this regulation at the issuer level (making it subject to Bank Secrecy Act). This creates, as we stated, a ‘perfect’ surveillance tool. So our point was that the main objective of this scenario would be to make a system that feels anonymous during transaction, but is fully transparent to the state once the wallet can be linked.

**So essentially, the core thesis stands: the utility of stable coins for users is the driver to let this scheme run for a different purpose at the state level. It allows the government to create a new and structural buyer for its debt, financed by interest-free capital. The fact that it’s useful for trading on the Polymarket doesn’t contradict this.**
