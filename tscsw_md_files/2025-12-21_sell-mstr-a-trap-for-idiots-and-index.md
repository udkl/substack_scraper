## **Destination Analysis and the Illusion of Compounding**

### **1. The Meaning of Destination Analysis**

“Destination Analysis” was consciously central to how businesses were analyzed.

The central question was never “What will the earnings be next quarter?” but rather “Where will this business be in ten or twenty years?” It was an attempt to understand a firm’s DNA.

When Nomad Partnership evaluated Amazon in the mid-2000s, the destination was clear: a business that could self-fund its growth, where barriers to entry increased with size, and where the “power law” of the internet allowed a mouse to turn into an elephant.

They sought “honestly run compounding machines”. The destination for Amazon was a reality where e-commerce grew from 3.1% of retail sales to a dominant plurality. The conviction required to hold Amazon when it could be bought for $35 came from understanding this destination, not from analyzing the volatility of the stock price.

### **2. MSTR: The Anti-Compounder**

Applying this same “Destination Analysis” to MSTR gives quite literally the opposite trajectory. The company is a financial construct growing through leverage.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Oc-n!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F45f081ac-5971-49fb-9a93-912d6b0ab773_1600x812.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Oc-n!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F45f081ac-5971-49fb-9a93-912d6b0ab773_1600x812.png)

* **Self-Funding Growth?** Charlie Munger prized businesses that could fund their own expansion. MSTR, conversely, relies on the capital markets to fund its asset accumulation. It issues convertible debt to buy Bitcoin. This is not “self-funding”; this is “market-dependent.”
* **Barriers to Entry?** Amazon’s moat widened as it grew because its logistics network became impossible to replicate. MSTR’s model has zero barriers to entry. Any entity, corporate or individual, can purchase Bitcoin. There is no proprietary process in hitting the “buy” button on a cryptocurrency exchange.
* **Scale Economics Shared?** Costco and Amazon passed savings to customers to drive loyalty. MSTR passes risk to shareholders to drive volatility.

The destination of MSTR is of a closed-end fund trading at a premium, structurally tethered to the mercy of debt covenants and index inclusion rules. If one looks ten years into the future, does one see a software giant? No. One sees a massive pile of digital assets encumbered by a massive pile of fiat debt, with the equity holders fighting over the residual “cigar butt”.

### **3. The Misunderstanding of Permanence**

We emphasize that equity is the only permanent capital in a company structure. Management leaves, assets depreciate, but the equity remains.

However, MSTR has inverted this relationship. By leveraging the balance sheet to the tits, they have made the equity *impermanent*. In a scenario of catastrophic asset drawdown, any “Black Swan” event, the equity can be wiped out by the priority of debt claims.

## **Risk: The Turkey Problem**

### **1. The Problem of Induction**

“The track record of economists in predicting events is monstrously bad... it is like medieval medicine”. - Nassim Taleb

This failure stems from the problem of induction, best illustrated by the parable of the Thanksgiving Turkey.

Consider the turkey. For 1,000 days, the farmer feeds it. Every single day, the turkey’s statistical model confirms that the farmer loves it. The confidence level is 99.9%. The “Alpha” is high; the “Beta” is low.

The turkey believes it has found a “compounding machine.” But on the 1,001st day, the Wednesday before Thanksgiving, something unexpected happens. The farmer does not bring corn; he brings a hatchet.

The critical insight is this: “Consider that the feeling of safety reached its maximum when the risk was at the highest!”.

The turkey felt safest on Tuesday night.

“Debt is a mistake between lender and borrower, and both should suffer.” - Nassim Taleb

“Abundance is harder for us to handle than scarcity.” - Nassim Taleb

## **Bureaucracy: MSCI and the GICS Reclassification**

### **1. The Mechanism of Indexation**

To understand the coming slaughter, one must look away from the narrative and toward the plumbing.

The Global Industry Classification Standard (GICS) is the taxonomy of the financial world. It determines where billions of dollars of passive capital flow. It is designed to be “Universal, Reliable, Flexible, and Evolving”.

However, evolution in bureaucracy is slow, until it is sudden.

MSCI, the gatekeeper of global capital flows, is currently conducting a consultation on the treatment of “Digital Asset Treasury Companies”.

### 2. The Consultation Paper (2025)

The specifics of this consultation are terrifying for the MSTR bull case.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!gF9e!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F941b3714-f228-4afd-9942-6ca608f7f05c_1600x903.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!gF9e!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F941b3714-f228-4afd-9942-6ca608f7f05c_1600x903.png)

* **The Criteria:** MSCI proposes to exclude companies whose primary business involves digital asset treasury activities if those holdings represent **50% or more of their total assets**.
* **The Target:** MSTR is the poster child for this definition. Its software business is now a dwarf standing on the shoulders of a Bitcoin giant.
* **The Timeline:**

  + Consultation Period Ends: **December 31, 2025**.
  + Final Decision Announced: **January 15, 2026**.

This is not a rumor.

It is a formal bureaucratic process. If MSTR is reclassified or excluded, it ceases to be a “software company” in the eyes of the index.

It becomes a “digital asset vehicle,” and crucially, it may be deemed ineligible for the *MSCI Global Investable Market Indexes* entirely.

### **3. The Foreign Inclusion Factor (FIF)**

The technical mechanism of the slaughter is the **Foreign Inclusion Factor (FIF)**.

**Definition:** MSCI defines the FIF as “the proportion of shares outstanding that are deemed to be available for purchase in the public equity markets by international investors”.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!R43T!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F3bfdaf2e-2f24-41d1-b110-b191e8895dc5_1600x726.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!R43T!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F3bfdaf2e-2f24-41d1-b110-b191e8895dc5_1600x726.png)

**Function:** The FIF drives the weight of a stock in the index. A stock with a FIF of 0.5 has half the weight of a fully free-float stock.

**The Trap:** If MSTR is designated as a “Digital Asset Treasury Company,” it falls into a category that may have “limited investability” for international institutional mandates. Many sovereign wealth funds and pension funds have strict mandates preventing them from holding “crypto assets.” If MSCI flags MSTR as a crypto proxy, the “Foreign Room” (the proportion of shares available to foreign investors) effectively collapses.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!DgIC!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F935d968e-98f6-4248-bcf3-3b0aa978cf79_1600x760.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!DgIC!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F935d968e-98f6-4248-bcf3-3b0aa978cf79_1600x760.png)

**Result:** The FIF could be adjusted downward, potentially to zero for the standard indices. When the FIF drops, the index funds *must* sell. They have no choice. They are not “investors” in the Nomad sense; they are automata following a code.

The Structured Product Anomaly: STRC and the Sale of Tail Risk

### **1. The Instrument**

In a move that would make the “financial engineers” of 2008 blush, Strategy has issued a “Variable Rate Series A Perpetual Stretch Preferred Stock” under the ticker STRC.

This instrument is a masterclass in what Taleb calls “selling tail risk” to the uninitiated.

### **2. The Yield Trap**

STRC offers a 10.50% annual dividend, paid monthly. In a yield-starved world, this attracts the “yield tourists”, investors looking for income who do not understand the underlying volatility.

It trades at ~$92.35, below its $100 par value, indicating that the credit market is already pricing in distress. A preferred stock trading at such a discount with such a high yield is not a “safe income stream”; it is “distressed debt” in drag.

### **3. The Correlation Anomaly**

The most damning piece of data regarding STRC is its correlation matrix:

* **Correlation to MSTR:** -35%.
* **Correlation to BTC:** -1%.

Why would the preferred stock of a Bitcoin holding company be negatively correlated to the company and the asset?

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!IR9l!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F9c504b34-f7aa-45d9-9681-e17e944829bc_1600x903.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!IR9l!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F9c504b34-f7aa-45d9-9681-e17e944829bc_1600x903.png)

This suggests that STRC is functioning as a short volatility instrument. When MSTR (the equity) rips higher (high volatility, risk-on), the preferred is ignored or sold off for the equity. When MSTR crashes (risk-off), the preferred might hold value momentarily due to the dividend promise, before collapsing when the credit risk becomes real.

By buying STRC, retail investors are essentially selling insurance to MSTR. They are collecting a premium (the dividend) in exchange for taking on the catastrophic risk of the entire structure imploding. As Taleb and Mark Spitznagel have argued, selling tail risk is a strategy that picks up pennies in front of a steamroller. The 10.5% yield is the penny; the index exclusion is the steamroller.

### **4. Structured Products and “The Greeks”**

In the structured products market, dealers effectively become “short long-dated equity index options”. MSTR, by issuing convertible debt and preferreds like STRC, is shorting its own volatility. It needs volatility to be low enough to service the debt, but the asset it holds (Bitcoin) is inherently volatile. This mismatch is the definition of fragility. They are trying to “cure debt crises with debt spending”.

### **5. The “Killing Fact”: A $730 Million Reality Check**

The most critical vulnerability in this structure is mathematical. A true compounder self-funds its growth.

On the Q3 2025 Earnings Call, management confirmed our claims from July: the company’s annualized interest and dividend obligations now total $689 million. Against this, the legacy software business is practically nonexistent. In the first nine months of 2025, core operating expenses ($277.4 million) ate gross profits, leading to an annualized operating loss of -$41.2 million.

On a cash basis, the picture is equally shit, with negative $45.6 million in Net Cash Used in Operating Activities.

The company must raise nearly three-quarters of a billion dollars every year just to keep the lights on and the creditors at bay, long before it should even consider purchasing a single satoshi of new Bitcoin (and if they do it just makes the issue worse). ### **6. The Ponzi Dynamic**

How does a company with a $730 million hole survive? By relying on the kindness of strangers, specifically, new equity investors.

In the first nine months of 2025 alone, MSTR raised $17.63 billion through the sale of common and preferred stock. Management has been refreshingly, if alarmingly, candid about this dependency. CEO Phong Le admitted that their strategy relies on the stock trading at a premium (mNAV > 1x) to fund operations via ATM issuances.

The “proven track record” of capital raising that CFO Andrew Kang boasts of is an embarrassing dependency, not a flex.

## **Lessons from Nomad’s Mistakes and the Cost of Anchoring**

They anchored on their original purchase price and failed to see that the destination of the business had changed. They sold too early (or in some cases, held the wrong thing) because they had a static view of the firm.

The mistake cost them $12 million at the time.

MSTR investors are committing a similar “anchoring” error. They are anchored to the “Saylor Narrative” of 2021, the visionary CEO who front-ran the Bitcoin adoption. They are failing to see that the facts have changed. The “mouse” did not become an “elephant”; it became a “piñata” for passive flows. The destination has shifted from “adoption” to “regulatory exclusion.”

### **2. The Conseco Warning**

Nomad also references Conseco, a financial services holding company that collapsed under the weight of its own leverage and poor acquisitions. The lesson? “What we learnt at Conseco may well have kept us out of the US banks” in 2008.

Conseco taught them that leverage disguises the deterioration of the underlying business. MSTR is the Conseco of the crypto age. The soaring Bitcoin price has disguised the fact that the core business is irrelevant and the structure is toxic.
