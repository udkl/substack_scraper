Amadeus Fire AG is a prominent German personnelservices and professional training provider. The company, with a market capitalizationof €411 million specializes in white-collar roles across accounting, finance, office, and information technology (IT). Its business is roughly split between personnel services (historically ~60% of revenues) and professional training (~40% of revenues). This focus on commercial and IT niches, which constitute about 14% of the total staffing market, has traditionally allowed Amadeus Fire to benefit from more favorable trends and economics compared to some generalist peers.

The company has an operational history of nearly four decades and serves a diversified client base from 22 locations across Germany; its largest customer accounts for only 1.4% of revenues, and no single industry contributes more than 10%. The training segment, significantly scaled through acquisitions over the past five years, is positioned as a strong complement to the more cyclical personnel services arm. Notably, while corporate and private customers make up 25% of training revenues, the remaining 75% stems from publicly funded training, which often exhibits counter-cyclical characteristics.

However, it now trades over 60% lower than it’s all time highs. This sharp derating is primarily attributed to the prevailing weakness in the German economy. The negative corporate business outlook and consequent hesitancy among companies to recruit new staff have heavily impacted Amadeus Fire’s personnel services business. In contrast, the training segment had, until recently, shown more resilience. Despite the challenging staffing environment, there's a belief that Amadeus Fire continues to gain market share, and the structural shortage of qualified labor in Germany is expected to persist. ### **Investment Overview**

#### **Strengths**

Amadeus Fire holds a leading position in its niche markets, specializing in white-collar roles within accounting, finance, office administration, and IT. These segments represent approximately 14% of the broader staffing market and have historically offered better trends and economic characteristics than many generalist staffing areas. This specialization allows for a more focused service offering and higher margins during favorable economic conditions.

A key strength is its diversified client base, with the largest customer contributing only 1.4% of revenues and no single industry accounting for more than 10%. This significantly mitigates concentration risk. The business model, combining personnel services (historically around 60% of revenue) with professional training (around 40%), offers a degree of balance. The training segment, notably expanded through acquisitions, includes a substantial portion (75% of its revenues) derived from public funding, which tends to be counter-cyclical. This structure should, in theory, provide a cushion during economic downturns. The company's nearly 40-year track record implies resilience and an established market presence.

There's also a belief that Amadeus Fire continues to capture market share, even within the current challenging staffing environment. Looking ahead, the company is forecasted internally to achieve a neutral net cash balance by the end of 2024, with expectations of solid cash generation over 2025. This is supported by external research forecasting positive free cash flow yields. Perhaps most importantly, the persistent structural shortage of qualified labor in Germany provides a powerful long-term demand driver for both staffing and training services.

#### **Weaknesses**

The personnel services segment is inherently cyclical and highly susceptible to economic downturns, a vulnerability clearly demonstrated by the current impact of Germany's weak economic performance. This has led to significant margin pressure, with the operating EBITA margin plummeting to a mere 4.4% in Q1 2025, a steep fall from 12.6% in Q1 2024. This compression stems from both lower revenue, which reduces operating leverage, and increased investments, particularly in IT infrastructure transformations. Productivity issues and high sickness rates were also cited as impacting results in 2023.We will touch base later in the conclusion but one must see this as a long-term play (3+ years), allowing for volatility.

The company's operational focus on Germany makes it highly sensitive to domestic economic conditions. Recent financial performance underscores these challenges: revenue declined by 1.2% in 2024, and operating EBITA fell by a substantial 21.1%. The first quarter of 2025 showed an acceleration of this negative trend, with year-over-year declines in sales by 14.5% and in operating EBITA by a staggering 70.2%.

Even the training segment, often touted for its counter-cyclical properties, is not immune. It experienced a 6.5% revenue decline and a 75.9% drop in EBITA in Q1 2025. This underperformance is partly due to specific, non-cyclical headwinds. For instance, Comcave, a key training subsidiary, has been negatively impacted by changes to the German government's training platform listing rules, which now restrict the number of course listings per provider, thereby reducing Comcave's visibility. This indicates that the training segment's resilience is currently being tested by operational and regulatory challenges.

#### **Opportunities**

A significant opportunity lies in a rebound of the German economy. Any recovery would likely provide a substantial boost to the personnel services segment. Recent data showed a slight improvement in the ifo Business Climate Index, offering a "glimmer of hope". The enduring structural demand for skilled labor in Germany remains a powerful tailwind, with estimates suggesting the country needs up to 400,000 new skilled foreign workers annually. This underpins long-term demand for both staffing and training services.

Amadeus Fire has avenues to expand its training services, venturing into adjacent areas like management and personal coaching, and offering English-language IT training across Europe, where AI-enabled translation could lower language barriers. The company also has a stated M&A strategy focused on acquiring training companies with revenues in the €10-15 million range or contributing €1-10 million in earnings, which could accelerate growth and diversification in this segment.

The increasing adoption of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and technology in recruitment presents another opportunity. Amadeus Fire is looking to leverage AI for personalized learning in its training programs and for enhancing operational efficiency. This proactive stance aligns with broader industry trends where AI is becoming essential for tasks like CV scanning and candidate matching. Furthermore, Germany's progressive Skilled Immigration Act reforms are making it easier for non-EU professionals to work in the country, expanding the talent pool and increasing demand for placement and training services. Finally, the company's currently depressed valuation could make it an attractive acquisition target itself.

#### **Threats**

The most immediate threat is a prolonged period of economic weakness in Germany, which would continue to depress demand for personnel services and impact training budgets. Competition in the German staffing market is intense. Amadeus Fire faces pressure from large global players like Randstad and Adecco, numerous specialized local firms, and from new tech-driven models, including gig economy platforms like Fiverr and Upwork for certain IT project roles. While Amadeus Fire has a niche focus, the definition of a "competitor" is evolving, particularly in the IT space.

Regulatory changes pose an ongoing risk. This could include alterations to labor laws, training subsidy programs, or data protection regulations, especially concerning the use of AI in recruitment, which the EU AI Act may classify as "high risk". The issues faced by Comcave due to changes in course listing rules serve as a stark example of platform and regulatory risk. Wage inflation and rising candidate expectations also present a challenge. With salary being a top priority for over 56% of German job seekers, Amadeus Fire could face margin pressure if it cannot pass on higher labor costs.

While M&A is an opportunity, poorly integrated acquisitions can become a drag on performance. Lastly, the failure to keep pace with technological advancements in AI and recruitment platforms could lead to a loss of market share, as competitors leverage these tools for greater efficiency.

#### **Competitive Analysis of the Company**

Amadeus Fire's sustainable competitive advantages, or its "economic moat," appear to be narrow but present.

* **Specialization:** The company possesses deep expertise in accounting, finance, office administration, and IT roles. This specialization allows for more precise candidate matching and a better understanding of client needs in these often complex fields.
* **Integrated Model (Staffing & Training):** The ability to both source and *train* candidates creates a unique value proposition. * **Counter-cyclical Training Element:** The significant portion of training revenue derived from public funding (75% of training revenues) is designed to offer resilience. However, this aspect is currently being tested by specific operational and platform-related challenges.

The economic moat is primarily built on intangible assets like brand reputation and specialized knowledge, alongside an efficient process stemming from the integrated model. It is less reliant on strong network effects or high switching costs for customers, which are typically associated with wider moats. The staffing industry is inherently competitive, and client and candidate loyalty can be transient. Technological shifts and economic cycles can erode existing advantages. The current performance challenges, particularly the sharp margin compression, indicate that this moat is not impenetrable. Therefore, a "narrow moat" assessment seems appropriate.

### **Key Trends & Challenges in German Recruitment**

Several macro trends contribute to the German recruitment landscape

* **Critical Talent Shortages & Skills Gaps:** Germany faces persistent shortages of skilled labor across numerous sectors, with particularly acute needs in IT, healthcare, and engineering. The economy is estimated to require up to 400,000 new skilled foreign workers annually to fill these gaps. This is a fundamental long-term demand driver for companies like Amadeus Fire.
* **Changing Candidate Expectations:** Today's candidates place a high value on competitive compensation (salary is the top factor for 56% of job seekers). There is also a strong preference for flexible and hybrid work arrangements (a decisive factor for nearly 45% of employees). Candidates also increasingly seek meaningful work, values alignment with their employer, and robust opportunities for career growth and development. Companies that invest in employee development see, on average, 34% higher retention rates.
* **AI Adoption in Recruitment:** AI and automation are rapidly moving from experimental to essential tools in recruitment. These technologies can significantly enhance efficiency in tasks like CV scanning, candidate matching, and initial screening via chatbots. An overwhelming 92% of HR leaders plan to increase their use of AI across HR processes by 2025. However, this trend also brings ethical considerations and regulatory scrutiny, with the EU AI Act expected to classify recruitment algorithms as "high risk," imposing standards to prevent discrimination and requiring human oversight.
* **New Labor Market Dynamics & Regulatory Shifts:** Recent reforms to Germany's Skilled Immigration Act are making it easier for non-EU professionals to work in the country, which will likely expand the talent pool. Remote and cross-border work arrangements are becoming more normalized. Discussions around pay transparency are gaining momentum, with an EU Pay Transparency Directive expected to push employers towards disclosing salary ranges and addressing pay gaps by 2026. There is also an increasing imperative for Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion (DEI) in hiring practices.
* **Need for Streamlined Hiring Processes:** Lengthy recruitment cycles (the average time to fill a position in Europe is around 44 days) can lead to losing top candidates to faster-moving competitors.

Amadeus Fire's strategic focus on specialization, its integrated training model, and its plans for AI adoption appear well-aligned to address these major industry trends and challenges. The critical factor will be effective execution.

The margin of safety, therefore, hinges critically on which 2025 outlook is more credible. If mwb research's more conservative 2025 forecasts (€402 million revenue, ~9.2% EBIT margin) prove more accurate, the upside is considerably more modest (around 22% to mwb's €97 target). The path to achieving the higher revenue and, particularly, the significantly improved margin implied by the optimistic scenario needs to become much clearer, especially given the severe margin compression observed in Q1 2025.

For this investment to perform poorly, several adverse conditions would need to materialize:

* A prolonged and deep recession in Germany could severely impact both corporate hiring and public/private training budgets for an extended period, well beyond 2025.
* The company could fail to improve its operating margins. The IT investments might not yield the expected productivity gains, or cost pressures could remain stubbornly high, leading the very low 4.4% Q1 2025 EBITA margin to become a persistent feature for several quarters.
* The challenges in the Training segment, such as the Comcave platform visibility issues or uncertainty around B2G funding, could worsen or remain unresolved, thereby undermining its intended counter-cyclical benefits and growth potential.
* Increased competition could erode Amadeus Fire's market share in its key niches, or the company might fail to adapt effectively to new recruitment technologies and evolving service delivery models.
* While less likely, the structural shortage of skilled labor in Germany could ease unexpectedly due to unforeseen demographic shifts or significant de-industrialization, thereby reducing long-term demand for staffing and training services.
* Poor execution of the M&A strategy in the training segment could lead to acquisitions that destroy value rather than create it.

#### **Conclusion**

Amadeus Fire AG stands as a well-established, specialized provider in the German personnel services and professional training landscape. The company is currently experiencing significant cyclical headwinds due to a weak German economy, compounded by some specific operational challenges that have severely impacted its recent financial performance, particularly its profitability.

The core investment thesis rests on the premise that Amadeus Fire is a fundamentally sound company whose shares have been heavily derated due to these current difficulties. This situation offers a considerable upside if the company can successfully steer through the present downturn and capitalize on powerful long-term structural tailwinds, most notably the persistent shortage of skilled labor in Germany. The integrated model of staffing and training, along with its niche specialization, provides a solid foundation.

However, the risks are undeniable. The sharp compression in operating margins is a serious concern and reflects the depth of the current challenges. The company's heavy dependence on a German economic recovery adds another layer of uncertainty.

The valuation presents a cloudy outlook. If management can achieve the optimistic 2025 targets for revenue (around €460 million) and operating margin (around 14%) then significant upside from the current share price exists. However, if the more conservative external forecasts prove to be more realistic, the upside is considerably more muted. We suggest that you keep Amadeus Fire on a close watchlist to see if the notable headwinds become less effective and if the tailwinds we provided in our research show more conviction.

The potential for Amadeus Fire to become an acquisition target, particularly at its currently depressed valuation, adds an interesting, albeit speculative, dimension to the investment case.

In essence, Amadeus Fire AG presents a classic "fallen angel" scenario. For investors with a strong conviction in a German economic rebound and in management's ability to restore margins, leverage its niche strengths, and realize the synergies within its training division, the current valuation could offer a compelling entry point. However, the Q1 2025 results indicate it will likely be more volatile. We will update this company in due time with a relooked recommendation. That’s it. For now.
