## **Investment Thesis: A Durable Business at a Cyclical Price**

The investment case for Kitwave rests on a simple but powerful premise: a high-quality, resilient business with a proven growth formula is trading at a valuation that implies a permanent impairment of its prospects.

This dislocation has been caused by a perfect storm of temporary headwinds, creating an opportunity to purchase a long-term compounder at a cyclical low.

### **Strengths**

The foundation of the investment thesis is the undeniable quality of the underlying business, forged over decades of disciplined execution.

This is not a turnaround story but a story of a proven winner that has temporarily stumbled.

First and foremost, Kitwave possesses an extraordinary track record of value creation. Over the last 15 years, the company has delivered an impressive 14% in annualized revenue growth.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!JPZz!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F0ec008bf-c233-4f5f-8962-cbb07f08a190_1705x954.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!JPZz!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F0ec008bf-c233-4f5f-8962-cbb07f08a190_1705x954.png)

This growth is not the result of a single lucky break but the product of a repeatable, dual-engine model: a steady 3-4% in annual organic growth, supplemented by a highly effective "buy-and-build" strategy that has contributed approximately 10% annually through acquisitions.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!evvr!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F496bb63b-8d1c-458a-b6da-11d38cf8ea2c_1863x1041.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!evvr!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F496bb63b-8d1c-458a-b6da-11d38cf8ea2c_1863x1041.png)

This long history demonstrates a core competency in both operating its existing assets and integrating new ones.

This growth is built upon a bedrock of operational excellence.

For Kitwave's core customer base of independent retailers and foodservice operators, reliability is paramount. The company's ability to consistently achieve over 98% on-time, in-full service levels is a critical competitive differentiator.

This commitment to service excellence fosters deep customer loyalty and creates a sticky revenue base.

Furthermore, Kitwave's business model is inherently resilient due to its sharp focus on a specific market niche. The company serves over 46,000 "mom & pop" convenience stores and independent restaurants; a customer segment that is too fragmented, requires too small an order size, and demands too high a service frequency for the industry's giants to address profitably.

This strategic positioning insulates Kitwave from direct competition with the largest players in the market.

Finally, the business model is highly cash-generative. In fiscal year 2024, the company achieved a pre-tax operational cash conversion of 90%, a testament to its efficient working capital management. This robust cash flow, which equates to a free cash flow yield of over 7% of the current market capitalization, provides the fuel for its dividend, operational investments, and, most importantly, its value-accretive acquisition strategy.

### **Weaknesses**

A credible investment case must also acknowledge the inherent risks and weaknesses of the business.

Kitwave operates in the wholesale distribution industry, which is characterized by structurally low margins. While the company has done an admirable job of expanding its gross margin over time (reaching 22.3% in FY24 and 22.6% in the first half of FY25) it remains a business of tight cost control and operational efficiency.

There is limited room for error in execution.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!6oso!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F03b6554a-5b0b-4424-89e1-daad5761ab39_1750x981.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!6oso!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F03b6554a-5b0b-4424-89e1-daad5761ab39_1750x981.png)

The July profit warning that triggered the share price decline highlighted some of these execution risks.

The sharp, greater-than-20% decline in Kitwave's share price was a direct reaction to the company's updated guidance for fiscal year 2025. The market, in its typical short-sighted fashion, interpreted this revision as a fundamental breakdown of the business model. However, a closer examination reveals that the downgrade was caused by a "perfect storm" of largely temporary factors (weaker consumer confidence, depot transition costs, national insurance hike, acquisition integration delays).

The company faced cost overruns related to the transition to a new depot in the South West and delays in realizing the anticipated benefits from recent acquisitions. These events, while viewed as temporary, underscore the operational complexities of integrating acquired businesses and expanding infrastructure.

The path to significant shareholder returns is clear and multi-faceted, resting on a continuation of its proven strategy within a highly favorable market situation.

With a market share of only around 8.0% in its retail and convenience segment and approximately 5.6% in the foodservice segment, Kitwave has a multi-decade runway to continue its successful consolidation strategy. ### **Threats**

The primary threats to the investment thesis are largely macroeconomic and regulatory in nature. The profit warning was, in part, triggered by weaker consumer confidence, which disproportionately affected spending in the high-margin leisure sector; a key end market for the foodservice division. A prolonged UK recession would likely continue to pressure this segment.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!n56z!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fca1d48dd-cd1d-4c08-a39a-fb682aa23429_1831x963.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!n56z!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fca1d48dd-cd1d-4c08-a39a-fb682aa23429_1831x963.png)

The company also faces persistent input cost inflation and regulatory cost pressures. The UK government's hike in National Insurance contributions, for example, is expected to add approximately £1.8 million to the Group's labor costs in fiscal year 2025, a direct headwind to profitability.

While Kitwave has historically been able to pass on supplier price increases, its ability to absorb government-mandated cost increases is more limited.

While the competitive moat is strong, the long-term threat of a larger competitor attempting to replicate Kitwave's high-service model or a disruptive new technology-led entrant cannot be entirely dismissed.

However, the scale-based and density-dependent nature of Kitwave's advantage makes such a direct assault a costly and challenging proposition.

The convergence of these temporary weaknesses and external threats is precisely what created the investment opportunity. The market has incorrectly extrapolated a series of transient issues (integration costs, consumer softness, a tax hike) into a permanent impairment of the company's long-term value.

This reaction ignores the durable strengths of the business model and the immense opportunities for future growth, creating the mispricing that astute investors can now exploit.

Kitwave mitigates supplier concentration by sourcing through Unitas Wholesale, a purchasing group with over £8 billion in annual buying power. This provides Kitwave with the negotiating leverage of a much larger entity. A new entrant would face a classic chicken-and-egg problem: to compete on service (offering daily deliveries with a low £100 minimum order), one needs high route density. To achieve high route density, one needs a large, established customer base. Building this from scratch would require years of loss-making investment to replicate the network that Kitwave has meticulously constructed over more than three decades.
* Threat of Substitutes: 7/10 (Low)  
  The main substitute for Kitwave's delivered wholesale service is the traditional Cash & Carry model. This model has been in structural decline for over a decade, with its share of the food wholesale market falling from over 80% in 2007 to less than 50% today. As independent retailers place an ever-higher premium on convenience, time savings, and efficient inventory management, the trend toward delivery is a powerful and enduring tailwind for Kitwave.
* Competitive Rivalry: 5/10 (Medium)  
  The wholesale market is intensely competitive, but the rivalry is segmented. Kitwave faces numerous small, regional distributors, but its primary competitors in terms of scale are the industry giants. However, as detailed below, these giants operate with a fundamentally different business model, limiting direct, head-to-head competition in Kitwave's core niche.
* Overall Comment (Score: 33/50 - Favorable Industry Structure)  
  The industry structure is highly favorable for an operator with Kitwave's specific model. Low customer power, high barriers to entry for its niche, and a declining substitute product create a protective environment that allows for sustained, profitable growth.

### **Competitive Analysis**

* **Sustainable Competitive Advantage:** Kitwave's competitive advantage is a process-based economic moat derived from the synergistic interplay of three key factors: **(1) High Service Levels**, evidenced by 98%+ on-time, in-full delivery and low minimum order values; **(2) High Route Density**, achieved through a fleet of c.650 vehicles making over 6,500 daily deliveries; and **(3) Buying Scale**, amplified by its membership in a £8 billion+ purchasing group.
* **Evaluation of Economic Moat:** The moat is narrow but deep and, crucially, it is widening. Each new customer added to a delivery route increases the density and profitability of that route, making it more difficult for a competitor to challenge. This, in turn, allows Kitwave to offer even better service, attracting more customers in a virtuous, self-reinforcing cycle. Similarly, each acquisition adds to the company's overall purchasing volume, improving its terms with suppliers and further enhancing its cost advantage relative to smaller regional players. This flywheel effect is the engine of Kitwave's durable competitive advantage.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!3Qo6!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F09eaee25-74ed-4938-a7c4-5f73e15c1c13_2048x1111.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!3Qo6!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F09eaee25-74ed-4938-a7c4-5f73e15c1c13_2048x1111.png)

The competitive landscape is not a zero-sum battle but a clear segmentation of the market.

The industry giants, Booker and Bestway, have optimized their operations for a high-volume, low-touch model based on large depots and franchise agreements. Kitwave, in contrast, has built its entire business around a low-volume, high-touch delivery model tailored to the specific needs of independent operators.

Their vans are up to 12 times smaller than those of the large distributors for a reason.

A logistics network designed to efficiently deliver full pallets to a supermarket cannot be easily repurposed to profitably deliver a £150 mixed order to a corner shop.

They are, in effect, playing different games on the same field, which is why Kitwave has been able to not just survive but thrive in their shadow.

## **Operational Overview**

Kitwave's operational footprint is the physical manifestation of its competitive strategy, designed for nationwide reach with localized service.

**Products:** The company offers a comprehensive range of over 40,000 products tailored to its customer segments. This includes ambient goods (confectionery, soft drinks, snacks, tobacco), frozen and chilled products (ice cream, pizzas, ready meals, dairy), and a full suite of foodservice items (fresh produce, butchery, groceries, alcohol). This wide basket allows customers to consolidate their purchasing, enhancing convenience.

**Geographies:** Kitwave operates a nationwide distribution network across the United Kingdom. This network consists of 37 strategically located depots, comprising 12 main stock-holding hubs and 25 smaller satellite depots. The acquisition of Creed was instrumental in filling previous geographical gaps, creating a truly seamless national delivery capability.

**Segments:** The business is managed through two primary channels, which are composed of its three historical operating divisions:

* **Retail & Wholesale (Ambient and Frozen & Chilled Divisions):** This is the company's foundational business, serving independent convenience stores, vending operators, and other wholesalers. In fiscal year 2024, this segment generated revenue of £440.1 million, representing steady growth of 3.9% year-over-year.
* **Foodservice:** This is the company's key growth engine, supplying independent restaurants, pubs, leisure outlets, care homes, and education-sector customers. Bolstered by recent acquisitions, this segment grew revenue by an impressive 25.2% in fiscal year 2024 to £223.6 million, with strong underlying organic growth of 7.8%.

## **Industry Overview**

### **Sector Overview & Trends**

Kitwave operates within the vast and essential UK grocery and foodservice wholesale market, an industry with an estimated annual size of £33.5 billion. This sector serves as the critical intermediary linking food and drink manufacturers with the tens of thousands of outlets where consumers make their final purchases.

The market structure is characterized by a fascinating dichotomy.

At the top, it is dominated by a few very large players, namely Tesco-owned Booker Group and the independent Bestway Wholesale. However, beneath this top layer, the market is highly fragmented, with a long tail of smaller, regional, and specialist wholesalers. It is this fragmentation that creates the fertile ground for a disciplined consolidator like Kitwave to execute its "buy-and-build" strategy.

The most powerful and enduring trend shaping this industry is the structural shift away from the traditional Cash & Carry model towards Delivered Wholesale. For decades, independent retailers would drive to large out-of-town depots to purchase stock. Today, they increasingly prioritize the convenience, time savings, and improved inventory management that comes from having goods delivered directly to their premises.

This secular trend, which has seen the Cash & Carry market share decline from over 80% in 2007 to under 50% today, provides a powerful, long-term tailwind for Kitwave's entire delivery-focused business model.

## **Revenue Trends, Drivers & Margins**

### **Revenue trends**

* **Overall Trends:** The company has a long-term track record of robust top-line growth. More recently, revenue has expanded from £503.1 million in FY22 to £663.7 million in FY24, and is forecast to reach £818.1 million in FY25. This represents a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of approximately 17.5% from FY22 to FY25E, driven by the company's dual engines of organic expansion and strategic acquisitions.
* **Volumes:** Underlying organic growth has been consistently in the 3-4% per annum range. This is achieved by winning new customers within its niche and, critically, by increasing the "share of wallet" with its existing 46,000+ customer base. By cross-selling products from its different divisions, for example, selling frozen foods to a convenience store that previously only bought ambient snacks. Kitwave drives incremental, high-margin growth.
* **Product/Services Mix:** The most important driver of profitability is the deliberate strategic shift towards the higher-margin Foodservice segment. In FY24, the Foodservice division grew by 25.2% (7.8% organically), far outpacing the 3.9% growth in the Retail & Wholesale segment. This favorable mix shift is a key tenet of the investment thesis, as it is expected to mechanically lift the Group's overall operating margins by an estimated 10 basis points every couple of years.
* **Pricing:** The nature of the wholesale business allows Kitwave to act as a conduit for price changes from its suppliers. The company has demonstrated the ability to pass through inflation in the cost of goods to its customers, thereby protecting its gross margin percentage even in an inflationary environment.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!1tIf!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fe520d90a-3c96-4b96-af0d-1d88c1d4f448_1600x898.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!1tIf!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fe520d90a-3c96-4b96-af0d-1d88c1d4f448_1600x898.png)



### **Margins**

* **Summary of Cost Structure:** Kitwave's cost structure is typical of a distribution business, with cost of goods sold (COGS) being the largest component, followed by significant distribution expenses (vehicle fuel, maintenance, and labor) and administrative overheads.
* **COGS & Gross Margins:** A key indicator of Kitwave's increasing scale and favorable business mix is the steady expansion of its gross margin. This has improved from 20.4% in FY22 to 22.3% in FY24, and is forecast to reach 22.5% in FY25. This reflects both the benefits of enhanced buying power through its purchasing group and the accretive impact of growing the Foodservice division. Kitwave's gross margin has expanded by 16 percentage points over the last 15 years, a clear sign of its strengthening competitive position.
* **OPEX & Operating Margin:** As the company scales, it benefits from operating leverage. The adjusted operating margin stood at 5.1% in FY24 and is expected to expand further as the synergies from the Creed acquisition are fully realized and the positive mix shift continues to play out.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!N7v8!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F1712f7fc-6d27-425a-ad68-3854e49f1301_2034x1138.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!N7v8!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F1712f7fc-6d27-425a-ad68-3854e49f1301_2034x1138.png)



* **ROCE:** Return on Capital Employed is a crucial measure of business quality. Prior to the large Creed acquisition, Kitwave was generating an excellent post-tax return on capital of 20%, indicative of a business with a strong economic moat. The reported ROCE in FY24 declined to 11%. However, this decline is a temporary accounting artifact. The acquisition of Creed was funded by a £31.5 million equity issuance and additional debt, significantly increasing the capital base (the denominator in the ROCE calculation) right at the end of the fiscal year. Because the acquisition closed on September 27, 2024, only one month of Creed's earnings was included in the FY24 results. As the full, annualized earnings power of the combined entity is reflected in the FY25 results, this key metric of business quality is expected to rebound sharply, which could serve as a catalyst for the market to re-evaluate the stock.

## **Other Things**

### **Management**

* **Profiles:** The company is led by a long-tenured and highly experienced executive team. Chief Executive Officer Ben Maxted was appointed in March 2024 but has been with the Group since 2011, previously serving as COO and leading the growth of the Frozen & Chilled division. Chief Financial Officer David Brind has also been with the company since 2011 and has been the architect of its successful acquisition strategy. This stability and deep institutional knowledge is a significant asset.
* **Capital Allocation History:** The management team's track record on capital allocation is exemplary and forms the cornerstone of the investment thesis. They have successfully executed and integrated 15 acquisitions over the past 13 years, paying a disciplined average multiple of 5.1x EBITDA. This demonstrates a clear ability to deploy shareholder capital into value-accretive opportunities, which is the company's core competency.

Both the CEO and CFO have substantial future ownership potential through the Management Incentive Plan (MIP) and Long-Term Incentive Plan (LTIP) awards. The MIP alone entitles them to a combined maximum of 4% of the company's market value upon exercise, directly linking their personal wealth creation to long-term shareholder returns.

Importantly for the take-private thesis, the company's founder, Paul Young, has reduced his stake to 4.6%. This means there is no single blocking shareholder that could prevent a potential acquisition of the company.

### **IPO**

Kitwave listed on the AIM market in May 2021 with a market capitalization of £105 million. The Initial Public Offering was a strategic move designed to accomplish two key objectives: first, to raise primary capital to de-lever the balance sheet, which at the time had net financial debt in excess of 4 times EBITDA; and second, to create a well-capitalized public platform to accelerate its consolidation strategy. The IPO successfully set the stage for the current phase of growth, including the transformative acquisition of Creed.

### **Valuation**

The culmination of this analysis is a business of demonstrable quality trading at a valuation that is demonstrably low. The margin of safety is provided by this significant discount to intrinsic value, which exists across multiple valuation methodologies. On a simple multiples basis, the stock is inexpensive. Based on analyst consensus estimates for fiscal year 2025, Kitwave shares trade at just 8.66 times forward earnings and an enterprise value to forward EBIT of 8.14x. The trailing twelve-month TEV/EBITDA multiple is a mere 6.8x.

This discount becomes even more apparent when compared to peers and precedent transactions.

### **The Compelling Take-Private Case**

Beyond the public market valuation, Kitwave presents a textbook case for a private equity buyout, making it a highly attractive acquisition target. The characteristics that contribute to its public market mispricing are the very same attributes that financial sponsors actively seek:

* **Ideal Size:** With a market capitalization under £200 million, Kitwave is in the sweet spot for a wide range of mid-market private equity funds, ensuring a competitive bidding environment.
* **Low Leverage & Debt Capacity:** The balance sheet is clean. Net debt (excluding IFRS 16 lease liabilities) stands at a conservative 1.7x LTM EBITDA. This is a fraction of the 4x+ leverage the company sustained prior to its IPO, indicating significant capacity for a new owner to add debt to finance the acquisition. A key driver of private equity returns.
* **Fragmented Ownership:** The founder, Paul Young, has reduced his stake to just 4.6%, and there are no other blocking shareholders This open share register makes a friendly takeover proposal significantly easier to execute.
* **Strong, Predictable Cash Flow:** The business model's high cash conversion is ideal for servicing the acquisition debt that a financial buyer would place on the company.
* **Valuation Arbitrage:** The most relevant precedent transaction in the UK wholesale space is Tesco's acquisition of Booker which was completed at a multiple of over 20 times post-tax earnings. This suggests a potential take-out valuation more than double Kitwave's current trading multiple, offering a clear and substantial upside for a buyer.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!r49L!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fdccc136f-c08c-46eb-9364-64f613614e89_1600x900.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!r49L!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fdccc136f-c08c-46eb-9364-64f613614e89_1600x900.png)

Crucially, the investment thesis does not depend on a strategic transaction.

A base case can be constructed that delivers compelling returns simply from the existing operations and strategy. An investor today collects a dividend yield of over 5%. In addition, the company generates free cash flow in excess of 7% of its market capitalization annually.

The disciplined deployment of this cash into accretive acquisitions that grow per-share earnings, combined with the dividend, provides a clear path to strong returns.

The high probability of a take-private event provides a powerful, unpriced upside optionality and a hard catalyst for value realization.

### **What could go wrong?**

To formulate a bearish case on Kitwave, one must subscribe to a series of highly improbable assumptions:

1. You must believe that the operational issues highlighted in the July profit warning are not temporary, but rather the beginning of a permanent deterioration in management's ability to execute its strategy.
2. You must believe that the transformative Creed acquisition will be a failure, destroying value instead of creating it, and that management will follow this error with a string of further value-destructive deals, completely reversing their 15-year track record of success.
3. You must believe that the powerful, multi-decade structural shift from Cash & Carry to Delivered Wholesale will suddenly reverse, or that a major competitor will successfully pivot to attack Kitwave's protected niche, permanently impairing its margins and returns on capital.

Given the company's long and consistent history of operational excellence, its deep-seated competitive advantages, and the enduring nature of the industry trends that favor its model, these bearish scenarios are considered to have a very low probability.

### **Conclusion**

Kitwave Group represents a rare and compelling investment opportunity where the primary upside catalyst is its clear attractiveness as an acquisition target.

It is a high-quality, shareholder-aligned compounding machine that has been placed in the "too hard" pile by the market due to a series of short-term, solvable issues. The market's myopic focus has created an attractive entry point into a business whose key characteristics are low leverage, strong cash flow, fragmented ownership, and a proven M&A platform.

This reads like a private equity buyout checklist.

The significant valuation disconnect between its current public multiple and established private market transaction values provides a substantial margin of safety. The take-private potential offers a hard floor for the valuation and a clear, plausible catalyst for realizing this value.

For the patient investor, Kitwave offers a highly asymmetric risk/reward profile, where the downside is protected by a durable business and the upside is driven by the high probability that a strategic or financial buyer will recognize the same deep value that is currently being overlooked by the public market.
