## Document 79

In September 2022, the Chinese government issued a directive so sensitive that high-ranking officials were only shown the paper and not allowed to make copies. It is formally known as Document 79. Informally, in the halls of State-Owned Enterprises (SOEs), it is known as the “Delete A” directive.

“Delete A” stands for “Delete America”.

The order is simple and non-negotiable: State-owned companies in strategic sectors, finance, energy, telecommunications, transport, must replace all foreign IT software and hardware in their systems by 2027.

This is not a suggestion. It is a national security mandate.

## The Scale of the Purge

We are talking about a total decoupling of the Chinese state economy from Western tech.

Hardware: Dell, Cisco, and IBM are being taken out of server rooms across the country. Dell’s market share in China has already almost halved in the past five years.

Software: Microsoft Windows and Office are the primary targets. The goal is to replace them with Linux-based alternatives like UOS (Unity Operating System).

Databases: Oracle is being shown the door. One of the last bastions of foreign tech profitability in the country is crumbling.

This directive expands a drive that is muscling U.S. technology out of the country. A Morgan Stanley poll of 135 CIOs in China found that many expected the share of computers powered by Windows to fall significantly over the next three years.

# Part V: YY Live Acquisition Termination

Finally, we must address the $3.6 billion elephant in the room that vanished.

In 2020, Baidu announced it would buy YY Live (the domestic livestreaming business of JOYY Inc.) for $3.6 billion. At the time, it seemed like a desperate move to buy traffic. Livestreaming was hot. Baidu needed engagement.

The Freeze: The deal sat in regulatory purgatory for three years. The State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) never signed off.

The Kill: In January 2024, Baidu formally terminated the deal. They cited “unmet conditions,” specifically that the closing was subject to regulatory approvals that had not been obtained by the long stop date of December 31, 2023. JOYY was furious. They threatened legal action, claiming the sale was “substantially completed” back in February 2021.

The Interpretation: Did Baidu fail to close, or did they choose to fail? I argue the latter. In 2020, livestreaming was the future. By 2024, livestreaming had become a regulatory minefield (crackdowns on “vulgarity” and tipping) and a low-margin business. Baidu had also launched “Baidu Youxuan,” its own e-commerce brand integrating livestreaming, making a dedicated platform like YY less critical.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!wxMC!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fdd5f2d39-7a93-41e3-90b8-8a1c67cd9e47_1405x759.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!wxMC!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fdd5f2d39-7a93-41e3-90b8-8a1c67cd9e47_1405x759.png)

Baidu realized: “Wait, we can just keep our $3.6 billion and spend it on AI instead.”

In fact, the $1.6 billion Baidu had already deposited into escrow was released back to them. Baidu explicitly stated it plans to invest such released funds into its cloud business and AI. This was a lucky escape. Baidu accidentally dodged a bullet because the government was too slow to approve the gun sale. Now, they have an extra $3.6B (gross) in liquidity to burn on chips and LLMs. It looks like incompetence, but it ended up being accidental brilliance.

# Conclusion: Value Trap or Deep Value?

So, what is Baidu?

Is it the toxic wasteland of Qu Jing’s PR department? Is it the failed car salesman of Jiyue? Is it the “Paper Wolf” that talks big about AI but gets out-hyped by startups like Moore Threads?

Or is it the Last Man Standing in a “Delete America” world?

## The Bull Case

“Delete A” is Real: The government will force billions of revenue into Baidu’s cloud and software stack by 2027. You don’t have to believe in Baidu’s management; you just have to believe in the CCP’s paranoia.

The Chip Hedge: If they can get Kunlun M300 working by 2027, they own their own destiny while Alibaba and Tencent scramble for smuggled Nvidias.

Valuation: It is priced for death. Any sign of life, a successful Ernie 5.0 monetization, a stabilized ad market, sends this stock up 50%.

## The Bear Case

### 1. “Delete A” Is Not a Baidu Monopoly

Document 79 benefits everyone. ByteDance, Tencent, Alibaba, Huawei, and fifty other vendors are all fighting for the same displaced spend. Baidu has no exclusive claim to government IT budgets.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!k142!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F530db7c9-bf04-4643-b985-e3ffa48b8103_1403x787.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!k142!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F530db7c9-bf04-4643-b985-e3ffa48b8103_1403x787.png)

Worse: “Delete A” affects enterprise procurement. Baidu’s core business is consumer advertising. Policy tailwinds in one segment do not offset structural collapse in another.

### 2. The Advertising Business Is Dying

Online marketing revenue: down 18% year-over-year in Q3 2025.

Douyin is the new front door to the Chinese internet. WeChat owns transactions. Baidu search is what you use when you have to, not when you want to. Management admits AI search monetisation “has yet to scale.”

#### What Advertising Decline Actually Means

Our discussion of advertising decline has been qualitative. Let us make it quantitative.

Baidu Core’s online marketing revenue was approximately RMB 66 billion in FY2024. Q3 2025 showed an 18% year-over-year decline. If we assume varying rates of decline and modest AI Cloud growth, the earnings impact is noticable:

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!wy40!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F97dab384-b7fa-4879-bbe7-0b2ef05c8f68_1403x787.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!wy40!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F97dab384-b7fa-4879-bbe7-0b2ef05c8f68_1403x787.png)

The consensus 2025E EPS of $7.50 implies the market is pricing something between our Base and Pessimistic cases. The projected recovery to $9.97 by 2027 requires either (a) advertising decline to moderate significantly, or (b) AI Cloud to grow faster than 20% annually, or (c) substantial cost cuts.

Consider what these numbers mean in practice:

At -12% annual decline (Base case), advertising revenue falls from RMB 66B to RMB 45B by 2028. That is RMB 21 billion in annual revenue destruction, roughly $2.9 billion USD. AI Cloud growing at 20% from a RMB 24B base adds approximately RMB 17 billion over the same period. **The core business is shrinking faster than the growth business is growing.**

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!T88I!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fb6e62aa6-0cc3-4bc0-b6a6-33b2fd66249d_1403x792.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!T88I!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fb6e62aa6-0cc3-4bc0-b6a6-33b2fd66249d_1403x792.png)

This is not a “transition”; it is a race against time. Every quarter that AI monetization fails to inflect is a quarter where the advertising melting accelerates the endgame.

The EPS surprise chart offers cold comfort. Yes, Baidu has beaten estimates in FY23 and FY24 (by 6% in FY24). But the estimates themselves have collapsed: from $11.43 actual in FY23 to $7.50 expected in FY25. Beating a number that is down 34% is not victory; it is losing more slowly than feared.

### 3. Governance Is Broken

May 2024: VP of Public Relations creates the company’s worst PR crisis in years. 60% of her team quits within months.

November 2024: Jiyue, a JV with Geely backed by billions, implodes. Employees protest unpaid wages. Rumours of a 7 billion RMB black hole. Allegations of procurement fraud. The CFO had already fled to the UK.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!toYy!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fe9621ed1-bc84-4b5f-92c9-fa63674034cd_1403x785.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!toYy!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fe9621ed1-bc84-4b5f-92c9-fa63674034cd_1403x785.png)

One incident is bad luck. Two incidents in six months is a pattern. If Baidu cannot prevent catastrophic failures in high-profile ventures with sophisticated partners, why trust them with AI monetisation?

### 4. Kunlunxin Is Not Nvidia

Kunlunxin chips are optimised for inference, not training. They work best for “government, telecom, and state-owned cloud users, where stable supply and lower cost matter more than top performance.”

Beijing is deliberately fragmenting chip procurement across Moore Threads, Biren, Cambricon, Huawei Ascend, and others. The state does not want a single winner.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!CRLx!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F8803d328-0f8f-4d29-bb4e-8539f1ca1ffa_1403x666.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!CRLx!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F8803d328-0f8f-4d29-bb4e-8539f1ca1ffa_1403x666.png)

Baidu’s 59% stake at a $3B valuation equals $1.8B. Even if it doubles, that is $3.6B in value creation. Meaningful. But not enough to offset an 18% annual decline in a $15B+ advertising business.

### 5. Competition Is Intensifying

One shouldn’t dismiss rivals too quickly

ByteDance has native AI integration across Douyin. 700 million daily users. Baidu cannot match that distribution.

Alibaba’s Qwen models compete directly with Ernie. Tencent is everywhere. Huawei’s Ascend 910C reportedly matches Nvidia’s H100.

### 6. The “Utility” Thesis Is Actually Bearish

Utilities trade at low multiples for a reason: low growth, heavy regulation, capital intensity, political risk. If Baidu’s future is as a state-adjacent infrastructure provider, the correct valuation is a discount, not a premium.

You are not buying a tech company. You are buying a regulated quasi-SOE with a search engine attached.

### 7. Robin Li Is Still in Charge

The same CEO who:

* Said Chinese users would “trade privacy for convenience” (2018)
* Approved the YY Live deal that sat in regulatory purgatory for three years
* Oversaw the Qu Jing hiring and the Jiyue disaster
* Has run Baidu for 25 years while it fell from BAT leader to distant third

## Valuation Reconciliation: What Is Baidu Actually Worth?

We have presented both bull and bear narratives. Now we must do what sell-side analysts are paid to avoid: commit to numbers.

**The Consensus Baseline**

As of January 2026, 33 analysts cover Baidu with a mean price target of $162.26 and a median of $160.95. The range spans from $89.69 (bear) to $265.78 (bull), a spread of nearly 3x that reflects genuine uncertainty about the company’s trajectory. The consensus recommendation of 1.62 (”Outperform”) sounds bullish, but the implied upside of only 13% from current prices (~$143) suggests this is a “buy because it’s cheap” thesis rather than a “buy because it’s working” thesis.

The Street’s multiples tell a different story than our normalized figures:

This does not mean Baidu is a short. It means the margin of safety is thinner than the narrative suggests. We are not buying a dollar for fifty cents; we are buying a dollar for eighty-five cents with a lottery ticket attached.
