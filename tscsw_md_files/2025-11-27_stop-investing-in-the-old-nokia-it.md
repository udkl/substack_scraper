Yesterday in New York, Nokia’s management completely changed the company’s past.

CEO Justin Hotard stood on stage and effectively declared the era of “Connecting People”, which sadly includes the nostalgia of 2G and Snake, over. The new mantra is “Connecting Intelligence,” and the thesis is that we are entering an “AI Supercycle” where the network, not the chip, becomes the bottleneck.

It would be easy, perhaps even prudent, to dismiss this as standard corporate noise, a legacy hardware vendor wrapping itself in the AI flag to beg for a multiple re-rating. We see “AI” plastered on everything from toasters to mining equipment these days.

But this time, the slide deck came with a receipt. Nvidia is not just a partner; they are now a shareholder, cutting a $1 billion equity check to acquire a ~2.9% stake in the company. When the shovel-seller (Nvidia) starts buying the land for the railroad (Nokia), you should most definitely to pay attention.

So… Here is the probability-weighted reality of what you’re actually investing in when you say that you are investing in ‘Nokia’, after yesterday’s Capital Markets Day (CMD).

### What You’re Actually Long

If you bought Nokia because you saw “AI” and “Nvidia” in the same headline, you need to look at the ledger. Post-CMD, management has explicitly bifurcated the company into two distinct vessels:

The AI Infra Compounder (Network Infrastructure - NI): This is the “good” business. Optical networks (boosted by the Infinera acquisition), IP routing, and data center switching. It’s a €7.8 billion business with decent margins (10% operating margin today, targeting 13-17% by 2028). They are promising a 6-8% CAGR here because hyperscalers need to move data between their AI factories.

The Call Option (Mobile Infrastructure - MI): This is the legacy business model; RAN plus the patent machine. It’s €11.6 billion in sales, but notice the target: no top-line growth promise. They simply want to keep gross margins at 48-50% and “grow from” the current €1.5 billion operating profit base.

Here is the critical nuance: AI-RAN lives inside their old Mobile Infrastructure.

The CMD couldn’t have expressed it clearer. Trials for AI-RAN start in 2026 with T-Mobile. Commercialization isn’t until “late 2027.” It utilizes the new “ARC-Pro” platform with Nvidia GPUs and Dell servers, but it is not baked into their 2028 profit KPIs.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!kneD!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F71a39b3b-5bf3-4c9f-a4ea-e26f1ec036f8_1600x889.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!kneD!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F71a39b3b-5bf3-4c9f-a4ea-e26f1ec036f8_1600x889.png)

So, your Nokia long is structurally:

60–70% “Boring AI Infra Compounder” (NI + Patents)

30–40% “Free Option on the AI-RAN Revolution”

If you size this position thinking AI-RAN is the engine driving the P&L in 2025, you are offside. You are betting on a narrative that management hasn’t even underwritten yet.

### Scenario Probabilities (In Our Opinion)

Let’s strip away the share price and look at the business reality in 2030.

The Bear Case (~20%) The “AI Supercycle” is just a capex bubble. Hyperscalers squeeze vendors, and Nokia’s NI growth comes in at something insignificant like 3-4%. In Mobile, AI-RAN is a science project that never scales because operators are too cheap to replace their hardware. The stock remains a cyclical value trap, trading on a low multiple of its cash flow. Probability: 0.20

The Base Case (~55%) The “Boring” thesis works. NI delivers the 6-8% growth as data centers demand more optical transport (North American optical orders are already up 40% YoY, so this is happening). The Infinera synergies hit. Mobile Infrastructure stays flat but profitable, fueled by patent licensing. AI-RAN is a nice niche product, not a revolution. The group hits its target of €2.7–3.2 billion in operating profit by 2028. Probability: 0.55

The Bull Case (~25%) This is Hugh Hendry’s “Acid Capitalist” scenario. The “Gold Rush” in the airwaves actually happens. AI-RAN becomes the dominant architecture for 6G because it’s the only way to handle the massive uplink demand of AI agents and robotics. Nokia monetizes this via high-margin software on Nvidia hardware, escaping the “box-shifter” curse. NI becomes a strategic asset for Western sovereignty. The group pushes toward €4 billion+ in profit in the early 2030s. Probability: 0.25

### Where the Value Chain Actually Fits

You say you want exposure to the “RAN value chain.” Fair enough. But let’s be precise about who gets paid.

In a traditional network, Nokia sells the proprietary box and keeps the margin. In the new AI-RAN world powered by Nvidia’s ARC-Pro:

Nvidia captures the economics of the compute (the GPUs and CUDA stack).

Dell captures the commoditized server margin.

Nokia captures the software (AI-RAN, automation), the integration, and the transport (the optical fiber connecting it all).

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!CH7z!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fa3b774a2-89a5-4c20-aac6-b43599985706_1864x1050.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!CH7z!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fa3b774a2-89a5-4c20-aac6-b43599985706_1864x1050.png)

A Nokia long gives you exposure to the deployment of this architecture, not the silicon itself. If you are long Nvidia, do not double-count the same dollar. Nokia’s upside here is margin expansion (selling software instead of heavy metal), not necessarily a revenue explosion.

### What CMD + Nvidia Actually Did to the Thesis

Yesterday’s event did two things:

1. It De-Risked the Floor: Nvidia putting $1 billion into Nokia for a 2.9% stake is not charity. It validates that Nokia is the chosen partner for the Western AI stack. It puts a floor under the “technological obsolescence” risk. Furthermore, the NI segment now has a credible path to 13-17% margins independent of the mobile cycle.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!ORj8!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F28b886d5-0c0b-453b-ad2f-6cee2a9af4f1_1864x462.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!ORj8!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F28b886d5-0c0b-453b-ad2f-6cee2a9af4f1_1864x462.png)

2. It Capped the Near-Term Hype: Management refused to give a revenue target for Mobile Infrastructure. They replaced the old “grow faster than the market” target with a profit target. This is discipline, and we like discipline. But it signals that inside the house, they view AI-RAN as 2027+ optionality, not a 2025 profit engine.

### Position Sizing: Don’t Be a Hero

Given that Nokia is now a “Solid Infra Play + High Beta Option,” how do you size it?

If you are already heavy: Downsize. This is not Nvidia. It is not a secular compounder growing 50% a year. It is a turnaround story with a call option.

If you are light/empty: This is a rational place to park capital if you are underwriting the Base Case (the boring infrastructure growth).

The Rule: Size it based on the NI and Patent cash flows. Treat the AI-RAN explosion as a free lottery ticket. If AI-RAN goes to zero, the position should still work. If AI-RAN works, you look like a genius.

### Moving Forward

The market wants a casino; Nokia is building a utility.

Nokia is now a reasonable, risk-aware long on the physical reality of the AI economy, the fiber, the switches, and the spectrum management with a genuine but distant option on the “gold rush” of AI-RAN.

That justifies a measured position. It does not justify betting the farm. The gold rush is coming, but Nokia is selling the shovels, and shovel sellers get paid slowly.
