# **2. Industry Structure: Porter’s Five Forces**

Understanding industry structure is essential to assessing whether SAF-Holland’s competitive position is sustainable. Here is a rigorous Porter’s analysis:

### **Supplier Power: 6/10 (Moderate)**

SAF-Holland sources steel, aluminum, and specialty components globally. Some concentration exists in specialty inputs, particularly brake components post-Haldex integration. The company can partially offset supplier power through vertical integration and multi-sourcing, but raw material price volatility (steel, aluminum) can compress margins. In 2022, SAF-Holland “compensated for the sharp rise in steel, freight and energy costs through price adjustments,” which indicates defensive rather than offensive pricing power.

### **Buyer Power: 5/10 (Split Profile)**

This is a tale of two customer bases:

**OEM customers** are concentrated large players (Daimler Truck, Traton, PACCAR) with significant negotiating leverage. No single customer exceeds 10% of revenue, but collectively they can exert pricing pressure. CEO Geis acknowledged “price pressure” while noting it is “not that” severe.

**Aftermarket customers** (fleet operators, service centers) are more fragmented and loyal to quality. Switching costs are moderate-to-high due to safety certification requirements and service network dependencies.

### **Barriers to Entry: 7/10 (High in Developed Markets)**

**High barriers:** Safety certification requirements (chassis components are safety-critical); established OEM relationships requiring years of qualification; global service network (~10,000+ points) extremely difficult to replicate.

**Moderate barriers:** Capital requirements for manufacturing footprint.

**Lower barriers:** Chinese entrants have succeeded in emerging markets with cost advantages. Fuwa and Wondee have already displaced Western suppliers in Russia and are expanding in the Middle East.

### **Competitive Rivalry: 6/10 (Manageable but Intensifying)**

Western markets are oligopolistic: SAF-Holland, JOST, and BPW dominate. Pricing behavior is generally rational; CEO Geis noted that competitors “also need to make some money.” However, JOST’s Hyva acquisition intensifies competition, and Chinese players are disrupting at lower price points. Competition is also intensifying through innovation (automated coupling, telematics integration).

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!7SFJ!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fa491f3b4-8708-44b3-b76c-7659613f88d1_1600x890.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!7SFJ!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fa491f3b4-8708-44b3-b76c-7659613f88d1_1600x890.png)

### **Threat of Substitutes: 4/10 (Low)**

Fifth wheels, axles, and brake systems have no direct substitutes for heavy trucking. Electrification creates opportunity (e-axles, electric trailer features) rather than threat. Alternative transport modes (rail, autonomous trucking) are long-term considerations, not near-term threats. Components remain essential regardless of powertrain evolution.

### **Overall Porter Score: 28/50**

SAF-Holland operates in a structurally attractive oligopoly with meaningful barriers to entry in developed markets. However, the “moat” is narrower than bulls suggest. JOST is a credible and strengthening competitor. Chinese players represent a real threat in price-sensitive markets. The aftermarket “stickiness” is real but not impenetrable, as distribution networks can be built over time.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Mtuq!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fbb13af43-4faa-4a95-ade3-b8a7fba037e3_1600x893.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Mtuq!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fbb13af43-4faa-4a95-ade3-b8a7fba037e3_1600x893.png)

# **3. Competitive Landscape**

## **3.1 Key Competitors**

**JOST Werke SE (Germany)** is SAF-Holland’s most direct competitor and the other half of the Western trailer components duopoly.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!VYvk!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fe9807875-b2c8-4df2-8540-6db5fa10ce6a_1600x897.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!VYvk!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fe9807875-b2c8-4df2-8540-6db5fa10ce6a_1600x897.png)

**Critical development:** JOST acquired Hyva (hydraulic solutions) in January 2025. Management expects this to boost consolidated sales by 50-60% in FY2025 vs FY2024, implying pro forma revenue of EUR 1.6-1.7bn. Post-acquisition, JOST will be comparable in size to SAF-Holland with arguably better growth trajectory.

**BPW Bergische Achsen KG (Germany)** is a family-owned company since 1898, estimated at EUR 1.5-2.0bn revenue. Strong in European trailer running gear (won ETM Award 15 consecutive years). Focus areas include ePower generator axles and VECTO-compliant products. Limited Americas presence compared to SAF-Holland.

**Hendrickson Holdings LLC (USA)** is a subsidiary of Boler Company with estimated revenue >USD 2bn. Historically dominant in North America, particularly strong in truck suspension versus trailer focus. Limited European footprint. Launched new lightweight axle system in March 2025.

**Guangdong Fuwa Engineering Group (China)** is the domestic champion in China with estimated revenue of USD 500-800m. Key products include trailer axles, fifth wheel couplings, landing gear, and suspensions. Fuwa offers cost-competitive products that have already replaced Western suppliers in Russia and are expanding in the Middle East. The Fuwa-K-Hitch combination creates an integrated offering. This is the competitor that should concern long-term investors most.

## **4.3 Economic Moat Assessment**

**Overall Moat Rating: NARROW**

The bull thesis implicitly assumes a wide moat. The evidence does not support this.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!AAz3!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F4046dfb2-95c0-474f-97e9-0620941b8570_1600x887.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!AAz3!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F4046dfb2-95c0-474f-97e9-0620941b8570_1600x887.png)

**Why not a wide moat?**

**No pricing power:** CEO Geis explicitly stated “There is price pressure.” Margins compress during downturns rather than holding firm.

**Duopoly structure helps but does not insulate:** SAF-Holland and JOST maintain pricing discipline (”competitors also need to make some money”), but this is a gentlemen’s agreement, not a structural barrier. It can break down.

**Aftermarket is sticky but not insurmountable:** Competitors like JOST have equivalent service networks in key markets. Chinese players are building distribution.

**Technological differentiation is temporary:** Innovations (e-axles, telematics) are quickly matched. JOST’s KKS automated coupling is a direct response to SAF-Holland’s SHAC development.

**Returns are compressing:** ROCE has declined from 22% (peak June 2024) to 15.5% (September 2025). A wide-moat business should maintain returns on capital; SAF-Holland is not doing so.

## **5.2 Margin Analysis: The COGS Problem**

The bull thesis claims SAF-Holland operates in “oligopolistic niches” with pricing power. The cost structure tells a different story.

**Cost of Sales: 78% of revenue (FY2024)**

This ratio is elevated for a “high-quality” supplier claiming oligopolistic positioning. True oligopolists typically command gross margins above 25%. SAF-Holland’s gross margin of ~22% sits in the lower quartile of auto suppliers generally.

The gross margin “improvement” in Q1 2024 (from 18.1% to 21.9% YoY) was largely mix-driven, not structural. Management explicitly attributed the jump to higher aftermarket share (OE trailer fell from 61.1% to 51.3% of the mix). This is not operational efficiency; it is cyclical OE business falling faster than aftermarket. When OE recovers, this margin benefit will reverse.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!xgXR!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F04c01d85-d292-400d-a8a1-b1bf2d26ff0e_1888x1060.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!xgXR!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F04c01d85-d292-400d-a8a1-b1bf2d26ff0e_1888x1060.png)

**The “Adjusted” EBIT Merits Scrutiny**

German industrial companies frequently use adjusted metrics to exclude restructuring charges, integration costs, and one-offs. With Haldex integration ongoing, plus IMS, Tecma, and Assali Stefen acquisitions in 2024, how much of “underlying” profitability is being masked by continuous add-backs? The reconciliation table should be examined closely.

**Operating Leverage is Unfavorable**

When sales declined 11.9% in H1 2025, adjusted EBIT margin fell from 10.2% to 9.3%. The bull thesis claims “limited downside risk,” but this demonstrates classic negative operating leverage. In Q3 2025, management noted tariff-related purchasing costs in the “mid-single-digit million-euro range,” which they expect to only “largely offset” through countermeasures. This is margin vulnerability, not resilience.

## **5.3 ROCE: The Returns Story**

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Dll-!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F4b9102a0-d645-42cc-9fcc-05cfcb2d83fd_1890x628.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Dll-!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F4b9102a0-d645-42cc-9fcc-05cfcb2d83fd_1890x628.png)

**SAF-Holland has not consistently achieved its medium-term ROCE target.** In 2022, they delivered 12.9% and stated this “exceeded cost of capital.” This is a low bar. A 12-13% ROCE barely covers typical WACC for European industrials (8-10%). The margin of safety above cost of capital is thin.

**Capital employed is inflated by acquisition goodwill.** The Haldex acquisition (~EUR 325m), plus IMS, Tecma, and Assali Stefen, have expanded the capital base with intangible assets. ROCE calculations using post-acquisition capital employed will mechanically be lower. The question is: what is ROCE excluding goodwill? If you strip out acquisition-related intangibles, does underlying ROIC look better? This is not disclosed, which is a red flag.

**JOST’s ROCE at 21% (FY2023) is 1.6x SAF-Holland’s.** If both operate in similar oligopolistic niches, why the dramatic divergence? Either JOST has superior competitive positioning, or SAF-Holland’s “oligopoly” thesis is overstated.

# **9. The Bull Case: Stress-Tested**

Let us examine each pillar of the bull thesis:

### **Claim 1: “10x EV/trailing EBIT is unjust”**

**Counter-argument:** JOST trades at similar multiples (~9-10x EBIT) with arguably better growth profile post-Hyva. 10x for a cyclical industrial at mid-cycle is not obviously cheap; it is fair if you believe in recovery. Forward 2025E NOPAT likely ~EUR 105-110m (EUR 160m EBIT x 0.65), implying 11-12x forward, which is market-appropriate. The “unjust” framing overstates the mispricing.

### **Claim 2: “Sales likely to return to growth in 2026, supported by pre-buying ahead of 2027 emissions standards”**

**Counter-argument:** This is the most problematic claim. The Trump EPA has signaled intention to roll back or significantly ease EPA 2027 regulations. FTR Transportation Intelligence has removed pre-buy volumes from baseline forecasts. CARB waivers have been revoked by Congressional Review Act. While OEMs will still release compliant engines, the demand surge the bull thesis expects is now unlikely. European recovery is more credible but modest (”low double-digit” from depressed base).

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!4oFw!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Ffbaf358f-a641-45eb-bc8d-c81970e18dd9_1888x1060.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!4oFw!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Ffbaf358f-a641-45eb-bc8d-c81970e18dd9_1888x1060.png)

### **Claim 3: “Majority of profits from aftermarket provides resilience”**

**Counter-argument:** Aftermarket is ~38-40% of sales, not “majority.” It contributes a higher share of EBIT (~75%), but margins are ~15-20%, not the 30%+ some analyses imply. The company’s overall profitability (10% EBIT margin) suggests competitive dynamics constrain returns on capital. ROCE has declined from 22% (2022) to 15.5% (Sept 2025).

### **Claim 4: “Limited downside risk”**

**Counter-argument:** Operating leverage is negative. When sales declined 11.9% in H1 2025, EBIT margin fell 90bps. The CEO describes the trailer market as “the lowest I have ever seen in the last 25 years.” It could worsen before recovering. The aftermarket cushions but does not eliminate cyclical exposure.

**Counter-argument:** The bull case assumes 2026 EBIT of EUR 169m and a 13x multiple. But EUR 169m is *below* FY2024’s EUR 190m. If sales are recovering, why would EBIT decline? Either the analyst is being ultra-conservative (creating artificial upside), or they expect margin compression (contradicting the “resilient aftermarket” thesis). And why 13x? No justification is provided. SAF-Holland trades at 10x for structural reasons (lower margins/ROCE versus peers). What catalyst drives re-rating?

**If you assign:** Bear 25%, Base 50%, Bull 25% Expected value: (0.25 x 19) + (0.50 x 32) + (0.25 x 45) = EUR 32 Upside from current: ~100%

**If you assign:** Bear 40%, Base 45%, Bull 15% Expected value: (0.40 x 19) + (0.45 x 32) + (0.15 x 45) = EUR 28.75 Upside from current: ~80%

# **12. Conclusion & Open Questions**

## **Verdict**

SAF-Holland is a decent company at a reasonable valuation. It is not the misunderstood gem that some analysts claim, nor is it a value trap. The truth is more mundane: a cyclical industrial with moderate returns, trading at a fair multiple, with a recovery that may or may not materialize in the next 12-18 months.

The bull case relies on three things happening:

1. European trailer market recovers in 2026 (60-70% probability) 2. The EPA 2027 pre-buy materializes (30-40% probability, down from 70%+ pre-regulatory rollback) 3. The stock re-rates from 10x to 13x (no clear catalyst)

The first is plausible. The second is now unlikely. The third has no justification.

If you buy SAF-Holland, you are betting on a cyclical recovery with modest upside and meaningful downside if the recovery stalls. The dividend provides a floor. The aftermarket provides resilience. But 65% upside? That is optimistic.

## **Open Questions for Management**

Before investing, demand answers to:

**1.** What is the explicit EBIT bridge from 2024 (EUR 190m) to projected 2026 figures?

**2.** What are the quantified Haldex synergies realized to date versus the original EUR 10-12m (2023) and EUR 25-35m (2027) targets?

**3.** What is management’s insider ownership, and why have there been minimal director dealings if the stock is so undervalued?

**4.** Why should SAF-Holland re-rate to 13x when it trades at 10x for structural reasons (lower margins and ROCE versus JOST)?

**5.** What is the strategy to address Chinese competition in emerging markets, given the company has ~1% revenue from a country producing >50% of global trailers?

If you cannot get satisfactory answers, you are not investing. You are speculating.
